# The 16 mission categories

For each: what it is, the source format, what makes it good, what kills it, seeds. Seeds are
starting points for step 2, not finished missions. Full examples per category are in
`docs/teen-missions-research-2026-10-20.md` part 4. Bank coverage table at the end.

**1 · Hunt** (GISH, scavenger hunts). Good: rarity, more than one way to get it, everyone
searching in parallel. Kills it: generic lists ("something red"). Seeds: the farthest country of
origin; four written numbers from four sources summing to exactly 100; the team name from letters
on signs, one sign per letter; the highest discount percentage on a shelf label.

**2 · Observation** (Michel's observation and Kim games; "noticing the obvious", 59% liked).
Good: the answer is already in front of them, only real looking finds it. Kills it: memorisation.
Seeds: what changed since the creator's photo; a tiny detail photographed by the creator, find
it within 100 m and match the angle.

**3 · Estimation** (Michel's אומדנא; Jet Lag measuring questions). Good: guess and commit
first, check after; the gap is the tension. Kills it: checkable before guessing, or a place
dependent answer nobody surveyed (bank rule 8). Seeds: the price of a named product without
entering the shop; stop when the whole team agrees exactly two minutes passed, timer hidden.

**4 · Navigation** (orienteering, multi and mystery geocaches, Jet Lag's Unguided Tourist).
Good: the map does not give it away (bank rule 9); they infer from what they see. Kills it: the
pin solves it; an information split nobody can enforce (lessons L1). Seeds: where was this photo
taken FROM; a multi stage where each stop yields part of the next address.

**5 · Puzzles** (escape rooms, MIT Mystery Hunt metas). Good: an "aha" built from information
that is physically in the field. Kills it: trivia, memorisation, brute force; a riddle in the
instructions (L3). Seeds: a sign is the key to a word/letter cipher; four named places with a
digit each and a clock, splitting allowed (gold B); four answers in a stage that together name
the final place (needs composer support).

**6 · Taskmaster** (open task, measurable result, precise wording). Good: precise to the point of
suspicion, so a clever loophole exists and can be judged; results comparable between teams.
Kills it: vague ("something cool"); a loophole that deletes the task (L2). Seeds: the highest
point any of you touches while all the others touch them; a phone at least 3 m above the ground
for an overhead shot, how is your problem; everyone counts a minute silently, the last to sit
must be between 55 and 65 seconds.

**7 · Physical** (second most wanted element in escape rooms; Michel's agility and relays; Jet
Lag Cairn, Endless Tumble, Featherless Flight). Good: everyone must take part, exact target.
Kills it: one tries and the rest cheer; camp exercises with no target. Seeds: a coin onto a
small target from behind a line, everyone tries once before anyone tries twice; beat the
previous team's cairn height; a one sheet paper plane from shoulder height, measured in steps.

**8 · Creation** (GISH, Taskmaster; constraints breed creativity). Good: a hard formal constraint
(one take, no words, a shot each), contrast, deadpan as cover, a part for each, a result worth
posting. Kills it: "be creative/funny", anything needing talent. Seeds: a 20 s movie trailer for
this street corner in one take; an Apple style ad for the most boring object here; a shop sign
sung as a dramatic ballad, a line each; forced perspective.

**9 · Strangers** (Amazing Race, Cruel 2 B Kind, Humans of New York). Good: the teen has a role
(photographer, student, interviewer), the stranger receives something (respect, a compliment, to
be the teacher), the whole team is involved. Kills it: the stranger as the joke; weirdness with no
reason. Never script a refusal. (No "ask permission" line in the text: self-evident, lessons L17.) Seeds: learn
one sentence in a language none of you speaks; one question "what was the best thing that
happened to you this week" plus a portrait with permission; a real compliment to three workers.

**10 · Trade** (red paperclip; Jet Lag Bargain Hunter). Good: a specific target object, every
offer must be accepted. Kills it: "trade up to something better" (no end). Seeds: trade until
someone hands you a marker pen; the next mission needs an object another team holds.

**11 · Secrets** (Werewolf, Mafia, Among Us, Senior Assassin; deception works from about 14).
Good: suspicion that lasts, a reveal at the end, something checkable. Kills it: no way to know
who was right. Product gap: no per-team secret answers or per-phone content yet. Seeds: a double
agent with a sealed envelope task, guessed at the finish; kindness assassination between teams.

**12 · Team vs team** (Jet Lag curses, Amazing Race U-Turn and Fast Forward, Journey to the End
of the Night). Key pattern: **one team sets the bar, the next must beat it**, which works even if
they never meet. Good: tension now, one team's result touches another. Kills it: piling more
punishment on the last team. In the product today: flash missions in "first" mode, trackables,
zones. Seeds: first to send a receipt of exactly 7.77; a rubber duck the holder must report every
10 minutes; beat the record; the stage winner curses a team to right turns only (needs product).

**13 · Decision** (Amazing Race Detour and Roadblock, "pick 3 of 5"). Good: a choice that really
matters, ideally a bet on themselves. Kills it: one obviously right option. Seeds: choose who does
the next mission before seeing it (sequence); bet how many high fives in 3 minutes (needs variable
points to pay off); short and hard vs long and safe (needs exclusiveGroups in the composer).

**14 · Ongoing** (Jet Lag Egg Partner, Lemon Phylactery, Water Weight, Right Turn). A rule that
runs in the background across missions. Good: a benign violation for a whole game, no strangers,
it escalates by itself, the story of the day. Kills it: nothing checks it at the end. Seeds: a
raw egg is now a team member, whole at the finish; banned words with a push-up price, total sent
at the end; a full 2 l bottle carried to the end, the carrier changes every mission.

**15 · Kindness** (SF0 reverse shoplifting, Cruel 2 B Kind, GISH). Good: it feels underground and
cool. Kills it: a moral, filming the recipient for likes, or anything that costs money (L5).
Seeds: leave something free and good for the next person (a note on a bench, a chalk drawing,
something built from leaves and stones), photo, walk on.

**16 · Openers and finishers** (peak end rule). Opener: fast, easy, no strangers (bank rule 39).
Finisher: something they hold, or a reveal. Kills it: ceremony ("look at each other"). Seeds:
the national team photo before a final; the end credits; leave something you built at the finish.
