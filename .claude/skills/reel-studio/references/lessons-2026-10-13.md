**The general lesson: a cut is judged where the viewer's eye is, and the eye is on his eyes.** Sound
decides where a line starts; his gaze decides where its picture ends.

## 1. Where a talking shot ends (`examples/race-opening/tails.py`)

- Per clip, at 30 fps: head yaw and pitch, iris offset, eye openness (mediapipe FaceLandmarker).
  Baseline = the median while he speaks. `leave` = where the FINAL run of "off the lens" begins
  (a blink counts), `vend` = where the last vowel ends.
- **The detector fires about 5 frames late**: the eyes drift before any threshold trips. My first
  thresholds (7 degrees, 0.09 iris) and a 2 frame margin still left seven shots ending on a side
  glance or closed eyes. I only saw it by tiling the last frame of every shot. Margin: `leave - 0.20`.
- Picture end = `min(vend - 0.02, leave - 0.20)`, never more than 0.45 s before the last vowel.
- **L cut, lips in sync**: talking shots are muted (`audio_gain: 0`), each line's voice is its own
  `sfx` file. The next voice may start once the previous line has 0.10 s left. When the next
  picture has to start before its voice, its first frame is held for the gap (`held()`), so the
  lips still start with the sound.
- **Never a shot longer than its clip.** The engine pads with a clone of the last frame.
- Hard cuts. A dissolve out of a talking shot always smears the worst frame.

## 2. White background ("רקע לבן לגמרי מאחורה")

- `engine/lib/whitebg.py` → `whiten_rvm()`: Robust Video Matting (ONNX, resnet50, in
  `toolkits/video-editing/models/`, on the onnxruntime already installed). Recurrent, so the matte
  does not flicker; its predicted foreground colour is used in the soft band, so no wall or sky
  fringes the hair. He approved the download; ask before any other one.
- **A colour key cannot do it**: a white shirt on a white wall, and two takes outdoors. Look at one
  frame of EVERY take before choosing a method.
- **A white shirt on white needs an outline**: a faint grey halo. It must die within ~60 px
  (sigma 3.5 at 1/8 scale, 22 levels). My first halo (sigma 9, 26 levels) left the corners of a
  cropped shot at 233, found after a 25 minute batch, which then ran twice.
- **Matting is ~0.5 fps per worker here** (25 minutes for 85 s of takes). So: cut first, matte only
  the kept spans; and **run ONE shot through the whole pipeline to the final file and measure it
  before the batch**. Tell him it is a 25 minute step.
- **"White" is measured in the FINAL file, per shot.** Segments made from a PNG loop came out
  tagged full range (`pc`), talking segments untagged; each file read 255 alone, but from the first
  `pc` segment on, the final pass turned white into 235. `build.py` now ends every segment with
  `scale=out_range=tv:out_color_matrix=bt709` and tags every encode; `qa.py`: **white background**
  (set `"white_background": true` in the timeline).
- The cut out makes every take one "location": indoor and outdoor takes cut together, and a tighter
  framing is a plain crop.
- His head fills the top of his takes: titles are a **lower third over the chest** (y ~955), captions
  `box` at y 1300, the phone ground white (`screens/_comp/ground_v.png` is reused by `phone_shot()`).
  On close shots a title lands on his chin: keep only structural titles (name, 1 2 3, stage, "2 of 4").

## 3. Voice: "sounds like me, but good" is a measured constraint

`examples/race-opening/bench13.py` and `bench13b.py` score a chain two ways: DNSMOS (quality) and
**timbre drift**: the third octave long term spectrum of the speech frames, level free, against the
raw take. A band that moves more than ~2.5 dB is a different voice.

| Chain | DNSMOS vs raw | Largest band moved |
|---|---|---|
| raw lav take | 3.14 / 3.22 / 3.32 | 0 |
| the 2026-10-05 MASTER chain (dynamic EQ at 3.4 kHz, de-esser 0.55) | lower on all three | **-10 to -12 dB at 3 to 4 kHz** |
| two compressors + an air shelf | lower or equal | **+6 to +11 dB at 5 to 10 kHz** |
| MossFormer2 blended 35% / 60% | no gain on a clean lav | same as its chain |
| **high pass 80, one 1.6:1 compressor, a -2 dB shelf at 5.5 kHz, limiter** | equal or higher | **under 1 dB everywhere** |

- **His lav takes are already the best version of him** (floor about -78 dB). Every "improvement"
  stage is what made him sound processed. Do less.
- Compression changes timbre, not only level (consonants are quieter than vowels, so squeezing lifts
  5 to 10 kHz). The -2 dB shelf after it restores the balance.
- Enhancers (DeepFilterNet, MossFormer2) only for noisy takes (DNSMOS BAK under ~3.6).
- The chain is `RP_VOICE_CHAIN` at the top of `examples/race-opening/assemble.py`, at -17 LUFS.
  **Run the timbre drift check on any new chain before using it.**
- I cannot hear. Say so when sending, and name what is waiting for his ear (voice, music level, joins).

## 4. Ingest: he improvises and repeats inside one long take

- One 3.5 minute take held 18 lines, 6 retakes ("אני עושה את זה עוד הפעם") and three attempts at one
  line. A whole file transcription **dropped entire retakes and endings**. Three passes:
  `par_ingest.py` (whole file), `phrases.py` (each phrase alone), `gaps.py` (energy with no words).
- **Round trip every cut**: `assemble.py --check` transcribes each cut line back and prints head and
  tail level. It caught two attempts ending in "את ה..." and one breaking at "לא ב...", all of which
  looked fine as timestamps. "The last attempt" is not always the complete one: read each.
- Words end in consonants above the voice band (ש, ס, ת). Find the word on the 150 Hz..1.5 kHz
  envelope, then widen on the full band while it is still sounding (0.12 s head, 0.4 s tail).
- The cut list is explicit (`CUTS` in `assemble.py`): take, a point inside the first word, a point
  inside the last word. Captions are what he SAID (he changed a third of the lines).

## 5. Energy for an instruction video (rule 57)

## 4. Evening: reel 16 "the button" (script rounds, then a screen insert hook)

**Script: six rounds before he accepted one.** What each rejection taught (rules 56 to 63):
a backstory opening ("half a year ago I built") has no event in the frame; a quiz about the race is
"not interesting to people"; a line like "you have a button like this too" is not understood; and any
mention of the app possibly failing is the opposite of what he wants said. What he accepted was HIS
structure: hesitate, press, phones burst onto a map, then explain the app to someone who never heard
of it. After two rejections, ask what bothers him and build what he describes.

**Screen insert on a black phone** (`examples/reel16-button/hook/`): `track.py` finds the phone as the
dark blob near the hands and fits a PARALLELOGRAM on the hull edges that face right and up (a min area
rectangle overshoots, the phone is in perspective and the hands bite the left and bottom); `comp.py`
corner pins the artwork, keeps the real fingers in front with a skin matte taken from the footage,
screen blends the footage's own reflections back over the artwork, adds a contact shadow and a little
blur to match the camera. The hesitation is a time remap of his own approach (forward, back, forward).
The dive into the screen interpolates the screen's four corners to the full frame while the artwork is
re warped from its full resolution source, so it lands sharp. Matte the take on white BEFORE the insert.

**What he actually filmed was not the brief** (I asked for a phone flat on a table from above; he
filmed himself in profile holding it). Plan the tool around what arrives, and measure the fingertip in
screen coordinates (`tip.py`) before designing the screen: the button goes where the finger lands.

**Phones on a real map** (`examples/reel16-button/map/`): MapLibre in headless Chromium, frame exact.
350 symbols at a far zoom are a caterpillar: keep the camera close (zoom 15 to 16), rotate the bearing
so the route runs up the 9:16 frame, vary speed widely, and scatter arrivals instead of clamping them
to one point.

**Small traps:** ffmpeg 9 has no `-vsync` (use `-fps_mode`), and with `-v error` the failure is silent
(an empty frame read looked like a logic bug). A very long bash heredoc with quotes failed to parse:
write files with the Write tool. `trim=start_frame` needs `fps=` before it on his VFR phone files.
Whisper stretched one word over a 2 s pause: cap caption words at 0.9 s. A cut that starts on an
"exact" time pulled in 0.6 s of breath: check the first word's start in the cut file.
`music.py` gained the style `heartdrop` (a heartbeat that becomes a half time drop).
