# Lessons 2026-10-16 to 17: taking five street reels from v2 to v3

Five reels (18 to 22, `engine/examples/street-200/`) went through two rounds of his notes in one
session. What went wrong, what he asked for, and what is now a tool or a rule so it does not repeat.

## 1. Look at every freeze, from the BUILT video
Two of eleven reaction hits in v2 were aimed at the wrong person: 19's "1,000" froze on the back of a
head, 22's ending on the boy beside the persuader. Both passed QA (QA cannot know whose face matters).
The face detector picks the largest face near `hint_x`; in a group shot that is whoever stands in front.
- **Tool:** `examples/street-200/hit_sheet.py <reel> ...` writes `build/<reel>_hits.jpg`, one tile per hit
  from the built video, labelled with the cut, the source second and the face it aimed at. Read it before
  every send. A wrong one is fixed with `face=(x, y)` in cuts.py, read from a gridded source frame (extract
  at t, draw a 10x10 grid, read the fractions).
- Do not judge hits from `_seg/`: it keeps every old render beside the current one, named by hash.

## 2. Pipeline the delivery: each reel ships the moment it is ready
He asked mid-session to work productively and save time. What saved the most:
- One chain per reel, run in parallel: `cuts.py` -> `make_timeline.py` -> `variants.py --final` -> `deliver.sh`.
  Skip the preview build for a small change: `--final` runs strict QA itself. Three reels took ~10 minutes
  wall clock instead of ~30.
- Deliver and send each reel as its chain finishes, not all five at the end. He watches the first while
  the rest render.
- `variants.py --final` renders only the openings the timeline declares (here only A) and overwrites
  `build/<reel>_A.mp4` at master quality; `deliver.sh` copies that file, so it runs after the master.
- One waiter per chain, each ending on a unique marker (`FINALDONE`, `FINALDONE2`): two waiters on one log
  both started a build and overwrote each other.
- Bump `deliver.sh`'s version (`-v2` -> `-v3`) with sed only while nothing runs it: bash reads scripts as
  it executes them.

## 3. His notes, and the rule each became (founder-rules 79 to 82)
- **79** "סחטנו" said as a joke reads as pressure: cut the clause, let his next real words carry the line
  (`אז באמת תעשו לייק. יאללה חבר'ה, ותשמרו.`). `qa.py` now fails a caption with a ruled out word.
- **80** an insert must not show another event: the Race to Zion route map ("מוצא") came out of 19 and 21;
  `maps/map-jerusalem-zoom.mp4` (the city with dots, no route) replaced it. The library catalogue now
  marks the three route maps "Race to Zion only".
- **81** a sarcastic "יאיי" is not an ending. End on the loudest real moment (the girls shouting
  "1,000 שקל!") and make picture and sound celebrate it: riser, colour freeze with `shake=26, flash=4`,
  a big gold `T.big` headline at chest height, a register, a pop.
- **82** "מבולגן, לא ברור": the camera showed a man who was being SOLD TO while the seller spoke, and a
  viewer reads the face on screen as the voice. A label switches when the camera turns ("משכנע עובר
  אורח"); one idea per beat; every voice that is not the story's is cut. 31 s became 18.6 s.

## 4. Backspaces where `\b` was meant: three checks were blind
`qa.py`'s numeric date check, `video-script/scripts/check_script.py`'s Hebrew number words and a comment
in `lib/captions.py` held control bytes (U+0008, U+000C) where `\b` / `\f` were written. The two checks
"passed" while matching nothing. Then **the fix hit the same trap twice**: writing `\\b` through a
heredoc'd Python string made a backspace again, and so did the gotcha written about it.
- Write such bytes by value: `bytes([92, 98])` is `\b`. Prove the result with a byte scan.
- `scripts/test-source-control-chars.ts` (in `npm test`) now scans `.claude/skills` too, `lib/` included,
  with a reach check that names `engine/lib/captions.py`; a planted backspace was shown to fail it.
- When a check prints a count, print the denominator (`dates: 0 of 34`). "0 found" over nothing looks
  the same as "0 found" over everything.

## 5. Post captions come from the FINAL cut
Write them after the last version, quote only lines that survive in it (20 and 22 changed between v2 and
v3, and 20's caption still promised "three tries" from v1). One shared file per batch
(`docs/marketing/videos/drafts/street-200-captions.md`) copied into each README's `## כיתוב לפוסט`, so the
two never drift. `check_script.py` is built for beat scripts and misreads a caption file; check captions
by hand against `anti-ai.md`.

## 6. The repo is public: commit text and tools, never faces
Cover PNGs are frames of real people, most of them minors. They were not ignored, five older ones had
been pushed. Now `docs/marketing/.gitignore` ignores `videos/**/*-cover.png` and `__pycache__`, and the
older five are untracked (still in the pushed history until it is rewritten). Before any commit under
`docs/marketing`: `git diff --cached --name-only | grep -Ei '\.(png|jpg|mp4|webm)$'` must print nothing.
First names said on tape end up in cuts.py and READMEs; they are public once pushed.
