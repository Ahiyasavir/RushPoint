# Safety, legal and ethical boundaries for night public-challenge missions by teens (15-18) in central Jerusalem

Researched 2026-10-18. Sources were found by web search; most of the Israeli legal sources are secondary (law-firm or press commentary), apart from the statute text on Wikisource. None of this is legal advice. Before the event, a lawyer should check the two highest-risk areas: publishing clips of strangers and minors, and toy/replica weapons.

## 1. Israeli law: filming strangers in public, publishing, minors, consent

### Takeaway
Filming in a public place is generally lawful in Israel. The legal risk sits in **publishing**. The Privacy Protection Law makes it a violation to publish a photo of someone in a way that could humiliate them. Following someone in a way that may harass them is also a violation, and it can be criminal. The "appears incidentally in public" defense covers background passers-by, but it does **not** cover a stranger who is the subject of a prank or interaction clip. Consent must be informed ("מדעת"), and for a minor, publication normally needs a parent's consent.

### Cited Findings
- Privacy Protection Law 1981, s.2(1): a privacy violation includes "בילוש או התחקות אחרי אדם, העלולים להטרידו, או הטרדה אחרת" (following or tracking a person in a way that may harass them, or other harassment) — [Wikisource: חוק הגנת הפרטיות](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הגנת_הפרטיות)
- s.2(3): photographing a person while they are in a private domain (ברשות היחיד) is a violation. s.2(4): "פרסום תצלומו של אדם ברבים בנסיבות שבהן עלול הפרסום להשפילו או לבזותו" (publishing a person's photo where it may humiliate or degrade them) — [Wikisource](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הגנת_הפרטיות)
- s.2(4א): publishing an identifiable photo of a victim of a sudden incident, taken during or shortly after the event, is a violation (with exceptions) — [Wikisource](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הגנת_הפרטיות)
- s.3: consent means "הסכמה מדעת, במפורש או מכללא" (informed consent, explicit or implied) — [Wikisource](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הגנת_הפרטיות)
- s.5: an intentional violation under s.2(1), 2(3)-(7) and others carries up to **5 years' imprisonment**. These sections include harassing surveillance and humiliating publication — [Wikisource](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הגנת_הפרטיות)
- s.18(2)(ה) defense: the photo or its publication was made in a public place **and the person appears in it incidentally (באקראי)** — [Wikisource](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הגנת_הפרטיות); the same reading appears in commentary: [דנס 100 – על היחסים שבין אדם למצלמה](https://www.duns100.co.il/articles/על_היחסים_שבין_אדם_למצלמה)
- Commentary: "ככלל, צילום ברשות הרבים הוא חוקי לחלוטין" (as a rule, filming in a public place is fully legal), but the people filmed may still have a privacy claim; a public-interest defense also exists — [דנס 100](https://www.duns100.co.il/articles/על_היחסים_שבין_אדם_למצלמה); [פרוטוקול – פגיעה בפרטיות](https://protocol.co.il/privacy-injury/)
- A family lawyer quoted in Israel Hayom: "ללא רשות ההורה, חל איסור לפרסם תמונה של קטין" (publishing a minor's photo is prohibited without the parent's permission); the parent or guardian decides — [ישראל היום](https://www.israelhayom.co.il/lifestyle/family-and-parenting/article/16379414)
- Prevention of Sexual Harassment Law, Amendment 10 (published 8.1.2014): publishing a photo, film or recording of a person that **focuses on their sexuality**, where it may humiliate them and without their consent, is sexual harassment — [ביזפורטל](https://www.bizportal.co.il/general/news/article/379805)
- The same law: repeated sexual propositions to someone who has shown they are not interested count as sexual harassment. For minors, the bar is lower where there is a relationship of authority, dependence or education — [Knesset bill explanatory notes](https://fs.knesset.gov.il/17/law/17_ls_ptk_76745.doc) (secondary; check against the current statute)

### Inferences
- A clip that centres on a stranger's reaction (being surprised, confused or embarrassed) is not "incidental". Unless the stranger gave clear consent, s.18(2)(ה) probably does not protect it, and a humiliating edit falls under s.2(4).
- The organizer collects and reposts participant clips, which puts the organizer in the "publisher" role. A blur/no-publish policy for strangers' faces protects the organizer as well as the participants.
- Participants are mostly minors, so publishing their own faces needs signed parental media consent on the registration form. A minor's "yes" on the street is not enough to publish another minor.
- A stranger who is visibly a minor (around 12-17) should be treated as off-limits for any featured interaction clip, because nobody can get their parent's consent on the spot.
- "Persistent asking" after a "no", and following a person, map onto s.2(1) harassment. Romantic or "flirty" dares map onto the sexual-harassment law.

### Gaps
- I found no Israeli ruling on prank or street-challenge videos specifically.
- I did not verify the exact wording of the Privacy Protection Law's Amendment 13 (in force 2025), which mostly concerns databases and enforcement powers.
- I did not confirm the current statutory text on minors' consent to publication. The parental-consent rule above comes from lawyer commentary.

## 2. Harassment, nuisance and platform rules (TikTok / YouTube)

### Takeaway
Both platforms ban dangerous challenges and pranks that make people believe they are in danger or that distress minors. YouTube explicitly lists fake violence, fake emergencies and emotional distress to minors. Israeli law adds harassment (following, persistent approaches), sexual harassment (repeated propositions, sexualised clips) and humiliating publication. A clip that breaks these rules is both illegal and unpostable.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube's Harmful or Dangerous Content policy bans pranks that cause physical harm to unsuspecting victims, pranks that make victims **believe they are in serious danger** (even with no physical harm, e.g. fake kidnapping or swatting), and pranks causing **emotional distress to minors or vulnerable people** (fake death, fake violence, fake abandonment). It also bans content endangering minors and showing dangerous acts by minors. Prank content involving adults that is otherwise allowed may be age-restricted — [YouTube Help: Harmful or dangerous content policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801964)
- When the rules were introduced in 2019, YouTube said violating videos are removed and a channel can get a strike; three strikes terminate the account — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/news/youtube-bans-harmful-or-dangerous-prank-videos) (2019; strike details may have changed)
- TikTok: content that "encourages or replicates dangerous challenges that could cause injury" violates its Community Guidelines and is removed; hashtags of dangerous challenges show a warning screen — [The Star (skull breaker coverage, 2020)](https://www.thestar.co.uk/read-this/tiktok-has-banned-skull-breaker-challenge-videos-heres-why-2097017)
- TikTok's current Community Guidelines version was released 14.8.2025 and took effect 13.9.2025, with sections "Youth Safety and Well-Being" and "Safety and Civility" — [ConductAtlas diff tracker](https://conductatlas.com/diff/tiktok/tiktok-community-guidelines/5940/5963/) (I did not read the full 2025 text)
- Israeli harassment and following: s.2(1) of the Privacy Protection Law (see §1); repeated sexual propositions after a "no" count as sexual harassment — [ביזפורטל](https://www.bizportal.co.il/general/news/article/379805)

### Inferences
- **Touching** a stranger (hugs, high-fives they did not offer, grabbing an arm, lifting someone): never. It is assault-adjacent, and with mixed genders in Jerusalem it is a sexual-harassment and community-conflict risk.
- **Following** a stranger, even "for a funny shot": never. That is the literal text of s.2(1).
- **One ask, one "no", move on.** A second ask after a refusal starts to look like harassment. Filming someone who refused, or keeping them in the published clip, combines harassment with non-consensual publication.
- **Scare pranks** (fake injury, fake fainting, fake emergency, pretending to be lost or kidnapped, pretending a child is abandoned): banned by YouTube policy, and in Jerusalem they trigger real security responses (see §4).
- A good test: "Would the stranger laugh *with* us if they saw the clip tomorrow?" If not, don't do it.

### Gaps
- I did not find published codes of conduct from Israeli street-video creators.
- I could not read TikTok's 2025 guidelines text on pranks directly.

## 3. Dangerous challenge history and standard safety measures

### Takeaway
Speed eating, extreme-spice and dry-powder challenges have killed or hospitalised young people. Time pressure plus food that is dense, dry or very hot is the deadly mix. Organizers who sanctioned such contests were sued. At night in Jerusalem, the physical hazards are the light rail tracks and roads, stone stairs, and water features.

### Cited Findings
- **Speed eating:** In March 2017, Sacred Heart University student Caitlin Nelson (20) choked during a **school-sanctioned charity pancake-eating contest** and died 3 days later. Her family sued the university, alleging it approved a dangerous contest and lacked adequate medical staff, and the case settled (terms undisclosed, ~2020) — [Campus Safety Magazine](https://www.campussafetymagazine.com/news/university-settles-lawsuit-with-family-of-student-who-died-during-pancake-eating-contest/); [CBS](https://www.cbsnews.com/detroit/news/college-student-dies-after-choking-at-pancake-eating-contest)
- **One Chip Challenge:** Harris Wolobah (14, Worcester MA) died 1.9.2023. The medical examiner gave the cause as cardiopulmonary arrest "in the setting of recent ingestion of food substance with high capsaicin concentration", with an enlarged heart and a congenital heart defect as contributing factors. The manufacturer Paqui pulled the product from shelves in September 2023 — [CBS News](https://cbsnews.com/news/spicy-one-chip-challenge-harris-wolobah-death-autopsy); [Fox LA](https://foxla.com/news/one-chip-challenge-death-paqui-harris-wolobah). Note: some outlets say the examiner could not confirm the chip was the capsaicin source — [Spectrum News](https://spectrumnews1.com/ma/worcester/news/2024/05/16/autopsy-confirms-challenge-aided-in-teen-death)
- **Cinnamon challenge:** a 2013 *Pediatrics* paper warned of aspiration, lung inflammation, aspiration pneumonia and asthma attacks. US poison centres logged 178 calls in H1 2012 (17% needed medical attention), up from 51 in 2011 — [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/pediatricians-to-kids-do-not-eat-straight-cinnamon-36980396/); [HealthDay](https://www.healthday.com/healthpro-news/pulmonology/dangers-of-the-cinnamon-challenge-need-emphasis-675509.html)
- **Jerusalem light rail:** there are recurring pedestrian injuries. Near Mahane Yehuda market, a pedestrian was hit and left in serious, unstable condition. At Yafe Nof (2013), a man who crossed recklessly was trapped under the train and firefighters had to lift it to free him. A 19-year-old woman was reportedly killed under the light rail near the central bus station (report dated 23.07.2026) — [JDN](https://www.jdn.co.il/news/israel/1818521/); [בחדרי חרדים](https://www.bhol.co.il/news/140488); [בחדרי חרדים 2026](https://www.bhol.co.il//news/1055195) (I did not open the 2026 item directly)

### Inferences
- **Eating missions:** no timed or speed eating, no "most in X seconds", no dry powders (cinnamon, cocoa, flour), no extreme-heat chili, no forced or "bottoms up" drinking, and nothing containing alcohol or energy shots. If there is food, make it "taste and rate" or "find the vendor who…", with no clock. Ask about allergies at registration.
- **No running** as a scored element anywhere near the light rail tracks (Jaffa Road, Mahane Yehuda, City Hall, Damascus Gate). Score with code checkpoints, not speed between them, or cap the speed advantage so sprinting doesn't pay.
- **Stairs and stone at night:** Old City and Nachlaot steps are uneven and slippery. Don't put a time bonus on a stair segment, and avoid unlit alleys.
- **Water:** no getting into or being pushed into fountains or water features. In late October, Jerusalem nights are cool and wet clothes bring a cold risk; in the Teddy Park fountain area, stay out of the water.
- **Standard measures** (for an event the organizer sanctions, given the Sacred Heart lawsuit): an adult responsible for each area, a first-aid kit and someone trained in first aid, an emergency phone line, a buddy rule (no one alone), and a curfew / end time.

### Gaps
- I found no Israeli statistics on youth challenge injuries.
- I found no MDA guidance written specifically for urban night games.
- I did not research cold-water shock in an Israeli context.

## 4. Jerusalem holy sites, sensitive places and security sensitivities

### Takeaway
At the Western Wall, private non-commercial phone filming needs no permit. But the Foundation's rules ban acting or staged scenes, costumes and props, and photographing worshippers' faces; close-ups need consent. Approval is never given for purposes that disrupt public order or violate privacy. In Jerusalem's security climate, people have been shot after being mistaken for attackers. Anything that looks like a weapon, a mask, sudden sprinting at people or shouting alarm words can trigger a lethal response.

### Cited Findings
- Western Wall Heritage Foundation filming procedure (under the Regulations for the Protection of Holy Places for Jews, 1981). It covers the whole complex: upper plaza, prayer plaza, tunnels — [thekotel.org filming regulations](https://thekotel.org/en/visitor_information/filming-regulations/)
  - 7.1: filming by a private individual for non-commercial purposes needs no approval. 7.4: the procedure's provisions still apply to unapproved filming "with the necessary modifications", with an emphasis on visitors' privacy and the site's sanctity.
  - 4.2: approval is not granted for commercial purposes or for purposes that **disrupt public order or violate privacy**.
  - 5.3: "No filming of any kind shall be conducted in the Western Wall Complex by actors"; costumes and external equipment are not allowed. 5.9: no production setup in the plaza.
  - 5.6: worshippers' faces should not be photographed; close-ups need the worshipper's consent.
  - 5.7: men do not enter the women's section and vice versa. 4.1: no filming on Shabbat or Jewish holidays.
  - 6.1-6.2: follow the instructions of Foundation staff and ushers, and keep order.
- The Foundation also bans photos and electronic devices on Shabbat and holidays — [thekotel.org conduct guidelines](https://english.thekotel.org/guidelines-for-behavior-at-the-western-wall-2/)
- **Replica weapons:** a 2023 amendment to the Firearms Law (in force 6.8.2023) defines "דמוי כלי ירייה" (an imitation firearm, e.g. airsoft) and requires a licence to make, import, store, transport or trade one — [gov.il notice](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/news/airsoft24/he/news_%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%99%D7%94%20%D7%A6%D7%95%20%D7%93%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%99%20%D7%9B%D7%9C%D7%99%20%D7%99%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%99%D7%94%20-%20%D7%A4%D7%A8%D7%A1%D7%95%D7%9D%20%D7%9C%D7%A6%D7%99%D7%91%D7%95%D7%A8.pdf). Reportedly, use and carrying are limited to licensed shooting clubs — [ice.co.il](https://www.ice.co.il/law/news/article/856748). In Karmiel, police confiscated an airsoft gun from a first-grader at school — [ynet](https://www.ynet.co.il/news/article/skbdr8qpo)
- **Mistaken-identity shootings in Jerusalem:**
  - 2015: a Jewish man was shot dead after being suspected of being a Palestinian attacker — [Mako/N12](https://www.mako.co.il/news-military/security-q4_2015/Article-0ad9277281c8051004.htm); [Forward](https://forward.com/fast-forward/323150/israeli-soldiers-shoot-and-kill-jewish-man-mistaken-for-a-terrorist/)
  - A Bhol report describes a yeshiva student shot dead on Yirmiyahu Street after being taken for a stabbing attacker; search results dated it July 2026 — [בחדרי חרדים](https://www.bhol.co.il/news/188786). **Unverified:** the page returned 403, so I could not confirm the date or details.
- **Light rail as a security-sensitive space:** security guards have used pepper spray on passengers during confrontations — [Jewish Press 2014](https://jewishpress.com/news/breaking-news/jerusalem-light-rails-new-zero-tolerance-for-arab-violence/2014/10/21) (partisan source); a State Comptroller survey found that sense of security and crowding are the weakest points of passenger satisfaction — [ynet](https://www.ynet.co.il/news/article/5042602)

### Inferences
- **Western Wall / Old City holy sites:** no missions at the Kotel or inside churches beyond quiet observation or finding a fact. No skits, costumes, props, singing or dancing, and no filming of worshippers. Men and women stay in their own sections. A "funny" mission there breaks the Foundation's rules.
- **Churches / Russian Compound / Holy Sepulchre / Armenian quarter:** treat them the same way: quiet, no filming of clergy or worshippers, modest dress. The Russian Compound also holds a police station and a detention facility (inference; not verified in this pass). Don't run or film around them.
- **Mahane Yehuda:** vendors are working. Ask before filming a stall, buy something if you take their time, never block a passage, and don't film customers. After stalls close, the shuk becomes bars, which is a different crowd: no missions involving bars or alcohol.
- **Security red lines (non-negotiable):**
  - No toy, water or replica weapons of any kind, including finger-gun skits.
  - No masks, balaclavas or full-face costumes; nothing hidden under clothing; no "abandoned bag" or "mystery package" missions.
  - No sprinting toward people, guards, soldiers or police.
  - No shouting alarm words ("פיגוע", "מחבל", "חפץ חשוד", "bomb", "Allahu Akbar" as a joke).
  - No filming of police, Border Police or soldiers, or their positions.
  - Stay out of security checkpoints and away from guards at the Old City gates.
  - Teams should look like what they are: matching event shirts help identify them as a youth group.
- Brief participants: if a security officer approaches, **stop, hands visible, comply, show the event card**.

### Gaps
- I found no official Israel Police guidance aimed at youth events or games.
- I did not find the Jerusalem municipality's rules for events in public space or whether a permit is needed for ~10 teams. This needs a call to the municipality / רישוי עסקים.
- I could not verify the 2026 Yirmiyahu Street report.
- I did not research rules at Christian holy sites.

## 5. Light rail rules (CityPass / CFIR)

### Takeaway
I found no official CityPass passenger code of conduct online in this pass. What is documented: inspectors fine riders whose card was not validated (₪180), security and crowding are known pain points, and pedestrians hit on the tracks is a recurring accident type. Treat the train as transport only, not a stage.

### Cited Findings
- Fine for an unvalidated card: ₪180 (a bill to cut it to ₪20 was discussed). Most fines come from validation-machine problems — [כיכר השבת](https://www.kikar.co.il/israel-news/98134)
- There has been criticism of light-rail inspectors' rough conduct, and an inspector-passenger confrontation ended in arrests — [ice.co.il](https://www.ice.co.il/auto/news/article/1015482)
- CityPass ran a courtesy campaign: let passengers off before boarding — [ynet 2013](https://www.ynetnews.com/articles/0,7340,L-4393412,00.html)
- Passenger satisfaction is weakest on sense of security and crowding — [ynet](https://www.ynet.co.il/news/article/5042602)

### Inferences
- Every participant must validate a Rav-Kav for each ride. A whole team fined at ₪180 each, or a confrontation with inspectors, would be a bad night.
- No performing, singing, dancing, or "ask a passenger" missions on board. It is crowded, security-tense, and people cannot walk away.
- Board as small groups, using several doors. Don't block doors to hold the train for a teammate, and don't rush onto a closing train.
- Never cross the tracks outside crossings. Don't stand on the platform edge to film.
- No filming staff, drivers or security on the train.

### Gaps
- I could not find the official CityPass terms of carriage (תקנון) on filming, groups or behaviour. Check citypass.co.il or call them before the event.

## 6. Religious-Zionist mixed-gender youth sensitivities

### Takeaway
I found no official Bnei Akiva written policy online. Rabbinic answers on popular religious-Zionist Q&A sites (Kipa, Yeshiva.org.il) treat touching with affection between boys and girls as forbidden and mixed dancing as problematic, and leave the degree of mixing to local rabbis. For a mixed group, the safe design assumes no cross-gender touch, no mixed dancing, and no girls singing solo or performing on camera for a mixed audience.

### Cited Findings
- Kipa "שאל את הרב" cites Rambam on the prohibition of touching with affection (נגיעה שיש בה חיבה) and seclusion (ייחוד) — [כיפה](https://www.kipa.co.il/שאל-את-הרב/%d7%90%d7%99%d7%a1%d7%95%d7%a8-%d7%a0%d7%92%d7%99%d7%a2%d7%94-%d7%97%d7%91%d7%a8%d7%94-%d7%9e%d7%a2%d7%95%d7%a8%d7%91%d7%aa/)
- Another Kipa answer cites the Chafetz Chaim as forbidding mixed dancing and watching girls dance; the permitted form is separate circles — [כיפה](https://www.kipa.co.il/שאל-את-הרב/%d7%a8%d7%99%d7%a7%d7%95%d7%93%d7%99%d7%9d-%d7%9e%d7%a2%d7%95%d7%a8%d7%91%d7%99%d7%9d-3/)
- Rabbinic opinion differs: one answer says a mixed movement has no halachic justification, while another (Yeshivat HaYotzrim) says it depends on local conditions and a local rabbi — [כיפה](https://www.kipa.co.il/שאל-את-הרב/%d7%91%d7%99%d7%a8%d7%95%d7%a8-%d7%a2%d7%9e%d7%95%d7%a7-%d7%9c%d7%92%d7%91%d7%99-%d7%93%d7%a8%d7%9b%d7%94-%d7%a9%d7%9c-%d7%91%d7%a0%d7%99-%d7%a2%d7%a7%d7%99%d7%91%d7%90/); [ישיבה](https://www.yeshiva.org.il/ask/47961)
- A forum claim that Bnei Akiva removed mixed dancing in the 1950s is a participant's anecdote, not an official source — [INN forum](https://www.inn.co.il/Forum/Forum.aspx/t798758)

### Inferences
- Mission rules for mixed teams:
  - No physical contact between boys and girls: no human pyramids, piggybacks, holding hands in a chain, or "lift your teammate".
  - Contact challenges only within one gender, or use a prop (rope, stick) instead.
  - Dancing missions in single-gender sub-groups, or as separate circles.
  - Singing: let the team choose who sings, or make it a whole-team chant. Never require a girl to sing solo on camera.
  - No "couple" or flirt dares; no dares involving strangers of the other gender (also a sexual-harassment risk, see §1).
  - Modest-dress assumptions (no "remove an item of clothing" or "swap clothes" missions).
- Ask the participating groups' madrichim/rabbi for their own norms. Different snifim (branches) and schools differ; this is an inference from the conflicting answers above.

### Gaps
- I found no official written Bnei Akiva (בני עקיבא) policy document; the answers above are individual rabbis' views.
- Kol isha (קול באשה, women's singing) was not answered by any source found.

## 7. Consolidated do / don't rule set for mission designers (synthesis of §1-6)

### Takeaway
Wild and funny works when the joke is on the team itself, not on strangers, the city's holy places or its security forces. Strangers are guests in the clip only if they choose to be and can walk away.

### Cited Findings
- (The rules below are synthesised from the sources cited in §1-6; each line points to its section.)

### Inferences
**DO**
- Make the team the butt of the joke (silly poses, team chants, re-enacting a historic moment among themselves). This avoids the s.2(4) humiliation risk for strangers (§1).
- Ask a stranger **once**, explain it's a youth game, and accept a "no" immediately. Persistent approaches count as harassment (§1-2).
- Get explicit on-camera consent ("זה בסדר שתופיע בסרטון?"), or keep strangers incidental and out of focus. Only incidental appearance is protected (§1, s.18(2)(ה)).
- Blur or crop stranger faces before the organizer reposts anything. The organizer is the publisher (§1).
- Collect parental media consent for all participants at registration. Publishing minors needs it (§1).
- Score by checkpoints and creativity rather than raw speed, especially along Jaffa Road and the tracks. There is a pedestrian/light-rail injury history (§3, §5).
- Make food missions "taste and rate" with no clock, and collect allergy information. The Sacred Heart death and lawsuit show what a timed contest can do (§3).
- Keep teams of at least 3 with a buddy rule, an adult responsible for each zone, first aid, an emergency number and a hard end time. That is the standard of care an organizer-sanctioned event will be judged against (§3).
- Wear identifying event shirts and carry an event card, and brief everyone: if security approaches, freeze, show your hands, comply. This lowers the risk of mistaken-identity incidents (§4).
- Validate a Rav-Kav every ride, board through several doors and let passengers off first. That avoids ₪180 fines and confrontations (§5).
- Check norms with the groups' madrichim in advance (§6).

**DON'T**
- Touch strangers, follow them, or block their path; this is harassment / s.2(1) (§1-2).
- Keep filming, or publish, someone who said no; non-consensual publication (§1-2).
- Feature visibly underage strangers; no parental consent is possible (§1).
- Use scare pranks: fake injury, fainting, emergency, kidnapping or abandonment. YouTube bans them, and in Jerusalem they trigger real security responses (§2, §4).
- Bring toy, water or replica guns, knives, masks or balaclavas, or suspicious-looking bags or "packages". The 2023 Firearms Law amendment applies, and the lethal mistaken-identity risk is real (§4).
- Shout alarm words (פיגוע / מחבל / חפץ חשוד / bomb) or sprint toward people, guards or police (§4).
- Film police, soldiers or security positions, or the Old City gate checkpoints (§4).
- Stage skits, wear costumes, sing, dance or film worshippers at the Kotel or in churches. Foundation rules 5.3/5.6 forbid it, and it disturbs worshippers (§4).
- Perform or interact with passengers on the light rail, cross tracks outside crossings, or hold doors (§5).
- Run speed-eating, chili, dry-powder (cinnamon), chugging or alcohol/energy-drink challenges; there have been documented deaths and injuries (§3).
- Run water/fountain challenges at night in October; cold and slips (§3).
- Create cross-gender contact, mixed dancing, a required girls' solo, or couple/flirt dares; religious-Zionist norms and sexual-harassment law (§1, §6).
- Make sexualised or "embarrass a stranger" content; it counts as sexual harassment under Amendment 10 (§1).

### Gaps
- The municipal event-permit question and an official police contact for a youth night game in central Jerusalem are still open and need direct calls.
- A lawyer's sign-off on the media consent form text is recommended.
