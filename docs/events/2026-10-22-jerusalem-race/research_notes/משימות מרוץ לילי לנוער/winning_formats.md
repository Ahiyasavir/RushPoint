# Winning challenge formats for teens, and the anatomy of a task that makes people laugh and share

Scope: reusable task patterns, with real examples, taken from Taskmaster, the big YouTube challenge channels, The Amazing Race, GISH and street challenge content. The aim is to adapt them into missions for a 2.5 hour night city race in Jerusalem, for teams of 4 to 12 teens.
Method note: 20 tool calls (web search and fetch). Most view counts come from third party trackers or press snapshots. Each one is labelled with its source and should be read as a dated snapshot.

## Taskmaster: the most loved tasks, and why they work

### Takeaway
The best Taskmaster tasks are one short sentence with a clear but judgeable win condition ("most X wins", "fastest", "most beautiful"). On top of that sit one or two odd constraints or secret rules, and the judging rewards interpretation, not only execution. The house rule is "all the information is on the task": players may exploit anything the wording does not forbid. When the show broke that rule by hiding rules in the room, fans criticised it.

### Cited Findings
**What Alex Horne says about design**
- Horne writes most of the tasks himself. He is described as someone who "only has to stare at a blank piece of paper for inspiration to strike." — [RTS, 10 things you might not know about Taskmaster](https://rts.org.uk/article/10-things-you-might-not-know-about-taskmaster)
- Horne's rule: "a task has got to be something that everyone can do", with no special equipment, yet it should still produce a wide range of responses. He also admits that a task which seems brilliant on paper often gives poor results. — [ShortList interview (via search summary)](https://shortlist.com/lists/exclusive-interview-taskmaster-assistant-alex-hornes-tips-for-surviving-lockdown-402241)
- The wording pattern is one sentence plus a superlative win condition. The first ever task was "Put as much money into my bank account – most money wins." The #HomeTasking lockdown task was "throw a piece of A4 paper into a bin. Most spectacular throw wins." — [RTS](https://rts.org.uk/article/10-things-you-might-not-know-about-taskmaster)
- One task was dropped because it did not work: bursting a full roll of bubble wrap as fast as possible. It was tried for three series, and the rolls survived even cars and steamrollers. — [RTS](https://rts.org.uk/article/10-things-you-might-not-know-about-taskmaster)
- Greg Davies on why it works: "there's been a world constructed and there are rules within that world… They then become a sort of micro-society within the rules." Contestants who commit fully are the delight. Horne: "you push yourself, you'll be remembered." — [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/taskmaster-game-show-alex-horne-greg-davies-interview/)

**Secret rules and twists (documented)**
- "Build a tower": a list of secret rules sat on the umpire's clipboard (no running, no entering the shed). Breaking one triggered a shock, and only then was the broken rule revealed. — [taskmaster.info (via search)](https://taskmaster.info/task.php?id=3502)
- A rule was hidden on the back of the second task brief: any contestant who reacted negatively to the task risked losing points. — [taskmaster.info (via search)](https://taskmaster.info/task.php?id=2321)
- A Series 20 task was criticised for breaking the show's cardinal rule, "all the information is on the task", because extra rules were hidden in the room. — [taskmaster.info / IIT TechNews (via search)](https://technews.iit.edu/?p=2215)
- The Series 16 secret task was announced to viewers only at the end of the series. — [AOL](https://www.aol.com/taskmaster-series-16-finally-unveils-000500546.html)

**Fan-ranked top tasks (BuzzFeed's ranking of 50, with the mechanism of each)** — [BuzzFeed](https://www.buzzfeed.com/hopelasater/best-taskmaster-tasks-ranked)
- #1 (S5): write and perform a song about a woman after five minutes of conversation with her. *Stranger interview turned into instant creative output.*
- #2 (S7): when a siren sounds during a future task, lie down in a boiler suit and tie yourself up. *A delayed trigger that interrupts later tasks.*
- #3 (S4): make and eat the most exotic sandwich (planning time, then build time). *Plan then build, and eating is part of the scoring.*
- #4 (S2): get a potato into a golf hole without touching the green. *A restriction forces lateral thinking.*
- #6 (S1): eat as much watermelon as possible in one minute. *Pure, simple, visual.*
- #7 (S12): solve a riddle while confined to your room. Bonus points for making a teammate say "submarine" without saying it yourself, with a penalty if you get caught. *A hidden side objective against your own team.*
- #9 (S1): do something that looks impressive when the footage is played in reverse. *A camera-native trick.*
- #12 (S1): buy the best present for the Taskmaster with £20.
- #13 (S11): direct a teammate into a circle while seated, giving one three-word instruction every 30 seconds. *Communication bottleneck.*
- #15 (S12): pop a balloon behind a rope, buying tools with time. *Time as a currency.*
- #20 (S12): make noises one at a time without being identified. Most unidentified noises wins.
- #21 (S4): caricature the person behind a curtain, who may only answer yes or no. Bonus point for finding their full name.
- #26 (S12): ride a bicycle as slowly as possible. Foot touches halve your time. *An inverted race.*
- #27 (S13): high-five Alex, where only the third fastest scores. *Inverted or middle-wins scoring defeats the obvious strategy.*
- #28 (S7): hit a drum at exactly 9.58 seconds, plus bonus points for the most magnificent walk. *A precision target with a style bonus.*
- #30 (S4): paint the Taskmaster, but smile at the camera with increasing enthusiasm every 30 seconds. *A ridiculous concurrent constraint.*
- #36 (S2): conceal a pineapple on your person. The winner is the one with the most *incorrect* guesses at inspection.
- #37 (S8): hide completely inside a phone box, and the timer stops when you shout "I'm invisible!" *A self-declared finish.*
- #38 (S4): destroy a cake; the most beautiful destruction wins.
- #43 (S8): park a buggy while blindfolded, after a preparation period.
- #45 (S13): waggle a body part out of a shower curtain while the Taskmaster guesses which part it is.
- #47 (S9): pass a drawing down a queue where each player sees only the picture on the back of the person in front. *A telephone game.*
- #49 (S7): be photographed in the most unusual situation wearing a fez (over a long window).
- BuzzFeed also mentions Mawaan's helium egg (S10) as a notable omission from its list. — [BuzzFeed/AOL](https://www.aol.com/news/50-best-taskmaster-tasks-time-233102703.html)

### Inferences
- **Reusable Taskmaster grammar for missions:** [verb] + [object] + a superlative ("most beautiful / most unusual / smallest / slowest") + one absurd constraint + an optional secret line revealed only after submission. Everything a team needs must be on the card; any loophole the wording allows is fair play and should be rewarded rather than punished.
- **Inverted scoring** (slowest wins, third fastest wins, most wrong guesses wins) is cheap to run and produces "wait, what?" moments. It also stops the strongest team from winning everything.
- **Delayed triggers** (#2 siren) map well onto a city race. A code word or sound announced at the start ("when HQ sends 'פינגווין', all of you lie on the ground within 10 seconds") pays off hours later, and works well as an in-app flash mission.
- **Subjective "most X" judging** needs a judge with personality. A live judging reveal at the finish (the show's studio segment) is the payoff, and the teens should know in advance that their videos will be shown on screen.
- Taskmaster tasks are usually filmed solo per contestant; here all teams do the same task in parallel. The comparison at the end (each team's interpretation side by side) is the main comedic engine.

### Gaps
- No long-form primary interview with Horne on loopholes or wording was found. The "dry wording + open interpretation" principle is well known among fans but is supported here only by the cardinal-rule quote and the task examples.
- Exact verbatim wording for most top tasks could not be retrieved (BuzzFeed paraphrases). taskmaster.info holds exact briefs, task by task.
- Episode view counts for Taskmaster YouTube uploads were not collected.

## YouTube creators (Sidemen, Beta Squad, MrBeast, Yes Theory, Ryan Trahan, Jet Lag, Dude Perfect): which mechanics drive teen views

### Takeaway
The repeatable winners are: a concept you can say in one sentence; visible, escalating stakes; a contest whose result is withheld until the end (last to leave, chase, hide and seek); social deduction ("6 vs 1 secret X"); random punishments (a wheel); and the cards and curses of Jet Lag, which let competitors sabotage each other.

### Cited Findings
**MrBeast (leaked production memo, ~36 pages, authenticity confirmed by two former producers to Passionfruit, per coverage)**
- "for 50 million people to understand something it must be simple… The simpler the better." — [Alexander Jarvis, memo text](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)
- "The first minute of each video is the most important minute." The memo cites losing 21 million viewers in the first minute out of about 60 million clicks. — [Creator Handbook](https://www.creatorhandbook.net/leaked-document-allegedly-reveals-mrbeasts-secrets-to-youtube-success-the-key-takeaways/); [Jarvis](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)
- Named formats: **Last to Leave** (stay to see who wins), **Stair-stepping** ("As you get deeper in the video the stakes get higher", e.g. from a $1 firework to the world's largest), **Chase videos**, and **Spectacles**. Formats rotate; two of the same kind never run back to back. — [Jarvis](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)
- The "wow factor" example: in "100 Days in the Circle", a house is brought in on a crane 30 seconds into the video. — [Jarvis](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/); [Creator Handbook](https://www.creatorhandbook.net/leaked-document-allegedly-reveals-mrbeasts-secrets-to-youtube-success-the-key-takeaways/)
- "Stop telling people what they will be watching and start showing them." "Strong payoffs at the end boost retention", and endings must be abrupt. — [Jarvis](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)
- Extreme framing beats mild framing: "I Survived" over "I Spent"; a vivid, high-contrast image (a person in a bathtub of ketchup) is far more clickable than a mundane one. — [Jarvis](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)

**Sidemen**
- "Tinder In Real Life": the third episode peaked at about 83M views and the first passed 42M (Trill Mag). Know Your Meme cites more than 77M for a February 2021 snapshot. The two sources conflict. — [Trill Mag](https://www.trillmag.com/entertainment/has-gen-z-lost-interest-in-the-sidemen/); [Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/indian-sidemen-indian-tinder-in-real-life)
- Hide and seek is a recurring format: a Dubai Aquaventure waterpark video passed 11M views, and a Paradise Wildlife Park video got 3.9M in its first day. — [Planet Attractions](https://www.planetattractions.com/news/WATCH:-KSI-and-The-Sidemen-play-hide-and-seek-inside-Paradise-Wildlife-Park/2521). Osea Island vs 40 YouTubers had about 5M views. — [Gazette](https://www.gazette-news.co.uk/news/24440993.sidemen-film-new-hide-seek-video-osea-island/)
- Sidemen Sundays regularly exceed 10M views per video. — [Trill Mag](https://www.trillmag.com/entertainment/has-gen-z-lost-interest-in-the-sidemen/)

**Beta Squad**
- The big performers are social deduction ("6 vs 1 secret X") and money races. "6 Kids vs 1 Secret Adult" (a take on Jubilee's Odd One Out) passed 2M views within a day of launch (Aug 2025). — [Drop Media](https://www.dropmedia.co.uk/?p=5697)
- "Who Can Make The Most Money in 24 Hours (USA)" is listed at about 13.5M views. A fan wiki claims more than 40M for "6 Females vs 1 Secret Male" (uncorroborated). A tracker lists "Murder Mystery" at about 9.2M. — [aggregator](https://nps-tissueconverting.valmet.com/who-can-make-the-most-money-challenge.html); [shapes.inc fan wiki](https://shapes.inc/fandom/beta-squad/episodes-guide); [Vidanalyze](https://vidanalyze.com/channel/betasquad/videos)

**Ryan Trahan**
- The penny series means travelling from a single penny within a time limit. The 2022 30-day LA→NC run got more than 190M views and raised $1.38M for Feeding America. — [Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2022/06/30/ryan-trahan-charity-vlog-series-views-raises-mrbeast/)
- 2023 Paris→NY in one week raised more than $400K for Water.org. — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2023/07/07/ryan-trahan-penny-challenge-international-water-org/)
- In "50 States in 50 Days" (2025, $11.5M for St. Jude), donors of $50K+ could trigger a "Wheel of Doom" that forced the hosts into tricky situations. *The audience controls the punishment.* — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Trahan); [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/07/30/ryan-trahan-haley-pham-50-states-st-jude-charity/)

**Yes Theory**
- Origin: "Project 30", meaning 30 never-done-before things in 30 days, one video a day, to get out of their comfort zone. That became the "Seek Discomfort" ethos. — [Rebel Essex](https://www.rebelessex.com/2018/11/the-challenge-of-seeking-discomfort/)
- Later work leaned on the kindness of strangers: asking strangers for favours, and the "Abandoned In" series (24 h in an unfamiliar city with no food or money). — [Rebel Essex](https://www.rebelessex.com/2018/11/the-challenge-of-seeking-discomfort/); [McGill News](https://mcgillnews.mcgill.ca/the-yes-theory-explores-the-upside-of-discomfort/)

**Jet Lag: The Game (Hide + Seek seasons)**
- One hider, the others seek. Seekers ask question types (matching, measuring, photo) and use tools (thermometer, radar). The hider draws cards, including **curses** that restrict the seekers, e.g. the "Curse of the Cairn": seekers must build a rock tower before asking another question. Others named in coverage include the curse of the impressionable consumer and the curse of the jammed door. — [Plugged In](https://www.pluggedin.com/youtube-reviews/jet-lag-the-game/); [TV Tropes / fan sources via search](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Podcast/TheLayover)
- Built-in safety rules: no exceeding speed limits, at least one meal a day, and play pauses overnight. Scales exist for city, region and nation. — [Plugged In](https://www.pluggedin.com/youtube-reviews/jet-lag-the-game/)

**Dude Perfect (Overtime)**
- "Wheel Unfortunate": a name drawn from a hat (sometimes by a celebrity guest) spins a wheel of punishments. Examples: "Spray Tan Level 10" (Cody came back deep orange), and running a mile in a mascot costume (Garrett, picked by Rob Gronkowski). Recurring segments like "Cool not Cool" are popular enough to anchor their live tour. — [Dude Perfect Wiki](https://dudeperfect.fandom.com/wiki/Overtime_1); [video summary](https://tube.archivarix.net/summary/-qATVnpaHTo); [CultureMap](https://dallas.culturemap.com/eventdetail/dude-perfect-thats-happy-dallas)

### Inferences
- **For a night race**, the strongest transferable mechanics are:
  1. **A secret imposter per team** (Beta Squad "6 vs 1"). One teen gets a secret objective through their phone, and the team guesses at the finish.
  2. **Curses and sabotage cards** (Jet Lag): finishing a mission earns a curse to fire at another team, e.g. "the next team must sing their answer" or "must walk holding hands for 3 minutes". This gives team-vs-team contact without physical confrontation.
  3. **A punishment wheel** (Dude Perfect, Trahan's Wheel of Doom) at a checkpoint for the last-placed team. It is visible, random and filmable.
  4. **Stair-stepping** (MrBeast): missions escalate in absurdity and points through the night, with the biggest reveal saved for the finale.
  5. **A "most money / most X in N minutes" race** (Beta Squad), e.g. "collect as many different signatures / languages / grandmothers' blessings as you can in 15 minutes".
- Money stakes and eating challenges are big on these channels but bring legal and safety problems for minors. Eating should stay mild ("taste three unknown foods"). Prizes should be visible (a physical trophy on stage), not cash bets between teens.
- "Last to leave" does not fit a moving race, but a small version can work at the finish (last team holding a plank or a balloon wins a bonus).

### Gaps
- No verified all-time view rankings for Sidemen or Beta Squad (the trackers disagree). Episode-level view counts for Jet Lag and Dude Perfect were not collected.
- No primary source found on which specific Yes Theory stranger videos performed best.
- Teen-specific audience data (age breakdowns per channel) was not found.

## The Amazing Race: iconic Roadblocks and Detours, and what makes them memorable on camera

### Takeaway
The most iconic Roadblock is the hay bale task in Sweden (Season 6). It is a needle-in-a-haystack search with a visible failure state, and it was brought back as the show's first "Switchback". A large, finite search field with few hidden prizes produces real despair and sudden joy on camera.

### Cited Findings
- Format: a Roadblock is done by one team member only, and the team must choose who does it **before** reading the full task. — [Wikipedia, The Amazing Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Amazing_Race) (via search summary)
- Season 6, Stockholm: teams unrolled hay bales to find hidden clues, with 270 bales and only 20 clues (per fan and listing sources). Lena Jensen unrolled about 100 bales over roughly 8 hours without finding a clue, until host Phil came to tell her she had been eliminated. These figures were not verified against CBS. — [BingeBase S6](https://www.bingebase.com/the-amazing-race-2001/season-6); [TV Guide](https://www.tvguide.com/news/tonights-tv-hot-1011632/)
- Season 15 brought the hay bales back as the series' first-ever **Switchback** (repeating an infamous past task), framed by a reviewer as a chance to "right one of the wrongs in its past". Later Switchbacks include a sand-castle dig in Bora Bora (a hay-bale variant) and a cheese-wheel task in Switzerland. — [Cultural Learnings](https://cultural-learnings.com/page/100/); [TheTVDB](https://thetvdb.com/series/the-amazing-race/episodes/253605)

### Inferences
- **"Choose your player before you read the task"** is a perfect fit for teams of 4 to 12. It forces a funny, consequential decision ("who's the brave one?") and gets quiet teens involved. Example card: "Pick ONE player. Then open." The hidden task might be "your player must sing the national anthem to a falafel seller."
- **Needle in a haystack** can be adapted safely at night: a cluster of 30 identical envelopes or chalk symbols in a plaza, only 3 of which carry the code. It gives visible frustration and a clear success moment.
- **Detour = choose between two tasks** (brains or brawn). This gives teams agency and creates debate content.
- A **Switchback** suits a series of events: re-running the hardest mission of the previous RushPoint race gives continuity.

### Gaps
- No authoritative ranked list of iconic Roadblocks or Detours was found. Other famously cited tasks (e.g. eating challenges, the Lyon/Rotterdam tasks) were not verified in this pass and are left out.

## GISH (Greatest International Scavenger Hunt): how absurdity, creativity and strangers are combined, and how it is judged

### Takeaway
GISH items mix an absurd image with a real-world location and a stranger or kindness element, and everything is proven by photo or video. The result is huge participation (55,000 registered in one year) and charity outcomes. The genius is the collision of incompatible things: formal wear at a sewage plant, swimsuit and floaties at a glacier.

### Cited Findings
- Exact item examples: "Do a dramatic reading of your grade-school report card." "Glaciers are melting—so act accordingly. Pose at a major glacier wearing a swimsuit with floaties." "Tour a sewage treatment plant dressed in formal attire with an accompanying violinist or flutist." "Have a tea party with a pediatric cancer patient, where you're dressed as a character from 'Alice in Wonderland.'" "Get a child to write a letter to the universe. Launch the letter into orbit." — [Yahoo, Inside the World's Greatest Scavenger Hunt pt 1](https://nz.finance.yahoo.com/news/inside-worlds-greatest-scavenger-hunt-part-1-184128389.html)
- Another documented item is a Storm Trooper in full costume cleaning a pool. A past item asked a published sci-fi author to write a story involving Collins, the Queen of England and an "elopus". — [TulsaKids (via search)](https://www.tulsakids.com/gishwhes/)
- Format: teams of 15, one week, about 200 items, photo or video proof per item, and a $20 entry fee to charity. About 55,000 people registered in one year. Seven Guinness World Records. More than $1M raised, plus blood donations and voter registrations. 2016 raised $250K for Syrian refugee families. — [Yahoo pt 1](https://nz.finance.yahoo.com/news/inside-worlds-greatest-scavenger-hunt-part-1-184128389.html)
- The prize was a trip with Misha Collins. Collins announced the 2019 winners in person at the Vancouver Art Gallery (a trip to Laos for all 15 members), and runners-up had to complete an extra final challenge. — [Prince George Citizen](https://www.princegeorgecitizen.com/local-news/video-bc-team-wins-greatest-international-scavenger-hunt-1978779)
- Collins on his college scavenger hunt: "one of the most educational aspects of my college experience, and infused with the most joy." — [Yahoo pt 1](https://nz.finance.yahoo.com/news/inside-worlds-greatest-scavenger-hunt-part-1-184128389.html)
- Status: Fanlore lists the event as 2011 to 2022, which suggests it may have ended. — [Fanlore](https://next.fanlore.org/wiki/Greatest_International_Scavenger_Hunt)

### Inferences
- **GISH formula = [mundane place] × [incongruous costume/prop] × [an art form or a stranger] × [proof by photo].** A Jerusalem night version: "Recreate a famous painting with a stranger at the shuk entrance", "Serenade a closed shop window as if it's a balcony in Verona", "Hold a 30-second formal press conference about a pigeon."
- GISH's kindness items (the tea party with a patient) show that "good deed" missions can be shareable. A night version could be "buy a stranger a pastry and get their best life advice on camera."
- A long item list with more items than can be done (200 in a week) forces teams to choose, which suits a 2.5 h race: offer more photo missions than can be completed, and score them by difficulty or creativity.

### Gaps
- No primary source on GISH's judging rubric (how creativity is scored versus mere completion). The articles say only that photo or video proof is submitted. A Yahoo part 2 article exists but was not fetched.

## Street challenge content with strangers: mechanics that don't feel like harassment

### Takeaway
The safe, shareable stranger mechanics found in this research are the ones where the stranger is a willing collaborator or a beneficiary. Examples: Yes Theory's "ask strangers for favours / kindness", GISH's tea party and the "letter to the universe" style items, and Taskmaster's #1 task (five minutes of conversation, then a song about the person). The stranger becomes the hero of the clip, never the butt of the joke.

### Cited Findings
- Yes Theory's stranger work is framed around "the kindness of strangers" and overcoming one's own insecurity (asking strangers for favours), not around pranking them. — [Rebel Essex](https://www.rebelessex.com/2018/11/the-challenge-of-seeking-discomfort/); [McGill News](https://mcgillnews.mcgill.ca/the-yes-theory-explores-the-upside-of-discomfort/)
- Taskmaster's top-ranked task turns a short conversation with a stranger into a performed song about her, so the stranger is celebrated. — [BuzzFeed](https://www.buzzfeed.com/hopelasater/best-taskmaster-tasks-ranked)
- Beta Squad's "6 vs 1 Secret X" builds on Jubilee's Odd One Out, a consensual, structured social game with strangers. — [Drop Media](https://www.dropmedia.co.uk/?p=5697)
- The Sidemen's most-viewed stranger format, "Tinder In Real Life", is staged with consenting participants, not passers-by (42M to 83M views per episode, figures conflict). — [Trill Mag](https://www.trillmag.com/entertainment/has-gen-z-lost-interest-in-the-sidemen/)

### Inferences
- **Rules for stranger missions (proposed):** (1) the stranger can say no, and a "no" scores points too ("film the most polite rejection"); (2) the stranger is complimented or helped, never mocked; (3) adults only by choice, never children; (4) no filming of people who did not agree; (5) a time cap of about 2 minutes per interaction.
- **Patterns that work:** "Get a stranger to teach you a word / a dance / a blessing in their language", "Ask a shopkeeper for the best thing they sell and why", "Find someone born in a different decade from each team member", "Collect 3 strangers' answers to one would-you-rather and predict the majority before asking."
- Night in Jerusalem (closed shops, religious neighbourhoods, Shabbat sensitivities) narrows who can be approached. Missions should point to busy, mixed areas (Ben Yehuda, Mahane Yehuda bars at night).

### Gaps
- No sources found that analyse "would you rather with strangers" or "talent on the spot" street channels directly, or their view counts. No research found on the line between consent and harassment in street content. This is a real gap and needs a dedicated search (e.g. Jubilee, street interview TikTok creators, Israeli "שאלות ברחוב" accounts).

## What structural elements make a task shareable

### Takeaway
Across sources, shareability comes from high-arousal emotion (amusement, awe, mild anxiety) rather than positive versus negative feeling. It also needs a concept simple enough to grasp in seconds, an unknown outcome withheld until a sharp payoff, visible escalation, and a public, comparable result. Each mission should produce one 10 to 20 second clip with a clear setup, an attempt, and a win or fail beat.

### Cited Findings
- Berger and Milkman's NYT-article study: sharing is driven by the physiological arousal an emotion creates, not by whether it is positive or negative. Sample size is reported as about 3,000 or about 7,000 articles depending on the source, and the sources conflict. In experiments, anxious or amused viewers shared more than sad or content ones. — [APS press release](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/why-do-we-share-our-feelings-with-others.html); [CustomerThink](https://customerthink.com/?p=65382)
- STEPPS = Social currency, Triggers, Emotion ("when we care, we share"), Public, Practical value, Stories. — [Unruly / Berger](https://unruly.co/blog/article/2015/10/26/jonah-berger-6-key-stepps-to-creating-contagious-content/)
- Simplicity, the first-minute hook, "show don't tell", a withheld payoff, abrupt endings, escalating stakes, and a "wow" moment early on. — [MrBeast memo via Jarvis](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)
- A visible failure state makes a moment iconic: Lena unrolling about 100 hay bales for 8 hours with no clue became the show's most revisited task. — [BingeBase](https://www.bingebase.com/the-amazing-race-2001/season-6)
- Contrast and incongruity are the core of GISH's most-quoted items (formal attire at a sewage plant, floaties at a glacier). — [Yahoo](https://nz.finance.yahoo.com/news/inside-worlds-greatest-scavenger-hunt-part-1-184128389.html)

### Inferences
- **A shareable mission checklist** (each mission should tick at least 4):
  1. A one-line brief understandable in 5 seconds (MrBeast simplicity).
  2. A built-in reveal: a secret rule, an opened envelope, or footage played backwards.
  3. Visible stakes or a timer on screen ("60 seconds").
  4. Incongruity: wrong outfit, wrong place, wrong tone (GISH).
  5. A clear fail state that is funny rather than humiliating (the hay bales, the potato).
  6. Comparable output across teams, so the finish-line screening compares interpretations (Taskmaster studio).
  7. Social currency: the result makes the teen look creative, brave or funny (STEPPS), not stupid.
  8. Clip length of 10 to 20 s by design (e.g. "film exactly 15 seconds").
- **Finish-line "studio segment":** project the best clips per mission, let a host judge "most X", and announce points live. That is the share moment, and it creates the reason to film well during the race.
- High-arousal emotions to aim for at night: amusement (absurd tasks), mild fear or tension (a timer, a curse fired at you), and awe (a big final reveal over the Old City walls).

### Gaps
- No data found on optimal clip length for teen sharing on TikTok or Reels specifically. The 10 to 20 s guidance is inference.
- No studies found specific to event or race-generated content sharing among teens.
