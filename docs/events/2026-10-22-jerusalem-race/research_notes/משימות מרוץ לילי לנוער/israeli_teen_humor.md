# Israeli teen humor and online culture (ages 15-18), 2025-2026, for a Jerusalem night race

Research date: 2026-10-18. The web surfaced little Hebrew writing that is both recent and specific to teens. Most Hebrew coverage is adult explainers, parent panic pieces, and creator profiles. Several points below are therefore inferences, and they are labelled as such. Older material is dated where it appears.

## 1. Dominant humor styles and the Israeli creators teens follow

### Takeaway
Israeli creator culture in 2025-2026 has two strands. One is POV/character skit comedy and lifestyle-comedy "it girl" content on TikTok and Instagram. The other is large-production challenge and vlog content on YouTube (Soltiz, Dylan Dror, Inda Game). The YouTube challenge strand skews toward kids and young teens. Older teens (15-18) are likely to read it as aimed at younger siblings.

### Cited Findings
- ice.co.il's 2026 "Star of the Web" list (dated 11/9/2026) names these creators and formats:
  - Hili Toledano: lifestyle, fashion and comedy across TikTok, Instagram and YouTube. makoZ TikToker of the Year 2025. Hosts a yes talk show ("Twenty-Something"). Her community is called "היליקלוז".
  - Idan Telem / Inda Game: gaming, challenges and vlogs. First independent Israeli creator over 1M Hebrew YouTube subscribers.
  - Soltiz (Uri Ben Ari, Shoval Eliezer, Neve Bar Moav, Paz Sagi): extreme challenges and productions, 500K+ YouTube subscribers. Released a film in July 2026 and sells collectible cards. The article says their audience is largely children and teens.
  - Dylan Dror: extreme challenges on YouTube, about 600K subscribers. Runs "Dyland" entertainment centers.
  - Amit Mizrachi: POV comedy skits on TikTok and Instagram. Appears in Kan Educational's "Motel Hotel".
  - Miryam Segal: character sketches and POV comedy, millions of views.
  - Matan Skaytel: humor built on Yemenite heritage. A 100K-follower meetup in Rosh HaAyin drew about 1,000 teens.
  - Yuli Waizman: influencer content including faith-related material.

  The article says these creators reach "hundreds of thousands of teens and young adults" overall. — [ice.co.il](https://www.ice.co.il/culture/news/article/1126971)
- TikTok Israel's 2025 wrap: Ness & Stilla were Artist of the Year at TikTok Awards 2024. Their "רשימת קניות" was among the most-used TikTok sounds of 2025. Nasrin Kadri performed "לא לפנות אלי". Creators on the panel included Dani Buller, Ben Shai and Tom Shaked. A religious 16-year-old, Aharon Liani (about 85K TikTok followers, French-Israeli accent), was listed among new creators for 2025. Two Israelis made TikTok's global Discover List 2026 for the first time. — [ice.co.il search summary](https://www.ice.co.il/culture/news/article/1126971) (search-snippet level; individual article pages not fetched)
- Soltiz's chocolate bar drew hundreds of children and teens to a Carrefour in Kfar Saba. Soltiz and Dylan Dror appeared together in a Hanukkah stage show ("Sol Stars"). Dror said his videos could bring about 8,000 people a day to a mall. — [mako](https://www.mako.co.il/makoz-viral/Article-1ddba967fc12b91027.htm), [gadgety 2023 YouTube recap (older)](https://www.gadgety.co.il/?p=319177)
- Street-interview format in Israel, from a mako profile of a 12-year-old street interviewer, December 2023 (older). Named Israeli street formats include:
  - Rina, who asks strangers about their dream
  - Galgul, who searches for "the funniest joke in Israel"
  - Alex Schulz, described as a TikTok star
  - matchmaking ("שידוכים") street videos, which the article says became a trend

  The profile's lessons: the creator keeps filming after people say no, and some people decline to be filmed once they hear it is for social media. — [mako](https://www.mako.co.il/nexter-news/Article-14687ea1de67c81026.htm)
- A Haaretz piece (June 2025) covers a trend set to the song "נולדתי לשלום": young people list every war and operation since they were born. It shows dark, self-aware generational humor about the security situation. — [Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.co.il/gallery/social/2025-06-22/ty-article-magazine/.premium/00000197-87fb-d6c0-a1b7-effb1e2f0000) (paywalled, headline and snippet only)

### Inferences
- POV skits ("POV: you're the team that…"), deadpan character bits, and "Israel's funniest X" street quests are native formats that teens already recognise. Missions shaped like them (film a 15-second POV, find the best joke from a stranger) will feel current.
- Copying Soltiz or Dylan Dror-style "extreme challenges" may read as a little-brother format to 16-17 year olds, because that audience skews younger (kids queueing for chocolate bars and collectible cards).
- Dark generational self-irony (the "נולדתי לשלום" trend) exists, but war humor is risky in a mixed group that may include bereaved families or relatives of hostages or soldiers. Avoid it.

### Gaps
- No audience-age breakdown for any Israeli creator was found. "Popular with 15-18" versus "popular with 10-13" is inferred, not measured.
- No Hebrew data on teen YouTube versus TikTok versus Instagram share for 2025-2026.

## 2. Current slang and memes, and which are dead

### Takeaway
Israeli teen slang in 2025-2026 is mostly English internet slang transliterated into Hebrew: ריל, ריז, קרינג', NPC, פיק, לנרמל, רד פלאג, פיק מי, סימפ, ברו, וייב, פלקס, גלואו אפ. It sits on top of long-lived Hebrew praise words built from military vocabulary (פגז, טיל, פיצוץ, אש, הורס). Once a word shows up in a "dictionary for boomers" article for parents, it is already on its way to being cringe when an adult uses it.

### Cited Findings
- A Channel 14 "dictionary for the beginner boomer" (31.08.2026) lists these as current kids' slang: Glow Up / Glow Down, Boomer, Peak (פיק), לנרמל / נירמול, Real (ריל, "true, I agree"), Rizz (ריז), Simp, Pick Me, NPC, Bro (ברו), Cringe (קרינג', "embarrassing but overdone"), Literally (ליטרלי), Vibe, Red/Green Flag, Iconic (אייקוני), Flex. — [Channel 14](https://www.c14.co.il/article/1685335)
- Hebrew praise slang from military words: פגז, טיל, פיצוץ, אש, הורס (ynet immigrant-kids dictionary, about August 2022, older but still in use). — [ynet](https://www.ynet.co.il/judaism/article/sk00yeeiko)
- Older but still-used basics: "אחי" as a general address, "טוב אחי" (tone-dependent agreement or praise), "באמאש'ך" (skepticism). This ynet piece is roughly a decade old. — [ynet](https://www.ynet.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-4582194,00.html)
- An earlier Hebrew teen-made slang dictionary (Ruvik Rosenthal, about 2022) has "קרינג'י" meaning weird or embarrassing, sometimes gross. — [ruvik.co.il](https://www.ruvik.co.il/%D7%94%D7%98%D7%95%D7%A8-%D7%94%D7%A9%D7%91%D7%95%D7%A2%D7%99/2022/1422.aspx)
- TheMarker's July 2024 guide to current slang ("skibidi", older) shows these terms had reached Israeli mainstream press by mid-2024. — [TheMarker](https://www.themarker.com/captain-internet/2024-07-10/ty-article/00000190-97f8-d9cc-a1b2-fffd968e0000)
- 2025 global slang that reached Israel (see section 5): 6-7, aura / aura farming, clanker, crashout, chopped, delulu, mogging, huzz, rage bait, slop. — [Mental Floss](https://www.mentalfloss.com/language/slang/kid-slang-terms-2025-decoded), [Fox 9](https://www.fox9.com/news/gen-alpha-slang-2025-meaning-behind-words-like-clanker-67-crashout), [YPulse](https://www.ypulse.com/article/2025/12/08/ypulses-gen-z-words-of-the-year/)

### Inferences
- "Dead" signals: Skibidi and rizz were the most common slang in UK classrooms by 2025 ([Teacher Tapp](https://teachertapp.com/articles/down-with-the-kids-slang-in-british-classrooms-2025/)). Words that saturated among 10-12 year olds and teachers (skibidi, rizz, sigma, Italian brainrot names) are likely to make 16-17 year olds roll their eyes when an organizer uses them sincerely. They may still work ironically, used by the players themselves. This is inference; no Israeli source rates which words are dead.
- Safe rule for mission copy: an adult or organizer voice should not use teen slang. Write in plain, confident, dry Hebrew, and leave room for the players to bring the slang (for example, "rate this from 1 to aura" as an open prompt, not as the organizer's own voice).

### Gaps
- No 2025-2026 Hebrew source, by linguists or by teens themselves, lists Israeli-born new slang or says which words are "dead". The Academy of the Hebrew Language and Kan Educational may have material that was not found.
- Fetching the Israel Hayom slang piece failed (403).

## 3. What makes something קרינג' or ילדותי, and what adults get wrong

### Takeaway
Teens judge an activity as cringe when it is forced, announced as "fun", infantilizing, or makes them wait or perform alone with no purpose. Boredom and condescension are the core failures, not games as such. "Cringe" in teen usage means embarrassing because it is overdone.

### Cited Findings
- "Cringe (קרינג')" is defined as something embarrassing, often because it is overdone. — [Channel 14](https://www.c14.co.il/article/1685335)
- Youth-work practitioner Jonathan McKee writes that kids, even tough kids, don't hate games, they hate being bored. He calls announcing "now we're going to play a game!" one of the worst moves. He never uses relays, because most participants stand in line waiting. Activities that put three people on stage while everyone else watches don't fit groups that need to burn energy. — [The Source for Youth Ministry](https://thesource4ym.com/tough-kids-are-destroying-my-games)
- A youth-ministry writer citing Springtide Research says teens want fun, but "it is not the small group format, it is the infantilizing." — [Goodreads author blog](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/25792570-teens-expect-to-learn-in-church) (secondary citation of research; US church context)
- Israeli youth-movement context: a Kipa column describes a Bnei Akiva counsellor whose by-the-book activity failed because the kids called it boring. The advice is to start from the kids' world, not the activity booklet. — [Kipa](https://www.kipa.co.il/הורים-וילדים/חינוך/%d7%91%d7%a2%d7%95%d7%96-%d7%a7%d7%93%d7%99%d7%9e%d7%94-%d7%91%d7%a0%d7%a2/?amp=1)
- Israeli youth movements run on "נוער מדריך נוער" (youth leading youth) and autonomy. — [Hamichlol](https://www.hamichlol.org.il/%D7%AA%D7%A0%D7%95%D7%A2%D7%95%D7%AA_%D7%94%D7%A0%D7%95%D7%A2%D7%A8)
- An Israel Hayom piece on the "Gen Z stare" says young people prefer authenticity to "playing the game" of fake politeness. — [Israel Hayom](https://www.israelhayom.co.il/lifestyle/diets-and-nutrition/article/18430927)
- Calcalist notes that humor ages fast because it depends on references. — [Calcalist](https://www.calcalist.co.il/article/b1qp7i00sa)

### Inferences (design rules for missions)
- Cringe triggers to avoid:
  - organizer-voice exclamation marks and "!!!" hype
  - kindergarten physical tasks with no twist: build a pyramid, hop on one foot, make a human chain
  - costumes the organizer hands out
  - singing or dancing alone in front of the group
  - mandatory "group cheer"
  - an adult using teen slang
  - tasks that only one team member does while the rest wait
- What can save a "silly" task:
  - give it a real, competitive or clever frame: a points trade-off, a secret, a bluff, a rival team
  - make it the team's own creative choice, not a prescribed act
  - make it filmable as content they would actually post: a short POV, a deadpan reenactment, a "found the most X in Jerusalem" quest
  - let the joke be on the situation, never on the player
- Irony is the bridge. A deliberately absurd task with a deadpan, bureaucratic instruction ("Mission 7: obtain official confirmation from a stranger that your team is the best-dressed in the city. Form must be signed.") reads as knowing. The same task written as "Have fun getting someone to say you're the coolest!!" reads as childish.
- Autonomy matters. Giving teams choice ("choose 2 of 4") fits the "youth leading youth" ethos.

### Gaps
- No Israeli survey or study of teens on what they consider cringe in organized activities was found. The practitioner sources are mostly American.

## 4. Street-video, prank and stranger-challenge culture

### Takeaway
Israeli street content that works is positive and stranger-flattering: ask strangers their dream, the funniest joke, matchmaking. Content that films people in humiliating or aggressive situations is policed and condemned, and filming people without consent is a known friction point.

### Cited Findings
- Israeli street-interview formats: Rina (strangers' dreams), Galgul (Israel's funniest joke), matchmaking street videos, a child interviewer with soldiers at central stations. Many people agree, some refuse once they learn it is for social media. (Dec 2023, older) — [mako](https://www.mako.co.il/nexter-news/Article-14687ea1de67c81026.htm)
- Israeli police in Jerusalem arrested a suspect who allegedly assaulted a passerby and uploaded it to TikTok. Others allegedly abused a Haredi teenager and streamed it live on TikTok. Police say they monitor social-media offences. — [JDN police items](https://www.jdn.co.il/?p=1737875), [JDN](https://www.jdn.co.il/news/law/1720785/)

### Inferences
- Good stranger missions are ones where the stranger is the hero and gets something:
  - collect a compliment, a blessing, a recommendation, a joke, or a life tip
  - get a stranger to teach the team a word or a song
  - have a stranger judge something ("rate our team photo out of 10")
  - give something away: a compliment card, a candy
- Bad stranger missions:
  - filming strangers without asking
  - getting strangers to do something embarrassing
  - startling, fake emergencies, or anything that looks like a threat at night
  - approaching lone women or men in ways that could read as harassment
  - asking for phone numbers
  - approaching Haredi passersby with gimmicks in religious neighbourhoods
- Jerusalem at night adds security sensitivity. Anything resembling a suspicious object, running at people, masks, fake weapons or toy guns, or shouting can trigger real alarm. Never design those in.
- Teens filming each other is fine and wanted. Faces of strangers should only appear with explicit consent.

### Gaps
- No Israeli article specifically on prank culture backlash in 2025-2026 was found. The police items are about assault, not pranks.

## 5. Global trends that crossed into Israel

### Takeaway
6-7, Italian brainrot (Tralalero Tralala, Ballerina Cappuccina and others), skibidi, rizz, aura / aura farming, NPC, sigma and clanker all reached Israeli kids in 2025. Hebrew press frames them as children's phenomena (ages 8-12 in the Walla piece), so to older teens they read as little-kid meme culture unless used ironically.

### Cited Findings
- Italian brainrot: AI-made surreal hybrid creatures with mock-Italian names. The trend began in early 2025 with a "Trallallero Trallallà" sound on an AI shark in sneakers. — [ABC News](https://amp.abcnews.com/family/story/parents-viral-italian-brainrot-trend-taking-kids-screens-121751636)
- Walla (11 July 2025), headlined "Gibberish, screaming and the Cappuccino Ballerina: our children no longer speak Hebrew": Israeli children aged 8, 11 and 12 chant the phrases at home and with friends. The content reaches kids through smartwatches, ringtones and notebook covers. The piece notes that very young children now lead some trends. — [Walla](https://www.walla.co.il/news/israel/3756963)
- 6-7: from Skrilla's "Doot Doot (6 7)" (2025). It is used meaninglessly, often to annoy adults, and banned in some schools as disruptive. A Kikar report covers hundreds of youths in New York marking "6-7 day" at 18:07. A Times of Israel "Letter from Israel" column says Israeli grandchildren brought 6-7 home, and an Israeli city used it as a summer-programme gimmick (June = 6, July = 7). — [Kikar](https://www.kikar.co.il/world-news/6-7-trend-celebration-new-york), [Times of Israel / Jewish Standard](https://jewishstandard.timesofisrael.com/sweet-6-7/), [Teacher Tapp](https://teachertapp.com/articles/down-with-the-kids-slang-in-british-classrooms-2025/), [Fox 9](https://www.fox9.com/news/gen-alpha-slang-2025-meaning-behind-words-like-clanker-67-crashout)
- Aura farming: actively trying to look cooler. Linked to the 2025 Indonesian "boat kid" dance-on-a-racing-boat clip. — [Mental Floss](https://www.mentalfloss.com/language/slang/kid-slang-terms-2025-decoded)
- Clanker (anti-robot / anti-AI slur from Star Wars) was Know Your Meme's meme of the month for August 2025 with about 19% of votes. — [Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/editorials/poll/see-the-winner-of-august-2025s-meme-of-the-month)
- Other 2025 terms: crashout, chopped, delulu, mogging, huzz, rage bait, slop. Teachers in the UK flagged misogynist Andrew Tate-linked terms and sexual slang ("gooning") among students. — [Teacher Tapp](https://teachertapp.com/articles/down-with-the-kids-slang-in-british-classrooms-2025/), [Quid](https://www.quid.com/knowledge-hub/resource-library/blog/from-mid-to-delulu-how-teen-language-evolved-in-2025), [YPulse](https://www.ypulse.com/article/2025/12/08/ypulses-gen-z-words-of-the-year/)
- Other big 2025 memes: "100 men vs 1 gorilla" debate; the Coldplay kiss-cam CEO. — [AOL](https://www.aol.com/best-memes-2025-far-090000392.html), [Know Your Meme roundup](https://knowyourmeme.com/editorials/meme-review/know-your-memes-top-internet-trends-and-memes-of-2025-roundup)

### Inferences
- The Times of Israel item shows that an Israeli municipality's official use of 6-7 is exactly the "adults co-opting a meme" move teens mock. Organizers should not build a mission called "6-7".
- Usable ironic hooks, where players perform the meme rather than the organizer:
  - "aura" as a scoring metaphor ("lose 100 aura points for asking for a hint")
  - NPC: get a stranger to stay deadpan, or play "find the real NPC"
  - "100 men vs 1 gorilla"-style absurd debate prompts
  - a Coldplay kiss-cam reenactment. This one is risky for a religious mixed group (see section 6): use only a non-romantic version, such as catching the organizer on camera.
- Avoid: sigma, alpha and Tate vocabulary (misogynist overtones), and gooning or other sexual slang.

### Gaps
- No Israeli data shows how many 15-18 year olds still use these terms, or whether they consider them dead.

## 6. Youth-movement game culture and the religious-Zionist audience

### Takeaway
Bnei Akiva is a mixed-gender movement, but modesty (צניעות), especially avoiding physical contact between boys and girls, is a central, actively debated norm. Mission design must assume no touching across genders, no dancing or singing performances by girls in front of boys (some families hold to kol isha), no romantic or dating framings, and nothing mocking Torah, mitzvot, Shabbat or religious figures.

### Cited Findings
- Bnei Akiva operates as a mixed framework. Branches see a fair amount of boy-girl contact, which some rabbis treat as reality and others criticise. — [Kipa Ask the Rabbi](https://www.kipa.co.il/שאל-את-הרב/%d7%a4%d7%a2%d7%99%d7%9c%d7%95%d7%aa-%d7%9e%d7%a2%d7%95%d7%a8%d7%91%d7%aa-%d7%91%d7%a0%d7%99%d7%9d-%d7%95%d7%91%d7%a0%d7%95%d7%aa/)
- A rabbinic modesty framework for a "mixed, modest youth movement" (Rav David Levi) calls for removing physical touch from the movement's language and behaviour. It says modesty occupies a central place in the movement's work. — [Kipa](https://www.kipa.co.il/שאל-את-הרב/%D7%90%D7%99%D7%9A-%D7%9E%D7%A1%D7%91%D7%99%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%9D-%D7%91%D7%90%D7%95%D7%A4%D7%9F-%D7%91%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%A8-%D7%9E%D7%94-%D7%94%D7%91%D7%A2%D7%99%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%91%D7%A4%D7%A2%D7%95%D7%9C%D7%94-%D7%9E%D7%A2%D7%95%D7%A8%D7%91%D7%AA/)
- What counts as permitted varies with family and community modesty norms. Haredi publics reject mixed camps entirely. — [Kipa](https://www.kipa.co.il/שאל-את-הרב/%d7%9e%d7%97%d7%a0%d7%94-%d7%9e%d7%a2%d7%95%d7%a8%d7%91-%d7%91%d7%91%d7%a0%d7%a2/), [yeshiva.org.il](https://www.yeshiva.org.il/ask/125449)
- Youth-movement principles: autonomy and youth leading youth. Counsellors are advised to build from the kids' world, not the booklet. — [Hamichlol](https://www.hamichlol.org.il/%D7%AA%D7%A0%D7%95%D7%A2%D7%95%D7%AA_%D7%94%D7%A0%D7%95%D7%A2%D7%A8), [Kipa](https://www.kipa.co.il/הורים-וילדים/חינוך/%d7%91%d7%a2%d7%95%d7%96-%d7%a7%d7%93%d7%99%d7%9e%d7%94-%d7%91%d7%a0%d7%a2/?amp=1)

### Inferences
- Formats youth-movement teens know and still like when done well: night games (משחקי לילה), treasure and clue hunts, "שטח" games with stealth and capture, team rivalry, riddles tied to place and history, and tasks with real secrecy. The race format itself is native to them. What makes it childish is kindergarten execution (sing a song, do a pyramid), not the genre.
- Offence list for a religious-Zionist mixed audience:
  - cross-gender touch tasks (human chains, carrying, holding hands, partner dances)
  - "kiss-cam", rizz, "date" or pickup-line missions
  - asking a girl to sing solo on camera
  - immodest costume or clothing dares
  - jokes about Torah, prayer, the Kotel, rabbis or Shabbat
  - anything involving entering or filming inside synagogues or yeshivot uninvited
  - war, terror, hostage or bereavement humor
  - mocking Haredim or Arabs
  - missions in religiously sensitive Jerusalem areas (Old City holy sites, Me'a She'arim) that make players gawk at or film residents
- Jerusalem-native humor that is safe across religious and secular teens:
  - Jerusalem in-jokes: the weather, the light rail, Machane Yehuda vendors, municipal bureaucracy, "Tel Aviv vs Jerusalem"
  - Hebrew wordplay
  - deadpan "official" mission language
  - asking strangers for blessings or recommendations
  - absurd but harmless collection quests ("bring proof of the weirdest shop sign on Jaffa Road")

### Gaps
- No official Bnei Akiva document setting rules for mixed activities was found online. Practice varies by branch and family, so the event should confirm norms with participating branch counsellors.
- No sources found on what Bnei Akiva teens specifically find childish in pe'ulot or masa'ot in 2025-2026.
