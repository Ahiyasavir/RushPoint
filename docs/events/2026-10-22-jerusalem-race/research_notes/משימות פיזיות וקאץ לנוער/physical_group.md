# Physical group challenges where headcount is an advantage (night city race, Jerusalem, teens 4-12, no cross-gender contact, no bought props)

> How to read this file. Every fact about a show or source carries an inline link. The
> ADAPTATIONS (the RushPoint version of each challenge, the no-contact rule, the scoring tiers)
> are design proposals derived from those facts and are therefore under "Inferences". Entries
> whose original mechanic I know only from general familiarity and could NOT confirm with a
> fetched source this session are marked **[unsourced]**: treat the attribution as a lead, not a fact.
>
> Universal no-contact rule used throughout (marked **NC**): bodies never touch; any "chain" or
> "line" is made of objects (shoelaces, jackets, bags, phones) or of people standing at a gap of
> at least one arm; same-gender pairs where a pair is needed; nobody lifts anybody.
> Universal safety rule (marked **SAFE**): wide pedestrian plaza only (e.g. Safra Square, Kikar
> Zion, the Mamilla promenade, Ben Yehuda pedestrian mall), no running across or beside a road or
> the light rail tracks on Jaffa Road, no climbing street furniture above knee height, no water.

## Korean variety shows (Running Man, 2 Days 1 Night, Infinite Challenge): street and market group missions, locals as hosts or judges

### Takeaway
The Korean shows supply the strongest template for "a local is the host or judge": Running Man has had landmark staff vote on winners and a market episode where the cast buys items and auctions them to staff whose tastes decide the winner, and 2 Days 1 Night has a "goblin shop" where supplies are won by beating the shopkeeper at games. I could not retrieve concrete, citable episode breakdowns for street missions that recruit strangers (Infinite Challenge in particular), so those remain gaps.

### Cited Findings
- Running Man began as an "urban action variety" in which MCs and guests race to complete missions at a landmark; from episode 48 it is one continuous race with missions woven through it — [Wikipedia: Running Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Running_Man_(TV_program))
- Episodes 44-47: missions were related to the staff of the landmark, and landmark staff voted for a winner and became that member's or team's supporters; the winners' supporters got a bonus from their boss — [Wikipedia: Running Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Running_Man_(TV_program))
- "Name Tag Elimination" was the opening mission in episodes 119, 131 and 144; other recurring missions include "Bells Hide and Seek" (eps 11-25), "Find the Guests", "Catch the Running Man", and a "Photo Zone Game" played at every landmark in early episodes — [Wikipedia: Running Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Running_Man_(TV_program))
- "One Chance" (eps 27-31): a single challenge whose success cleared the punishment for everyone — [Wikipedia: Running Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Running_Man_(TV_program))
- Running Man "Running Merchants" (aired 20 April 2025 per a pre-broadcast preview): two teams buy items and auction them to the show's staff; winning depends on reading the staff's tastes and quirky requests; the team with the most profit wins — [SBS Star](https://sbsstar.net/article/N1008068581/running-merchants-returns-running-man-cast-faces-off-in-hilarious-sales-showdown). ### Inferences
Catalogue entries derived from this block (numbering continues across the file):

1. **Vendor auction ("Running Merchants" adaptation).** Mechanic: each team gets a "budget" of in-game points, must find three street objects matching a brief from a vendor-judge (e.g. "something round, something that smells, something older than you" — found, not bought), then pitch them to the vendor, who awards points. Why size helps: more people fan out and return with more candidates, then the team picks the best. Camera: vendor deliberating, team pleading in unison. NC: pitching is verbal. Metric: vendor score 1-10 → gold 8+, silver 5-7, bronze <5. Caveat: needs a cooperative shop or kiosk owner briefed in advance.
2. **Beat the shopkeeper ("goblin shop" adaptation).** Mechanic: a kiosk owner or volunteer plays rock-paper-scissors (or "who blinks first") against team members in sequence; the team needs N consecutive wins to earn a clue. Why size helps: every member is another try in the sequence, and a bigger team can rotate in its "lucky" players. NC: none needed. Metric: number of attempts used → fewer = gold.
3. **Locals vote ("landmark staff vote" adaptation).** Mechanic: the team performs a 20-second show (a cheer, a frozen tableau) in front of passers-by and asks strangers to vote with a thumbs up on camera. Why size helps: louder, bigger show and more people asking. NC: performance is distanced. Metric: count of strangers' thumbs-up in a 60-second clip → 10+/5-9/1-4. Caveat: keep requests polite and short; minors approaching strangers at night needs a staff member nearby.
4. **Photo Zone game (Running Man early landmark game).** Mechanic: recreate a reference photo at a landmark exactly (poses, positions). Why size helps: references with many figures can only be matched by big teams. NC: poses side by side. Metric: staff judge similarity 1-3 stars.
5. **Name-tag elimination, no contact version.** The original involves ripping tags off backs — contact, so the adaptation is a **clothespin-free "sticker on the back" variant only within same gender**, or better, a "shadow tag": you are out if a rival photographs your team number written on paper on your bag. Why size helps: more lookouts. NC: photography only. SAFE: walking pace only, plaza boundaries. **[mechanic of the original sourced; the adaptation is a proposal]**
6. **"One Chance" for the whole race.** A single all-team challenge (e.g. every team in the plaza claps in sync once) whose success gives every team a bonus. Size helps because it is a crowd effort. Camera: the whole event cast in one frame.
7. **Bok-bul-bok lot draw as a penalty roulette.** Luck draw between members (paper lots in a cap) decides who does the forfeit task (sing a line, do 10 squats). Lightweight morale tool, not a scored challenge.

### Inferences
Adaptations (proposals):

8. **Phone-stack tower (Beast Games Block Stacking).** Stack the team's own bags/jackets/shoes into the tallest free-standing tower on a bench in 90 s, no hands touching it at the measurement. Size helps: more members = more material. NC: one builder at a time. Metric: cm via a phone measure app → 120+/80-119/<80. Camera: tower wobble, collapse = gold content. Shoes count as material too: everybody has two.
9. **Coin pong (Beast Games Giant Beer Pong).** Each member throws a coin from a line 3 m away into an upturned cap; every hit = team point. Size helps linearly (each member is a thrower). NC: none. Metric: hits in one round per member, scaled to team size (hits ÷ members) so a 4-team is not crushed → or deliberately NOT scaled where the brief is "numbers are an advantage".
10. **Fragile relay (Beast Games Glass Ball Relay).** A phone (in case) balanced flat on an open palm is passed down a line of members without being gripped — handoff is "place it on a bench, next one picks up", benches or a step between each person at 1.5 m gaps, never hand to hand. Size helps: longer line = longer, more dramatic run; score by time per handoff. NC: bench handoff. Camera: tracking shot along the line. Metric: seconds.
11. **Hold till the end (Red Ball Timer).** Everyone holds a coin at arm's length; the team must drop coins one at a time so that the LAST coin falls as close as possible to the 3:00 mark without phones visible. Size helps: more coins = more chances to land close. Metric: seconds off 3:00 → ≤2 s gold, ≤5 s silver, ≤10 s bronze.
12. **Blind step-in (Blindfold Circle).** Eyes closed (jacket over head is optional, eyes closed is enough), team must get EXACTLY K members (K announced, e.g. 3) to step into a chalk-free circle marked by four bags, without speaking, within 60 s. Size helps and hurts: it's a coordination puzzle that gets harder with size, so give bigger teams a bigger K. NC: circle wide (3 m). SAFE: flat plaza, staff spotter.
13. **Closest to the target (Briefcase Toss).** Each member slides a coin along the plaza paving toward a target (a phone face-up as the target); closest coin of the team counts. Size helps: more throws. Metric: cm.
14. **Flag race, no running (Beast Games Flag Race).** Eight objects (team's own items) placed at plaza spots; one member at a time fast-WALKS to fetch one; team with more items in 2 min. Size helps: rotation means fresh legs. SAFE: walking only, heel-toe rule.
15. **Falling tiles, paving-stone version.** Plaza paving grid = tiles. Staff calls out row/column "drops" each round; members on a dropped tile are out; can step one tile after each call. Size helps: more survivors to the end. NC: one person per tile, tiles are 50 cm+ apart in practice (use every second tile). Camera: from above (stairs).
16. **Dead hang → wall sit.** "Dead hang" requires a bar and has a fall risk; replace with a wall-sit against a building wall or a plank, team picks a champion OR all members sit and the team's score is the SUM of seconds. Sum version is the "numbers help" version. Metric: summed seconds.
17. **Red light, green light (SG:TC).** Classic; team must all cross a 20 m plaza strip; a member caught moving goes back to start; team time = when the last member arrives. Size helps negatively (more people to catch) — use it as a "big team handicap" leveller, or flip: team gets points for each member who reaches the line in 90 s. NC/SAFE: no contact, walking pace forced by the "freeze" mechanic itself.
18. **Human battleship (SG:TC Warships).** Two teams meet at a plaza, each secretly places its members on an imaginary 5x5 grid of paving squares while the other team turns away, then they alternate calling coordinates; a hit member sits down. Size helps: more "ships". NC: one person per square, squares spaced. Camera: drone-like overhead from a balcony/stairs. Best for two teams meeting at the same checkpoint.
19. **Circle of trust → "who touched the bag" (SG:TC).** Eyes closed in a wide circle, one member quietly moves an object (a cap) next to one person; that person must guess who put it there. Size helps: more suspects = more suspense. NC: object placed on the ground, not handed.
20. **Glass bridge → paving bridge.** A path of paving tiles where staff has secretly marked "safe" tiles on a phone; members cross one at a time choosing left/right, a wrong step sends that member back; later members learn from earlier ones. Size helps exactly as in the show: more members = more lives to discover the route. NC/SAFE: flat ground, no falls.
21. **Hole in the wall, frame version (Nōkabe).** Staff holds up (or shows on a phone) a silhouette of K figures in poses; the team must freeze in that exact formation within 10 s, photographed. Size helps: silhouettes have more figures than small teams have members, so big teams can match the 8-figure cards. NC: silhouettes designed with gaps between figures. Camera: the reveal "card vs. team" side by side. Metric: figures matched / total.
22. **Queue drawing (Taskmaster S9).** Members stand in a line facing forward; the last sees a picture, draws it on the back of... — contact: replace with "draw on a phone note and show only the next person for 3 s", passing back to front; the front person draws on paper/phone; compare. Size helps: longer chain = funnier distortion (the content is the funny result, not winning). NC: phone-only.
23. **Mime the list (Taskmaster S6).** One member mimes 10 items from a staff list; the rest shout guesses. Size helps: more guessers. Metric: items in 60 s → 8+/5-7/<5.
24. **Direct into the circle (Taskmaster S11).** One member eyes closed, team steers them by voice only into a circle of bags 15 m away across the plaza. Size helps/hurts: chaos of many voices — rule that each member may speak only one word in turn. SAFE: plaza only, staff spotter, no obstacles.
25. **Team anthem (Taskmaster S13).** Record a 20 s song about the team, everyone must sing a line. Size helps: choir. Camera: great for the end-of-night reel. Score: staff/vote.
26. **Exactly 9.58 seconds (Taskmaster S7).** The team's last member must arrive at a marker exactly X s after the first member leaves the start (walking). Size helps: chain of departures. Metric: error in tenths of a second.
27. **Slowest crossing (Taskmaster S12).** Cross a 5 m strip as slowly as possible but always moving; staff eliminates anyone seen stationary; team score = summed seconds. Hilarious on camera; size helps via sum.
28. **Recreate a computer game (Taskmaster S7).** Team physically performs Pac-Man/Tetris/Space Invaders in the plaza, filmed 30 s from above. Size helps: more sprites. NC: choreography with gaps.
29. **Camouflage (Taskmaster S4).** Team must hide all members in a 20 m area within 60 s using jackets/bags/street furniture; staff walks in and counts how many it spots in 15 s. Size helps: one point per unspotted member, so more members = more possible points. SAFE: no hiding near roads, behind parked cars, or in closed spaces.
30. **Most people + most items in one photo (Taskmaster S6 eggs).** One photo in which every member is visible AND holding the maximum number of distinct objects (coins, phones, keys, shoes), all held in own hands only. Size helps literally: score = people × items. NC: standing in a row, no shoulders/arms around each other.

## Youth movement / camp / team-building games that scale with headcount (human structures without touching, shapes, sync, freezes, object chains, sound, letters from above)

### Takeaway

Generic no-contact team-building lists exist but are thin on rules; the Israeli youth-movement material I searched for in Hebrew did not surface usable no-contact physical games, so most entries in this block are standard camp/team-building formats marked **[unsourced]**.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
Adaptations (proposals). Unless a link is given, these are common camp / team-building formats **[unsourced]**.

31. **Team freeze (Improv Everywhere Grand Central).** At a busy plaza, on a phone signal, the whole team freezes mid-action for 60 s while passers-by walk around them; one member films from outside. Size helps: the bigger the frozen group, the bigger the effect. Metric: seconds until first movement (staff watches) → 60/40/20. NC: freeze poses are solo. Camera: one of the most proven street stunts there is.
32. **Synchronized clap (GWR clapping ensemble).** The team must produce ONE clap that sounds like one — recorded and judged by the waveform on a phone voice-memo app (one spike vs. a smear). Size helps: more people = louder spike, but harder to sync; the difficulty is the fun. Metric: best of 3 attempts, staff judges "single / double / smear".
33. **Everyone jumps at once (sync jump photo).** Burst-mode photo; score = number of members airborne in the same frame. Size helps directly. NC: side by side, 1 m apart. SAFE: flat ground, no jumping from benches.
34. **Line up without talking (Surf Office line-up).** Order by birthday / shoe size / number of siblings in silence, in 60 s; staff checks. Size helps: harder, so give bigger teams more time or score by % correct. NC: gestures only.
35. **Living map of Israel (Surf Office living map).** Plaza = map of Israel (staff names north/south); members stand where they or their grandparents were born. Good as a warm opener; not scored.
36. **Body letters from above.** Spell the team name or "RUSHPOINT"/"ציון" with bodies lying or standing, photographed from stairs/upper level of a plaza (e.g. the steps at Safra Square or Mamilla's upper promenade). Size helps: more letters/strokes. NC: each body is one stroke; letters chosen so strokes don't meet at a person (strokes can end with a jacket or bag bridging the gap). Metric: letters legible in the photo (staff judges).
37. **Shoelace chain.** Tie all the team's shoelaces (and belts, phone cords, hoodie strings) into one line; measure length on the paving (paving stone count or phone measure). Size helps linearly. NC: objects only. Metric: meters → 12+/8-12/<8 (needs calibration with a real test).
38. **Jacket line / object line.** Lay out every object the team carries in one continuous line along the plaza in 60 s; measure. Size helps linearly; on camera it's a satisfying overhead reveal. Combine with 37 for the "longest chain of the night" leaderboard.
39. **One note (choir drone).** Whole team holds one note as long as possible; team score = seconds until the last voice stops (breathe in turn = staggered relay, smart teams discover it). Size helps through staggered breathing. Camera/audio: strong in a stone plaza.
40. **Count to 20 [unsourced, classic drama/camp game].** Team stands in a circle facing out, counts 1-20 with any member saying the next number at random; if two speak at once, restart. Size helps make it harder; score = highest number reached in 2 min.
41. **"How many of you can..."** Short tiered counts: how many members can stand on one leg with eyes closed for 30 s; how many can say the alphabet backwards; how many can touch their toes. Score = count. Size helps directly. NC: solo actions.
42. **Human shape from a card.** Staff shows a shape (triangle, star, Magen David, the number of the checkpoint); team forms it as a spaced outline, photographed from above. Size helps: more points on the outline. NC: outline points 1 m apart, lines implied.
43. **Domino wave.** Team in a long line performs a "stadium wave" (sit-stand / arms-up) passing down the line; filmed; score by smoothness or by how many waves in 30 s. Size helps: longer wave. NC: no contact needed.
44. **Mirror mob.** One leader moves, the whole team mirrors in sync for 30 s while a stranger tries to spot who's leading. Size helps: harder to spot the leader in a big group. Metric: did the stranger guess (yes/no) + seconds to guess.
45. **Echo chant.** Team shouts the race slogan; staff measures peak dB on a phone sound-meter app. Size helps directly. Metric: dB → 95+/85-95/<85 (calibrate; also respect neighbours, short single shout, not near residences late).
46. **Captain's Orders (Youth Group Games).** Staff calls commands ("to the ship", "captain's coming" = salute and freeze, "man overboard" — replace any pair/lift command with solo poses); last member to comply is out. Size helps: more survivors.
47. **Team Architect (Youth Group Games).** Build the tallest/most beautiful structure from only what the team carries (no street furniture moved). Covered by 8.

## Physical challenges that measure a number (meters, seconds, count) for tiered scoring

### Takeaway
Most entries above already produce a number; the cleanest tiering candidates are length (shoelace/object chain, metres), duration (freeze, held note, summed wall-sit seconds), count (airborne in one frame, items in a photo, mime guesses), and precision (seconds off a target time, cm from a target). Official record numbers can anchor "wow" tiers but none are directly comparable to a 4-12 teen team.

### Cited Findings
- Taskmaster's S6 egg task scored "most eggs and people" — a direct people×items count — [Sporcle (fan)](https://www.sporcle.com/games/SirDoris/taskmaster-task-winners-series-6)

### Inferences
- Use three metric families so teams of 4 vs 12 are treated fairly by design: (a) **"sum" metrics** where size is meant to win (chain length, summed seconds, airborne count, people × items) — the brief explicitly wants these; (b) **"per-member" metrics** (hits ÷ members) when a challenge should be fair; (c) **"precision" metrics** (seconds off target) that size barely affects. A balanced route mixes all three so a 4-person team isn't buried.
- Tier thresholds should be set per team-size band (4-6, 7-9, 10-12) for sum metrics, or the 12s sweep gold everywhere. This matches the product's existing automatic scoring presets, but the threshold numbers themselves need a field test.
- Photo/video proof fits the platform: `photo` tasks with staff review can carry the number (a photo of the measured chain, a burst frame) — the jump-count and people×items photos are self-evidencing.

### Gaps
- No source gives realistic teen-team benchmarks (e.g. typical shoelace chain per person, sustainable group held-note duration). Test with a real group before setting gold/silver/bronze.

## Safety for a night race on Jerusalem plazas

### Takeaway
No safety source was fetched in this session; the constraints below are the brief's own rules applied to each mechanic.

### Cited Findings
- (None retrieved. The Beast Games/Squid Game formats are run on closed sets; the Monster Truck Pull and Dead Hang show the type of strength/height task that must be dropped — [Looper](https://www.looper.com/1750674/beast-games-every-game-explained/).)

### Inferences

- Approaching strangers (3, 44) needs a staff member within sight and a polite script; stop at 22:00-ish near residential blocks for loud tasks (45).
- No-contact compliance is easiest when the rule is built into the geometry: one person per paving tile, objects as connectors, bench hand-offs, solo poses in silhouettes.
