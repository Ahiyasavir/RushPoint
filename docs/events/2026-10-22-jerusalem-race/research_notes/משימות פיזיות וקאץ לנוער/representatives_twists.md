# Representative mechanics and built-in "catch" twists for a night city race (teens, teams of 4-12, photographer per team, vendor-run stations)

Scope note: 15 tool calls. Sources are Wikipedia, community wikis (Jet Lag fandom, taskmaster.info), and press. Everything under "Inferences" is design application for the 22.10 Jerusalem race, not a sourced fact. The scoring tiers and verification ideas are my proposals; no source describes them.

## Representative mechanics (who does the task)

### Takeaway
The strongest proven representative mechanic is the Amazing Race Roadblock: the team commits to ONE member from a vague hint BEFORE the real task is revealed, with a per-person cap so the same strong player cannot take every one. Everything else (auction, vote, rep vs rep, team helps under a constraint) builds on that "commit before you know" moment, which is where the on-camera reaction lives.

### Cited Findings
- Roadblock: only one team member performs the task, chosen from a cryptic prompt read before the full task description; total Roadblocks per racer are limited; quitting costs a 4-hour penalty — [Wikipedia: The Amazing Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Amazing_Race)
- From Season 6, no individual racer can perform more than a set number of Roadblocks, so teams must spread them; the exact cap was not stated in what I read — [Wikipedia: The Amazing Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Amazing_Race)
- The same "choose from a brief clue, then reveal" rule is used in the Asia and Australia editions — [Wikipedia: The Amazing Race Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Amazing_Race_Asia); [Wikipedia: The Amazing Race Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Amazing_Race_Australia)
- Head-to-Head / Face Off (Latin America S2, Norway S2): teams compete 1-on-1 in a task; the winner advances, the loser waits for the next team to arrive — [Wikipedia: The Amazing Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Amazing_Race)
- Intersection (S10): two teams must pair up and do all tasks and decisions together until told otherwise — [Wikipedia: The Amazing Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Amazing_Race)
- The Israeli version (המירוץ למיליון) uses Roadblocks; the fan wiki records a "Roadblock Performer" per leg per team — [Amazing Race Wiki: Roni & Yael](https://amazingrace.fandom.com/wiki/Roni_%26_Yael); [Wikipedia: HaMerotz LaMillion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HaMerotz_LaMillion)
- Taskmaster's "secret teammate goal": players earned two bonus points for getting a teammate to say "submarine" without saying it themselves, and lost two points if the teammate suspected — a one-member-knows-something structure — [BuzzFeed: 50 greatest Taskmaster tasks (#7, S12)](https://www.buzzfeed.com/hopelasater/best-taskmaster-tasks-ranked)
- Taskmaster yes/no-only constraint: drawing a caricature of a person behind a curtain using only yes/no questions, bonus point for learning their full name — [BuzzFeed (#21, S4)](https://www.buzzfeed.com/hopelasater/best-taskmaster-tasks-ranked)
- Taskmaster S9 team task where sub-tasks are opened one at a time, each only after the previous is done, starting with a branch ("look in the toaster or go to the shed") — [BuzzFeed (#48, S9)](https://www.buzzfeed.com/hopelasater/best-taskmaster-tasks-ranked)
- Korean 1 Night 2 Days "bok-bul-bok" decides who eats and who sleeps outside, usually by luck; a March 2026 variant was a "prediction foot volleyball" match where members had to win AND hit the scores they each predicted in advance — [Sports Khan / 1 Night 2 Days coverage](https://sports.khan.co.kr/en/article/202603232004007); [TenAsia](https://www.tenasia.com/amp/2025031825414)

### Inferences
Representative catalogue for the race (each: example, why it films well, how the photographer verifies, tiered scoring):
1. **Blind Roadblock ("מי מכם...?")**. The app shows only a teaser, e.g. "Who of you has the best memory for faces?" The team names one person in the app, and only then does the full task unlock (e.g. "Memorize the vendor's 8 spice jars, then rebuild the order with your back turned"). Footage: the face of the picked person when the real task appears. Verify: the photographer films the pick (team says the name on camera) and then the attempt. Tiers: full / partial / failed, plus a cap of 2 Roadblocks per person over the night so 4-12-person teams rotate.
2. **"The person who chose does it", inverted**. The task says "whoever was picked now picks someone else, and THAT person does it" or "the one who volunteered sits out". Footage: the betrayal laugh. Verify: video of the hand-off. Tiers: as above.
3. **Auction for the hot seat**. Each member bids seconds (or points) for the right to represent; highest bid plays, and the bid is subtracted from the team's time bank. Mirrors Taskmaster's "buy tools with time" (see the twist section). Footage: the bidding argument. Verify: one shot of the bids on fingers. Tiers: base points minus the bid.
4. **Rep vs rep (Face Off)**. Two teams that arrive at a station together each send one person; a 60-second duel (vendor counts, vendor judges). Winner's team moves on with full points; loser's team waits a fixed 3 minutes or for the next team. Footage: two-camera duel, both photographers film. Verify: both photographers' clips, vendor's verdict stated on camera. Tiers: win / loss / no opponent arrived (solo attempt against a time).
5. **Team helps from outside, under a constraint**. Only the rep can touch/answer; the rest may help only with gestures, or one word each in a fixed order, or yes/no only (the Taskmaster S4 shape). Footage: frantic charades. Verify: continuous shot showing nobody spoke out of turn. Tiers: done with no rule breaks / done with 1-2 breaks (each break costs points) / failed.
6. **Secret mission for one member**. One member, chosen by the app at random, gets a private note: "Make your teammates say the vendor's shop name without saying it yourself. If they guess you have a mission, you lose the bonus." Footage: the reveal at the end ("you were playing us the whole time?"). Verify: the rep films a short selfie confession BEFORE starting (timestamped), the photographer films the moment. Tiers: bonus / no bonus / caught (small penalty).
7. **Prediction rep (bok-bul-bok shape)**. Before the task, each rep predicts their own score; points only if they win AND land within ±1 of the prediction. Footage: the gap between confidence and reality. Verify: prediction filmed before. Tiers: exact / ±1 / miss.
8. **Team "weakest link" vote** - use with care for teens: frame it as "vote who is the dark horse" (the person the team thinks will surprise everyone), never "the weakest". Avoids humiliation.

### Gaps
- I did not find a specific, sourced Running Man "choose your fighter" mechanic; the merchant episodes I found are auctions, not representative selection.
- No confirmed exact Roadblock cap per racer in current seasons.
- I found no source confirming whether a team can switch its Roadblock performer after reading the full task (the show's general understanding is no, but unverified here).
- No Israeli-specific source on representative mechanics beyond the fan-wiki performer column; the Hebrew on-screen term for Roadblock was not confirmed.

## Twist / catch mechanics

### Takeaway
The best catches share one structure: a rule that is true but withheld (Taskmaster secret addendum, "slowest wins", "third fastest wins"), a choice made under incomplete information (Roadblock, Split/Steal), or a rule that fires later (Taskmaster sleeper task, Jet Lag curses). All three produce a single reveal moment that a phone camera can capture, and all can be scored in tiers without a human judge beyond the photographer and the vendor.

### Cited Findings
**Taskmaster (hidden rules, inverted win conditions, delayed triggers)**
- A task brief said "fastest wins", then a secret addendum revealed in the studio turned it into "slowest wins" — [taskmaster.info task 2951](https://taskmaster.info/task.php?id=2951)
- "The third fastest high-fiver wins": first and last get zero, so racing to be first is wrong — [BuzzFeed (#27, S13)](https://www.buzzfeed.com/hopelasater/best-taskmaster-tasks-ranked)
- Slow bike ride: slowest wins; a foot down pauses the clock and halves the final time; leaving the course halves it too — [BuzzFeed (#26, S12)](https://www.buzzfeed.com/hopelasater/best-taskmaster-tasks-ranked); [Sporcle S12 task list](https://www.sporcle.com/games/SirDoris/taskmaster-task-winners-series-12)
- Pineapple concealment: "most incorrect guesses wins", inverting the normal goal — [BuzzFeed (#36, S2)](https://www.buzzfeed.com/hopelasater/best-taskmaster-tasks-ranked)
- Sleeper task: contestants had to put on a boiler suit and lie flat whenever a siren sounded during a FUTURE task (it went off twice later in the episode) — [BuzzFeed (#2, S7)](https://www.buzzfeed.com/hopelasater/best-taskmaster-tasks-ranked)
- Stealth task that only starts when Alex ducks behind a wall a minute after the brief is given — [BuzzFeed (#10, S8)](https://www.buzzfeed.com/hopelasater/best-taskmaster-tasks-ranked)
- "The time stops when you shout 'I'm invisible!'" — the player decides when their own time ends — [BuzzFeed (#37, S8)](https://www.buzzfeed.com/hopelasater/best-taskmaster-tasks-ranked)
- "You may buy the tools you need with time" — time as currency — [BuzzFeed (#15, S12)](https://www.buzzfeed.com/hopelasater/best-taskmaster-tasks-ranked)
- Indirect prize: your prize depends on the person below you alphabetically — [BuzzFeed (#34, S12)](https://www.buzzfeed.com/hopelasater/best-taskmaster-tasks-ranked)
- Blindfold retrace: go as far as possible, then retrace your steps; longest successful retrace wins — [BuzzFeed (#23, S5)](https://www.buzzfeed.com/hopelasater/best-taskmaster-tasks-ranked)
- Hidden-rules tower task: only the umpire knows the secret rules; when one is broken a penalty is given and only THEN is the rule announced — [taskmaster.info task 3502](https://taskmaster.info/task.php?id=3502)
- Series 16 had a secret task hidden in the house, hinted at for several episodes before being found under the Greg portrait; a three-person team got a rule ("say another contestant's name after 10 seconds") without being told the points or consequences — [AOL: Taskmaster series 16 unveils the secret task](https://www.aol.com/taskmaster-series-16-finally-unveils-000500546.html)
- Series 22: contestants had to convince an audience whether they were or were not getting secret earpiece instructions — [AV Club exclusive clip](https://www.avclub.com/taskmaster-season-22-exclusive-clip-isy-suttie-chloe-petts)

**Amazing Race (sabotage between teams, forced choices)**
- U-Turn: forces a designated team to complete both Detour tasks; variants Blind, Double, Automatic, U-Turn Vote — [Wikipedia: The Amazing Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Amazing_Race)
- Yield: one team forces another to stop racing until an hourglass drains — [Wikipedia: The Amazing Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Amazing_Race)
- Speed Bump: an extra task the last-place team must do in the next leg — [Wikipedia: The Amazing Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Amazing_Race)
- Fast Forward: an optional task; the first team to finish it skips the rest of the leg — [Wikipedia: The Amazing Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Amazing_Race)
- Express Pass: skip one task of your choice before a specified leg — [Wikipedia: The Amazing Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Amazing_Race)
- Detour: choose between two tasks testing different skills; teams may switch at any time — [Wikipedia: The Amazing Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Amazing_Race)

**Jet Lag: The Game curses (delayed / movement constraints)** — community wiki text, not official card text
- Curse of the Unguided Tourist (Japan): for the next hour seekers may only turn right at any intersection — [Jet Lag wiki: Japan curses](https://jetlag.fandom.com/wiki/Hide_%2B_Seek:_Japan/Curses)
- Curse of the Lemon Phylactery: before asking another question, each seeker must find a lemon and attach it to their outer clothing — [Jet Lag wiki: Japan curses](https://jetlag.fandom.com/wiki/Hide_%2B_Seek:_Japan/Curses)
- Curse of the Distant Cuisine: seekers must visit a restaurant serving food from a country at least as far away before asking another question — [Jet Lag wiki: Japan curses](https://jetlag.fandom.com/wiki/Hide_%2B_Seek:_Japan/Curses)
- Curse of the Gambler's Feet: for 20/40/60 minutes seekers roll a die before moving and may take only that many steps before rolling again — [Jet Lag wiki: NYC curses](https://jetlag.fandom.com/wiki/Hide_%2B_Seek_(NYC)/Curses)
- Curse of the Egg Partner: seekers must acquire an egg, which becomes an official team member — [Jet Lag wiki: NYC curses](https://jetlag.fandom.com/wiki/Hide_%2B_Seek_(NYC)/Curses)
- Curse of the Impressionable Consumer: seekers must enter a place or buy a product they saw advertised before the next question — [Jet Lag wiki: NYC curses](https://jetlag.fandom.com/wiki/Hide_%2B_Seek_(NYC)/Curses)
- Curse of the Gilded Inquiry (UK): the hider secretly picks one question; if the seekers ask it, it is vetoed and the hider draws three cards — [Jet Lag wiki: UK curses](https://jetlag.fandom.com/wiki/Hide_%2B_Seek:_UK/Curses)
- Curse of Hide-and-Seek-Ception (UK): all seekers but one close their eyes; the remaining one goes at least 500 ft away out of sight — [Jet Lag wiki: UK curses](https://jetlag.fandom.com/wiki/Hide_%2B_Seek:_UK/Curses)
- Curses carry casting costs (e.g. discard your hand) in some seasons; the official expansion PDF clarifies curse wording — [Jet Lag wiki: NYC curses](https://jetlag.fandom.com/wiki/Hide_%2B_Seek_(NYC)/Curses); [Official expansion rules PDF](https://jetlagthegame.com/JLTG-Home-Game-Expansion-1-Rules.pdf)

**Golden Balls Split or Steal (prisoner's dilemma between rival players)**
- Both Split: each gets half. One Steal, one Split: Steal takes all. Both Steal: nobody gets anything. Players may talk before choosing — [Wikipedia: Golden Balls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Balls)
- Unlike a strict prisoner's dilemma, Steal vs Split and Steal vs Steal pay the Split player the same (zero), so it is only a weak Nash equilibrium — [Puget Sound econ blog](https://blogs.pugetsound.edu/econ/?p=3678); [Cornell INFO 2040 blog](https://blogs.cornell.edu/info2040/2014/09/14/gaming-the-system-a-prisoners-dilemma-in-the-game-show-golden-balls)
- Players split about 53% of the time (study including Richard Thaler); explicit promises ("I will split") predicted cooperation better than conditional ones ("I will split if you split") — [Wikipedia: Golden Balls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Balls)
- Famous final: Nick announced he would Steal and promised to share afterwards, with a £13,600 jackpot (the Ibrahim episode) — [Radiolab "Golden Rule" transcript](https://radiolab.org/podcast/golden-rule-2304/transcript)

**The Traitors (imposter mechanics)**
- Traitors are secretly chosen; each day players debate at the Round Table and vote in secret; the banished player reveals their affiliation; ties go to a run-off — [Wikipedia: The Traitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Traitors)
- A shield, won secretly in missions, protects from "murder" but not banishment — [Peacock: The Traitors game rules](https://www.peacocktv.com/blog/the-traitors-game-rules); [One Night of The Traitors rules](https://www.drumondpark.co.uk/rules/onenightofthetraitors)

### Inferences
Twist catalogue for the race (example → footage → photographer verification → tiers):
1. **Secret addendum ("the real task was X")**. Station card: "Build the tallest tower of vendor cups in 2 minutes." After submission, the app reveals: "The vendor was counting how many times you said thank you." Footage: the reveal reaction, filmed by the photographer reading it out. Verify: the full attempt clip is needed anyway, so the hidden criterion is countable afterwards. Tiers: 3+ / 1-2 / 0. Keep the hidden criterion something POSITIVE (politeness, teamwork, everyone participating) so the reveal is a laugh, not a shaming.
2. **Inverted win condition**. "Slowest wins": walk the length of a market alley as slowly as possible without ever stopping; a stop halves your time (Taskmaster S12 shape). Or "third fastest wins" across the whole race at one station (rank shown at the end of the night). Verify: one continuous take with a visible start and end. Tiers by time bands.
3. **Wording loophole**. Write tasks so a literal reading is legal and clever: "Bring back something red from this street" (a photo of something red counts if "bring back" is not defined). Announce in the briefing that loopholes are legal. Footage: the "wait, can we...?" moment. Verify: photographer films the loophole being stated out loud. Tiers: done straight / done via loophole (bonus) / not done.
4. **Delayed trigger (sleeper rule)**. Given at station 2: "From now until the end, whenever you hear the word 'Zion' from a vendor, the whole team freezes for 5 seconds." Later a vendor says it. Footage: the group freeze in a street. Verify: the vendor tells the photographer the word is coming. Tiers: everyone froze / some / nobody.
5. **Curses (Jet Lag) as a power a leading or trailing team can cast**. Teen-safe city versions: "only turn right for the next 10 minutes" (only where the route is safe; define a geofence); "carry a lemon/pita as a team member until the next station"; "speak only in questions until the next station". Footage: the curse arriving on the phone. Verify: photographer films each moment of compliance at the next station. Tiers: kept / broke once / broke repeatedly.
6. **Bet before you see it (double or nothing)**. Before unlocking the task, the team stakes 0, 50% or 100% of its points value. Win = stake added, lose = stake deducted. Footage: the debate before the bet. Verify: the stake is locked in the app before reveal (no human needed). Tiers: built in.
7. **Split or Steal between two rival teams at a joint station**. Each team's rep secretly chooses on their own phone; vendor counts to three, reps show. Both split = both +X; one steals = stealer +2X; both steal = both 0. Footage: the reveal is the most-watched shape in game TV. Verify: both photographers film both phones simultaneously. Note: the "Split/Steal = same zero for the betrayed" weakness means cooperation is fragile; to make it more interesting, give a small consolation to a betrayed splitter.
8. **Prisoner's dilemma without a meeting**. Two teams who never meet each get the same choice at the same station during the night (results revealed at the finale). Footage: finale reveal on the big screen.
9. **Sabotage cards (U-Turn / Yield / Speed Bump)**. The first team to reach a station may "Yield" another team (3 minutes waiting at its next station) or "U-Turn" (must do both options of the next choice). Make it public who did it, which creates rivalry footage. Keep the penalty a wait or an extra harmless task, never something that separates a teen from the group.
10. **Imposter (Traitors) inside a team**. For teams of 6+: at the start one member gets a secret "saboteur" role, whose goal is to make the team fail exactly one mission without being caught. At the finale each team votes. Footage: the unmasking. Verify: the saboteur records a private confession video to HQ before the race. Tiers: team catches them (team bonus) / saboteur survives (saboteur's personal prize). Caution: needs a clear end-of-night reveal so nobody leaves feeling accused; skip for teams that do not know each other.
11. **Information asymmetry**. Only one member gets the station's map or code; they may describe it but not show it.
12. **Trust test**. "The vendor will give your rep a sealed envelope. If the team arrives at the finish with it unopened, +X. Open it and you get the content (a shortcut hint) but lose X." Footage: the temptation. Verify: the envelope is shown sealed at the finish.

### Gaps
- No complete official list of Jet Lag curses; wiki text is community-maintained and varies by season.
- No source quantifying which twist types produce the most-watched footage; the "reveal moment" claim is my inference from show structure.
- The full Taskmaster wiki secret-task index was not fetched; examples come from the BuzzFeed list and taskmaster.info pages.

## Missions where a stranger or vendor is the game master

### Takeaway
Real formats that put ordinary people in the game master seat exist (Japanese "Old Enough!", Running Man merchant episodes), but the documented examples treat the locals as briefed helpers or buyers, not as secret judges. For the race, the vendor works best as a briefed co-host who holds a hidden criterion, a code word, or the final verdict.

### Cited Findings
- Old Enough! (Hajimete no Otsukai) is a Nippon TV show since 1991 showing toddlers running errands alone, followed by a camera crew; episodes are 10-20 minutes — [Wikipedia: Old Enough!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Enough!)
- One account (quoting the Guardian) says routes are inspected by parents and staff, the crew has hiding places, and local neighbours are informed of the task; a forum commenter disputes that the crew is hidden. Contested — [JREF forum thread](https://jref.com/threads/hajimete-no-otsukai.396429/); [ParentMap](https://www.parentmap.com/article/japanese-tv-show-old-enough-netflix-whats-fuss-about)
- Running Man "Running Merchants": two teams buy items and auction them to the show's staff for the biggest profit, playing street vendors in a marketplace — [SBS Star](https://sbsstar.net/article/N1008068581/running-merchants-returns-running-man-cast-faces-off-in-hilarious-sales-showdown)
- Running Man Ep. 749 "Merchant Chronicles: Earn or Perish" (2024): members buy items from staff and auction them — [Wikipedia: List of Running Man episodes (2024)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Running_Man_episodes_(2024))
- Running Man began as a race format with missions at landmarks before moving to a game-based variety format — [Wikipedia: Running Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Running_Man_(TV_program))

### Inferences
Vendor-as-game-master patterns for Jerusalem shops (vendor is briefed, gets a one-line card, and a code to give):
1. **Vendor secretly judges something else** (twist #1 above): the visible task is a haggle or a quiz; the vendor scores politeness, or whether every team member spoke, or whether they asked the vendor's name. The vendor tells the photographer the score on camera at the end.
2. **Vendor withholds the code until a condition is met that is not stated**: e.g. the vendor hands over the code only after someone asks "how is your evening?". Teams must figure out the "password" of humanity. Cap the time (5 minutes) then the vendor gives a hint.
3. **Running Man market shape, tiny scale**: each team gets 10 shekels-equivalent in play tokens; buy one item from the vendor list and "sell" it to the next station's vendor with a 20-second pitch; vendor rates the pitch 1-3. No real money changes hands.
4. **Vendor duel referee**: rep vs rep (representative #4) with the vendor judging and counting down.
5. **Vendor as the "Yield" holder**: a team that has been Yielded must wait at this vendor until the vendor finishes a short story / teaches them a word.
6. **Old Enough shape**: the youngest member (or a randomly chosen one) does a small errand alone at a shop 20 meters away while the team watches from a marked spot, everyone in sight, the photographer filming from the team. Safety: inside the group's line of sight only, no crossing roads, vendor briefed.
Constraints applied throughout: the vendor is always briefed and consenting; no task asks teens to embarrass a passer-by; no physical contact between opposite-gender participants or with vendors; no running in traffic; night routes must be lit and geofenced.

### Gaps
- No source confirms that Old Enough shopkeepers are briefed; this is contested.
- No Israeli TV examples of vendors acting as secret judges were found.
- No Korean variety source found where merchants secretly judge the cast; the documented merchant format is buying/auctioning.

## Verification by the team photographer and tiered scoring (cross-cutting)

### Takeaway
Every mechanic above can be verified by one rule: the photographer films the DECISION moment (who was chosen, what was bet, which ball) before the reveal, and one continuous take of the attempt. Show formats rely on cameras for the same reason; tiered scores come from countable things in that clip.

### Cited Findings
- Amazing Race enforces task rules with fixed time penalties for quitting (4 hours for a Roadblock, 6 hours for a Detour, previously 24) instead of point judgments — [Wikipedia: The Amazing Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Amazing_Race)
- Taskmaster's hidden-rule task reveals the broken rule only after a penalty is given, i.e. scoring is based on observed rule breaks — [taskmaster.info task 3502](https://taskmaster.info/task.php?id=3502)

### Inferences
- Standard verification checklist: (1) a pre-reveal shot of the choice/bet; (2) a continuous take of the attempt with start and end visible; (3) the vendor states the verdict to the camera; (4) for secret roles, a timestamped private video sent to HQ before the mission.
- Standard tiers: Full (all conditions, no rule breaks) / Partial (completed with 1-2 breaks or half the target) / Attempted (filmed but failed) / Not filmed = 0. Bets and Split/Steal have their payoff built in.
- Use time penalties (wait 2-3 minutes at a station) rather than point deductions for sabotage, mirroring Yield; it is easier to enforce at night and less punitive for teens.

### Gaps
- No source on how real shows verify tasks when no host is present; this whole section is design inference except the penalty structure.
