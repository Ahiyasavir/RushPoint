# Writing mission cards in spoken Israeli teen Hebrew (ages 15-18)

Scope: how to write short mission cards (2-4 lines) that Israeli teenagers read as natural and cool rather than formal or childish. Covers grammar, how youth-facing Hebrew sounds, slang, tone and brevity, and ends with 20 before/after rewrites.

A note on evidence: Hebrew-language web sources on current (2025-2026) teen slang are thin. Most of what turned up is general learner guides, opinion columns or anecdotes. Anything below that is not tied to a source is marked as inference. The before/after rewrites are craft recommendations, not sourced facts.

## 1. Grammar: future-as-imperative ("תצלמו") vs. formal imperative ("צלמו"), and plural address

### Takeaway
Using the future form as a command ("תצלמו", "תביאו", "תקליטו") is the normal informal register of Modern Hebrew. Linguists describe it as established usage, not an error. The Academy says its rulings bind only formal writing and speech. "צלמו/הקליטו" reads as written, official or school-like. For a teen game card, the future form is the correct register choice. Address the team in the plural.

### Cited Findings
- Chanan Ariel (Tel Aviv University, 2019) describes the use of the future form to express commands in Modern Hebrew as confined to the informal register. He argues it is a fairly recent development, rooted in Rabbinic Hebrew of the 18th-19th centuries ("insubordination" and system simplification), not inherited from Biblical Hebrew. — [Benjamins, Linguistik Aktuell 256](https://www.benjamins.com/catalog/la.256.05ari); [TAU CRIS](https://cris.tau.ac.il/en/publications/why-did-the-future-form-of-the-verb-displace-the-imperative-form-/)
- David L. Gold: in non-formal Israeli Hebrew the imperative forms simply are the future forms (his example: "tachzor!"). The deciding factor is how formal the utterance is, not whether it is spoken or written. Ran Ari-Gur partly disagrees, saying true imperatives are much more common in writing. — [LanguageHat discussion](https://languagehat.com/israeli-hebrew-imperatives/)
- Glinert (quoted there): casual Hebrew "mostly uses the future form" for positive requests. Short true imperatives survive mainly for qal/pi'el verbs that form them by dropping the prefix. He calls these "fairly elegant in style" but "slightly brusque in meaning." Gold names "שב" and "בוא" as everyday exceptions. — [LanguageHat](https://languagehat.com/israeli-hebrew-imperatives/)
- Negative commands have always used the future: "אל תשב", never a negative imperative form. This may have helped the future spread into positive commands. Hif'il verbs (הקליטו, הביאו) never get the clipped spoken form. — [LanguageHat](https://languagehat.com/israeli-hebrew-imperatives/)
- The future-as-imperative also appears in the lowest written registers, such as text messages. The infinitive ("לשבת", "לצלם") is used on signs and written instructions, and to create social distance. — [LanguageHat](https://languagehat.com/israeli-hebrew-imperatives/)
- The Academy of the Hebrew Language says it does not police spontaneous speech. It treats its decisions as binding only for written texts and formal speeches. — [Academy, Mission and Vision (EN)](https://en.hebrew-academy.org.il/?p=240)
- In general linguistic description, the imperative is a command form derived from the future, not a separate tense. — [Hamichlol, זמן (בלשנות)](https://www.hamichlol.org.il/%D7%96%D7%9E%D7%9F_(%D7%91%D7%9C%D7%A9%D7%A0%D7%95%D7%AA))
- Ruvik Rosenthal (רוביק רוזנטל), the linguist, on "היה שלום לכם" in a pop song: grammatically it does not fit a plural audience, but he sees it as a quoted formula ("like 'thanks', like 'hello'") and a harmless bit of wordplay. In other words, informal grammar in pop/youth text is not treated as a problem by language commentators. — [mako](https://www.mako.co.il/good-news-news/Article-7790c6dbf73f061006.htm)

### Inferences
- **Default to the plural future: "תצלמו, תביאו, תרוצו, תמצאו."** It is what a friend or counselor says out loud. "צלמו / הביאו / הקליטו" sounds like a school worksheet, a museum sign or a government notice. The infinitive ("לצלם את...") sounds like the most bureaucratic option.
- **Hif'il verbs are where the formal register gives itself away most** ("הקליטו", "הביאו", "הגיעו", "החזירו", "הראו"). Their future forms ("תקליטו", "תביאו", "תגיעו", "תחזירו", "תראו לנו") are the clearest gain. Short qal/pi'el imperatives that are still in casual use ("רוצו!", "שבו", "בואו", "קחו", "תנו") can stay. "יאללה רוצו" sounds fine, but "הקליטו" in a teen card sounds stiff.
- **"תקליטו" is standard spoken Hebrew.** Writing it is a register choice, the way "gonna" is in English dialogue. No source found calls it an error in informal text, and the Academy explicitly does not rule on informal language.
- **Plural, not masculine singular.** The team is a group. This also matches the project's existing Hebrew voice rule (CLAUDE.md, "hebrew-one-voice": sentences of four words or more use "אתם" or impersonal forms, never one man). "אתם / שלכם / תעשו" covers mixed-gender teams without "תצלם/י" slashes, which read as a form.
- Negatives stay in the future, which is natural anyway: "אל תגלו לאף אחד", "אל תשכחו".
- **Watch the clash between register and brusqueness.** Glinert's "slightly brusque" point means a flat future command can sound bossy ("תביאו את זה"). Teen-friendly cards soften it through playfulness and a reason or a dare, not through "בבקשה" (which, per Y on LanguageHat, can actually read as impatient or ironic).

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve a specific Academy Q&A page ruling on "תכתוב" vs "כתוב" or "תקליטו" vs "הקליטו". Academy search results only gave general material. The claim "it is standard spoken usage" rests on Ariel, Gold and Glinert, not on an Academy ruling.
- Ariel's full chapter is paywalled. Only the abstract was available.

## 2. How Israeli youth brands, youth movements, creators and game venues write to teens

### Takeaway
I found almost no retrievable primary examples of youth movement posts, teen campaigns or Israeli TikTok captions. Search engines do not index them well, and social platforms are not fetchable. The one domain with usable text was escape rooms. Their copy uses plural "אתם", second-person future/imperative, a clock, a threat and "בהצלחה". That is the closest template to a mission card.

### Cited Findings
- Escape-room briefing style, from Israeli venue copy: "יש לכם 60 דקות בלבד לברוח... אבל צרות גדולות יותר מחכות לכם בדרך". Pattern: plural you, hard deadline, an ominous twist. — [tiuli.com, MISTORY](https://www.tiuli.com/attractions/1703/mistory-%D7%97%D7%93%D7%A8%D7%99-%D7%91%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%97%D7%94-%D7%95%D7%A7%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%95%D7%A7%D7%99)
- Escape-room tip copy in the formal-plural imperative ("היו בבקרה על הזמן", "השתמשו בכל הרמזים בחדר"). This is marketing/advice register, noticeably more formal than a briefing. — [bizportal](https://www.bizportal.co.il/advertismentandmarketing/news/article/764244)
- Rosenthal's "הזירה הלשונית" column records youth coinages such as "לרבקו" (to ride on a friend's Rav-Kav card). It notes that "אחי" spread from comedy and advertising, alongside "כפרה" and "מאמי". — [Makor Rishon, הזירה הלשונית](https://www.makorrishon.co.il/nrg/online/47/ART2/863/016.html)
- A mako parenting article cites "שחנ"שים" (שיחות נפש) as youth slang. — [mako](https://www.mako.co.il/news-tip12/body_and_spirit/Article-36ba93e41f53281027.htm)

### Inferences
- The escape-room briefing formula maps directly onto a mission card: **[what you face] + [what you must do, future plural] + [the clock or the catch] + [a taunt]**.
- Venue *marketing* copy uses formal imperatives ("השתמשו", "היו"), while the *in-game* voice uses "יש לכם... תצטרכו...". That suggests the register split runs along "talking about the game" vs. "talking inside the game". Mission cards are inside the game.

### Gaps
- No retrievable posts from Bnei Akiva, Tzofim, HaNoar HaOved or similar. No Israeli teen-campaign case studies. No Israeli YouTube challenge titles (for example "24 שעות ב...", "האחרון ש... זוכה", which are common in the genre from general knowledge but not verified here). Someone should collect 20-30 real lines by hand from Instagram/TikTok accounts of youth movements and Israeli challenge creators before the event. This is the biggest gap in these notes.

## 3. Slang: what reads natural from an organizer, what is dated or trying too hard, and English loanwords

### Takeaway
Sources confirm that cringe, וייב, פלקס and טוקסיק are now mainstream in Hebrew talk. They also confirm that slang ages fast and passes down to ever younger kids, and that a teen will call adults using kid slang "קרינג'". For an adult organizer, the safe zone is a few broad, long-lived words used lightly ("יאללה", "סבבה", "תכלס", "אחלה", "קטע", "בול"). Trend words (skibidi, sigma, rizz, Ohio) and very dated ones (מגניב said seriously, "חבל"ז") should be avoided.

### Cited Findings
- Israel Hayom: "קרינג'" (embarrassing), "וייב" (atmosphere), "פלקס" (bragging) and "טוקסיק" (toxic) have taken over everyday talk, and "לייק" has replaced "כאילו". Each generation of young people makes new slang, and the old slang moves down to an even younger generation. — [Israel Hayom via search summary](https://www.israelhayom.co.il/viralai/article/18104893) (page itself returned 403. Claim taken from the search excerpt.)
- The Academy of the Hebrew Language asked the public for a Hebrew word for "cringe". Proposals such as "זוונוג" and "מביכוש" were considered amusing, and no single replacement stuck. — [Israel Hayom via search summary](https://www.israelhayom.co.il/culture/books/article/6022332) (403 on fetch)
- TheMarker (July 2024) slang guide: סקיבידי, ריז, סיגמה, בטא, סאס, אוהיו, גיאט. A 13-year-old interviewed says some kids use them as sentence filler, like adults' "כזה כאילו", that meanings vary by social circle even between Tel Aviv and Givatayim, and warns adults off them: "זה קרינג'". An 8-year-old says classmates say skibidi and sigma without knowing what they mean. "כפרה" is described as used by everyone as an endearment. — [TheMarker, Captain Internet](https://www.themarker.com/captain-internet/2024-07-10/ty-article/00000190-97f8-d9cc-a1b2-fffd968e0000)
- Hebrew Wikipedia lists current Hebrew slang: "בלתי" (unbearable), "גזור" (positive, someone doing crazy stunts), "דיבור"/"יש דיבור" (agreement, something of interest), "זורם", "חצי קלאץ'" (an old phrase back in use), "מקריפ" (creepy), "מת" (dying of laughter), "תסביך", "שבור/ה". Also "קרינג'", "קרינג'י", "להקרינג'". — [Hebrew Wikipedia, ביטויי סלנג של דור ה-Z ודור האלפא](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%91%D7%99%D7%98%D7%95%D7%99%D7%99_%D7%A1%D7%9C%D7%A0%D7%92_%D7%A9%D7%9C_%D7%93%D7%95%D7%A8_%D7%94-Z)
- "מגניב" with final stress (the general praise word) is worn out and marks the speaker as out of touch. The penultimate-stress "מגניב", used about a stylish person, is the young form. Teens make the distinction by ear. — [דגש קל blog, 2022](https://dagesh.wordpress.com/2022/05/01/%D7%A0%D7%95%D7%A2%D7%A8-%D7%9E%D7%93%D7%91%D7%A8-%D7%9E%D7%92%D7%A0%D7%99%D7%91/)
- Positive "war" slang used by Israeli kids with friends: "פיצוץ", "הורס", "פגז", "אש", "טיל" (all compliments). Article circa Aug 2022, date inferred from image paths. — [ynet, מילון לילדי העולים](https://www.ynet.co.il/judaism/article/sk00yeeiko)
- "סבבה" is described as an all-purpose "cool/okay", used mostly by younger speakers. "יאללה" is used constantly to hurry friends along. "אחי" is a friendly address to almost anyone. "סוף הדרך" and "אחלה" mean "great". These are learner guides, not teen surveys. — [USC Folklore Archive, סבבה](https://folklore.usc.edu/hebrew-slang-%D7%A1%D7%B7%EF%AC%B1%D7%B7%EF%AC%B1%D7%B8%D7%97-sababa/); [Lingopie](https://lingopie.com/blog/hebrew-slang/); [Rosen Hebrew School](https://rosenhebrewschool.com/blog/hebrew-slang-for-hanging-out-in-israel)
- A 1990s youth phrase ("חבלז מזמז שוכו קר") and "כזה כאילו" are cited as earlier generations' slang. — [TheMarker](https://www.themarker.com/captain-internet/2024-07-10/ty-article/00000190-97f8-d9cc-a1b2-fffd968e0000)

### Inferences (recommended word lists)

These classifications are my judgment, built on the sources above (slang ages fast, adults using trend slang is "cringe", broad words last). None of them come from a survey.

**Use freely (broad, long-lived, an adult can say them without sounding fake):**
- יאללה (opener or closer: "יאללה, זזים")
- תכלס (= "bottom line": "תכלס, יש לכם 5 דקות")
- סבבה / אחלה (sparingly, in the reward line: "צילמתם? אחלה. הבא בתור")
- בול (= exactly: "בול 30 שניות")
- קטע (= "the thing is / a funny thing": "הקטע הוא שאסור לדבר")
- וואלה (works as a surprise beat: "וואלה, לא חשבנו שתגיעו עד לפה")
- על הזמן / לפני הזמן; "שעון רץ"
- "אין מצב ש..." / "בלי צ'יטים"
- loanwords that are now ordinary Hebrew: וייב, קרינג', פלקס, צ'אלנג', סטורי, לייב, באג, ריל (as "reel").

**Use once, with intent (good in a taunt or a reward line, tiring if repeated):**
- אחי / אחותי (fine when one player is addressed, as in "מי שמחזיק את הטלפון, אחי, אל תפיל". Avoid it as the default address to a mixed team)
- חבל על הזמן, פצצה, אש, פיצוץ, הורס (praise words. Keep them for the "well done" screen, not the instruction)
- לא נורמלי ("משימה לא נורמלית")
- שוס (works, but carries a slightly older, 2000s feel. Inference, not verified)
- "יש דיבור" (as a reply to success, per Wikipedia's current-slang list)

**Avoid (trying too hard, dated, or risky):**
- Meme-trend words: סקיבידי, סיגמה, ריז, אוהיו, גיאט. Per TheMarker, even a 13-year-old calls their use "קרינג'", and they skew to younger kids.
- Dated praise: "מגניב" said seriously (per דגש קל), "אחלה בחלה", "חבל"ז", "סחתיין" (reads like a grandfather), "כפיים!", "יופי טופי".
- Childish/camp-counselor register: "ילדים", "חברים יקרים", "כל הכבוד לכם!!!", "בואו נראה מי הכי מהר", diminutives ("משימונת"), stacked exclamation marks.
- Bureaucratic markers: "יש ל...", "על המשתתפים ל...", "נא ל...", "הנכם מתבקשים", "יש להקפיד", infinitive commands ("לצלם את...").
- Slurs or slur-adjacent slang, even "ironically" (for example "פרחה", which a source describes as a teen term targeting Mizrahi girls). — [Hadassah Magazine](https://www.hadassahmagazine.org/?p=7056)
- Gendered slashes ("צלם/י"), masculine singular to the whole team.

### Gaps
- No 2025-2026 Hebrew corpus or survey of what 15-18-year-olds actually say. The Israel Hayom pieces were 403, so their dates and exact wording are unverified. Whether "שוס", "פצצה" or "לא נורמלי" feel dated to 2026 teens is unverified. The quickest fix is to read the draft cards aloud to 2-3 real 16-year-olds before printing.
- Regional/sector differences (religious-Zionist vs. secular Tel Aviv vs. Jerusalem Haredi-adjacent youth) were not researched. TheMarker's informant says meanings vary even between neighboring cities. For a Jerusalem event with possibly mixed or religious participants, avoid any slang with sexual connotations.

## 4. Tone: playful taunting, brevity, emoji, and the before/after rewrites

### Takeaway
Write like a confident game master who is slightly against the players: short lines, a concrete action first, a clock or a catch, one taunt line at most. No emoji, which fits both the product rule and a clean look. Sources only give partial support for the taunt-and-clock voice (escape-room copy). The rest is craft judgment.

### Cited Findings
- The escape-room voice combines a deadline with a dare ("יש לכם 60 דקות בלבד... צרות גדולות יותר מחכות לכם"). — [tiuli.com](https://www.tiuli.com/attractions/1703/mistory-%D7%97%D7%93%D7%A8%D7%99-%D7%91%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%97%D7%94-%D7%95%D7%A7%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%95%D7%A7%D7%99)
- Flat future-form commands can sound managerial or curt, and adding "בבקשה" can add impatience or irony instead of politeness. — [LanguageHat](https://languagehat.com/israeli-hebrew-imperatives/)
- Product constraint: RushPoint's UI has no stock emoji (project rule, `scripts/test-no-stock-emoji.ts`), and Hebrew sentences address the reader in the plural (CLAUDE.md "hebrew-one-voice").
- I found no source on emoji habits in Israeli teen writing.

### Inferences: style rules for a 2-4 line card
1. **Line 1 = the action, a future-plural verb near the start.** "תמצאו", "תצלמו", "תשכנעו". Not "המשימה שלכם היא ל...".
2. **One number per card.** Time, count or distance ("30 שניות", "3 זרים", "50 מטר"). A number is the strongest concrete hook.
3. **One catch.** "בלי לדבר", "רק ביד שמאל", "אף אחד לא יכול לזוז".
4. **At most one taunt or flavor line, last.** "בהצלחה. תצטרכו אותה." / "קבוצה אחרת עשתה את זה ב־40 שניות. סתם. אולי." / "אם זה לא מביך, עשיתם לא נכון."
5. **No "בבקשה", no "נא", no "יש ל".** Teens read them as adults talking down.
6. **No exclamation-mark stacks.** One "!" per card at most. Dry beats loud.
7. **Talk to them as capable.** No "זהירות ילדים", no "תזכרו להיות נחמדים". Safety lines, when needed, are dry and specific ("לא חוצים כביש באדום. לא שווה את זה.").
8. **No emoji.** Use typography and a dry voice for energy.
9. **Do not explain the joke or the rules twice.** If a rule needs a second sentence, the card is too long.
10. **Read it aloud.** If a 17-year-old would not say it to a friend, rewrite it (inference based on the register research in section 1).

### Inferences: 20 before/after rewrites (formal or childish → teen register)

1. Before: "על הקבוצה לצלם תמונה משותפת ליד המזרקה."
   After: "תצלמו סלפי של כל הקבוצה עם המזרקה. כולם בפריים. כן, גם מי שמחזיק את הטלפון."
2. Before: "הקליטו סרטון קצר שבו אתם שרים שיר."
   After: "תקליטו 15 שניות של שירה בקול. לא משנה איזה שיר. משנה כמה חזק."
3. Before: "יש לאסוף חמישה עלים בצבעים שונים ולהביאם לנקודת הבדיקה."
   After: "תביאו 5 עלים, 5 צבעים שונים. ירוק אחד בלבד. תכלס, זה הקל."
4. Before: "ילדים, בואו נראה מי הכי מהיר! רוצו עד לעץ וחזרו!!!"
   After: "עד העץ וחזרה. כל הקבוצה. השעון כבר רץ."
5. Before: "נא לפנות לעובר אורח ולבקש ממנו לומר מילה בשפה זרה."
   After: "תשכנעו זר להגיד לכם משפט בשפה שאתם לא מבינים. תקליטו. בהצלחה, תצטרכו אותה."
6. Before: "המשימה שלכם היא לבנות פירמידה אנושית."
   After: "פירמידה אנושית. 3 קומות. תחזיקו 5 שניות ותצלמו לפני שזה מתפרק."
7. Before: "כל הכבוד! הצלחתם במשימה! אתם מדהימים!"
   After: "יש. עברתם. הבאה פחות נחמדה."
8. Before: "יש לפתור את החידה על מנת לקבל את המיקום הבא."
   After: "תפתרו את זה ותקבלו את הנקודה הבאה. לא תפתרו? תמשיכו לעמוד פה."
9. Before: "הקפידו לשמור על בטיחות בעת חציית כבישים."
   After: "לא חוצים באדום. שום ניקוד לא שווה את זה."
10. Before: "צרו סרטון ריקוד יצירתי באורך של עד 30 שניות."
    After: "ריקוד של 30 שניות, כולם ביחד, באמצע הרחוב. אם זה לא קצת קרינג', זה לא נחשב."
11. Before: "מצאו פריט בצבע אדום והצטלמו איתו."
    After: "תמצאו משהו אדום, גדול ממכם. תצטלמו איתו. אופניים לא נחשבים."
12. Before: "על כל חברי הקבוצה לשתוק במהלך המשימה."
    After: "מעכשיו אף אחד לא מדבר. אחד מדבר? מתחילים מההתחלה."
13. Before: "בבקשה הגיעו לנקודת המפגש בזמן."
    After: "תגיעו לנקודה תוך 10 דקות. מי שמאחר רואה את כולם עוברים אותו."
14. Before: "חברים יקרים, לפניכם משימה מיוחדת ומהנה!"
    After: "המשימה הזאת? קבוצה אחת כבר ויתרה עליה. סתם. אולי."
15. Before: "יש לשאול חמישה אנשים מה המאכל האהוב עליהם ולרשום את התשובות."
    After: "5 זרים, שאלה אחת: מה האוכל הכי טוב בירושלים? תרשמו. אסור לקבל אותה תשובה פעמיים."
16. Before: "צלמו את הקבוצה בתנוחה מצחיקה."
    After: "תמונה אחת, כולם בתנוחה הכי מביכה שאתם מוכנים לשלוח לקבוצת הכיתה."
17. Before: "אנא החזירו את הציוד לנקודת ההתחלה בסיום."
    After: "גמרתם? תחזירו את הציוד. בלי זה, אין ניקוד. בול ככה."
18. Before: "הצליחו לזרוק את הכדור לסל שלוש פעמים ברציפות."
    After: "3 סלים ברצף. החטאה אחת? מאפסים. יאללה."
19. Before: "בדקו את השלט שמעל הדלת וכתבו את המספר המופיע עליו."
    After: "יש מספר מעל הדלת. תכתבו אותו פה. שימו לב, יש שם שניים."
20. Before: "מומלץ להתחלק לתפקידים לפני תחילת המשימה."
    After: "תחליטו מי מצלם, מי רץ ומי מדבר. יש לכם 20 שניות לריב על זה."

What changed, across all of them: infinitives and "יש ל/נא/בבקשה" became future-plural verbs; the number moved to the front; "כל הכבוד!!!" became dry understatement; each card got exactly one catch and at most one taunt; gender slashes and "ילדים/חברים יקרים" were dropped.

### Gaps
- No source data on emoji or punctuation habits in Israeli teen writing, or on how teens rate "taunting" copy vs. encouraging copy. The rewrites have not been tested with real 15-18-year-olds. A 10-minute read-aloud with 2-3 teens is the recommended check before the 22.10 event.
- The geresh/maqaf in "ב־40" and "קרינג'" should follow the project's existing marketing typography rules (the founder dislikes hyphens in marketing visuals). Check that before printing.
