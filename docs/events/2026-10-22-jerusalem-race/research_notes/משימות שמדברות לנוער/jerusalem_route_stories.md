# Night race route, central Jerusalem: true stories and physical details for teen missions

Scope note: the event runs 19:00 to 22:15 on a Thursday. Every item below says what can be seen from the street at night. Terror attacks and deaths are left out as game material on purpose. The 1975 Zion Square bombing is NOT used, and neither are the Barazani/Feinstein deaths at the Russian Compound. The Mamilla Pool sits inside a historic Muslim cemetery, so it is flagged as unsuitable for playful missions (see that section). Jaffa Gate is used only from OUTSIDE the gate. Nothing inside the Old City and nothing in East Jerusalem is used.

Labels: **[V]** = verified against the cited source. **[PARTLY]** = sources disagree, or only one weak source. **[UNVERIFIED]** = from general knowledge, not checked in this pass. Check these on site before building a mission on them.

---

## 1. Central Bus Station plaza and the Chords Bridge

### Takeaway
The bridge is a numbers mine, and those numbers make clean answer keys: 66 cables, a 118 m pylon, a cost that went from NIS 129M to NIS 246M. It also has an absurd opening night story that teens will enjoy. It is lit at night and visible from the bus station plaza.

### Cited Findings
- **[V]** Designed by Santiago Calatrava. Built 2005 to 2008, inaugurated 25 June 2008. Pylon is 118 m, the tallest structure in Jerusalem when it was finished. **66 steel cables** in a parabolic shape. Total length 360 m, longest span 160 m — [Wikipedia: Chords Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chords_Bridge)
- **[V]** Cost NIS 246 million (about $70M) against an initial estimate of NIS 129 million, so it ended at more than double the budget. Critics called it extravagant — [Wikipedia: Chords Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chords_Bridge)
- **[V]** At the $500,000 dedication, the teenage girl dancers were made to wear long skirts and cover their hair after ultra-Orthodox threats to disrupt the event. The press nicknamed them the "Taliban troupe" — [Wikipedia: Chords Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chords_Bridge)
- **[V]** It carries the light rail Red Line, which began service on 19 August 2011, three years after the bridge opened. A glass-sided pedestrian bridge links Kiryat Moshe to the Central Bus Station — [Wikipedia: Chords Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chords_Bridge)
- **[PARTLY]** The "King David's harp" reading of the cables is attributed to "some observers", not to Calatrava — [Wikipedia: Chords Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chords_Bridge)

### Inferences (mission ideas)
- **Count the strings:** "The harp has N strings. Count them from the plaza." The answer is 66, a numeric mission with a tolerance of ±4, because counting at night is hard. Teens love "the bridge with no train for three years" (2008 to 2011). A quiz: "How many years did the bridge stand before a single train crossed it?" (3).
- **Budget absurdity:** a numeric guess, "the plan was 129 million; what did it cost?" Score by closeness.
- **Reenactment:** the team plays the harp. A photo mission with the team as "strings" under the pylon.

### Gaps
- Exact cable count visible from the plaza side, and whether the pylon lighting is on at night. Check on site.

## 2. Mahane Yehuda market (incl. the Iraqi market, Solomon Souza shutters)

### Takeaway
It is a strong night location. Its streets are named after fruit, which makes a natural code. The painted shutters are a ready-made "find the face" hunt. One catch: on a Thursday night many stalls are open late, so a lot of shutters will be rolled UP and their art hidden. Scout which shutters are down at 19:00 to 21:00.

### Cited Findings
- **[V]** The Mahane Yehuda neighbourhood was founded in 1887 by Johannes Frutiger, Shalom Konstrum and Joseph Navon, and **named after Navon's brother, Yehuda** — [Wikipedia: Mahane Yehuda Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahane_Yehuda_Market)
- **[V]** The Etz Chaim Yeshiva opened shops in the market and used the rent to fund the yeshiva. The British cleared it and built permanent stalls and roofing in the late 1920s — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahane_Yehuda_Market)
- **[V]** **Iraqi market:** in 1931, 20 traders with temporary wooden stalls built a new section west of the market. It got its name because many traders were of Iraqi Jewish descent — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahane_Yehuda_Market)
- **[V]** **Solomon Souza** started spray-painting shutters in January 2015. By July 2016 he had done **more than 250 of the market's 360 shutters**. Portraits include Golda Meir, Naomi Shemer, Yossi Banai, Hadassah and local storeowners. **David Ben-Gurion is painted upside down** (his famous headstand). The article doesn't map faces to shutters — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahane_Yehuda_Market)
- **[V]** The cross streets are named after fruit and nuts: Afarsek (peach), Agas (pear), Egoz (walnut), Shaked (almond), Shezif (plum), Tapuach (apple), Tut. *(Note: Wikipedia glosses Tut as "strawberry", but תות in older Hebrew usage is also mulberry, so don't build an answer key on the English gloss.)* Agas Street was renamed for Yaakov Eliyahu Banai (the Banai family home) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahane_Yehuda_Market)
- **[V]** Rami Levy opened his first store on Hashikma Street in 1976 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahane_Yehuda_Market)
- **[V]** The painted shutters are described as an after-hours attraction. 20+ bars, and a city ban on loud music after 23:00 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahane_Yehuda_Market)

### Inferences (mission ideas)
- **Fruit code:** each team gets a list of fruits in an order. They walk the street signs and take the Nth letter of each street name to build a code word. It works fully at night, because street signs are lit.
- **Upside-down hunt:** "Find the prime minister standing on his head and copy his pose." A photo reenactment that teens will actually do.
- **Name origin trick question:** "Which Yehuda is the market named after?" (The founder's brother, not Judah the tribe.) A good "gotcha" quiz.
- **Iraqi market count:** "How many traders built the Iraqi market in 1931?" (20) as a numeric task.

### Gaps
- Which specific shutters (and faces) are down on a Thursday evening. Needs a scouting walk at the event hour.
- Current shutter count after 2016 is not verified.

## 3. Nachlaot

### Takeaway
It is a maze of 32 small neighbourhoods with founding dates on gates and lintels. Narrow, dim lanes make it atmospheric, but plan for lighting and noise: it is a residential area at night.

### Cited Findings
- **[V]** It is a cluster of 32 neighbourhoods, many of them courtyard neighbourhoods, built 1875 to 1933 because the Old City was overcrowded. The first was Even Yisrael (1875) — [Wikipedia: Nachlaot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nachlaot)
- **[V]** Mazkeret Moshe was founded by Moses Montefiore in 1882 (Ashkenazi), with Ohel Moshe (Sephardi, listed 1883) alongside it. Also Sukkat Shalom (1888), Batei Broide (1902), Zikhron Yosef (1931) — [Wikipedia: Nachlaot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nachlaot)
- **[PARTLY]** "At one time ~300 synagogues within a few blocks, more than anywhere in the world." This is tagged *citation needed* — [Wikipedia: Nachlaot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nachlaot)
- **[V]** Yitzhak Navon, later Israel's president, grew up in Ohel Moshe, and it inspired his play *Bustan Sephardi*. The Banai family lived here. Rabbi Aryeh Levin, "the prisoners' rabbi", lived in Mishkenot Yisrael. That is a direct link to the Russian Compound prison (see §6) — [Wikipedia: Nachlaot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nachlaot); [Wikipedia: Museum of Underground Prisoners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Underground_Prisoners)

### Inferences (mission ideas)
- **Date hunt:** many courtyard gates carry the neighbourhood's name and year (to be confirmed on site). "Find the gate of the neighbourhood Montefiore founded and enter its year" (1882).
- **Story thread:** "A rabbi walked from here every Shabbat for 25 years to visit prisoners. Follow his route to the prison." This links Nachlaot to the Russian Compound as one chain mission.
- **Hidden synagogue count:** "How many tiny synagogues can you spot in one lane in 5 minutes?" Score by count. Avoid entering, and keep it quiet: it is a residential area.

### Gaps
- Which gate plaques and inscriptions exist, and whether they are lit. A scouting walk is needed.
- The 300-synagogues figure is unsourced.

## 4. Davidka Square

### Takeaway
This is the best "absurd weapon" story on the route. A homemade mortar that was inaccurate, but so loud the enemy reportedly thought it was an atomic bomb. It has a short Bible verse on the monument that makes a clean code.

### Cited Findings
- **[V]** The Davidka ("Little David") was a homemade mortar from 1948, invented by engineer **David Leibovitch** and named after him — [Wikipedia: Davidka Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davidka_Square)
- **[V]** It was not accurate, but its thunderous blast often sent enemies fleeing. The page says Arab forces **mistook it for an atomic bomb** and abandoned Safed. *(This is a widely repeated claim. Treat it as a legend-level anecdote and frame it as "the story goes".)* — [Wikipedia: Davidka Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davidka_Square)
- **[V]** The memorial was commissioned by the municipality in 1956, architect Asher Hiram. A real Harel Brigade Davidka sits on a stone platform. Inscription from **2 Kings 19:34**: "I will defend this city, to save it" (וגנותי אל העיר הזאת להושיעה) — [Wikipedia: Davidka Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davidka_Square)
- **[V]** Design details: two small rounded projections on one side and one large on the other evoke the **Palmach cap** (the "kova tembel" style). The square was redesigned by Mexican architect Ricardo Legorreta, with reddish paving, a blue water trench, and **4.5 m stone columns that serve as lamps at night** — [Wikipedia: Davidka Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davidka_Square)

### Inferences (mission ideas)
- **Verse code:** "Find the verse on the monument. The book and chapter:verse are your code" (19:34, giving code 1934). It's on the stone itself and lit by the lamp columns.
- **Sound mission:** "This weapon won by being LOUD. Record your team's loudest shout as a video." Keep it short, because it is a residential crossroads.
- **Spot the hat:** "What soldier's hat does the monument hide?" (Palmach cap), a multiple choice.
- Keep the tone on ingenuity and the bluff, not on casualties.

### Gaps
- The exact Hebrew text and layout of the plaque as it appears on the stone. Photograph it on the scouting walk.

## 5. Ben Yehuda Street and Zion Square

### Takeaway
Use the cinema story and the street's own name, and leave the attacks out entirely. The 1912 silent cinema in a hut, which collapsed under snow, is the hook.

### Cited Findings
- **[V]** Zion Square took its name from the **Zion Cinema**. It started as a 400-seat silent-movie house **in a hut, 1912 to 1920**. **Heavy snowfall collapsed the hut**, and it was rebuilt as a 600-seat theatre for films and live opera. It closed in 1972 and was demolished. The tower that replaced it holds a hotel and a Bank Hapoalim branch — [Wikipedia: Zion Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zion_Square)
- **[V]** The square was originally called "Zion Circus". Ben Yehuda Street was **built by the British in 1922**, and it and the square became a **pedestrian mall in 1983** — [Wikipedia: Zion Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zion_Square)
- **[V]** Hamashbir Lazarchan opened in 2011, the largest department store in Israel. The Sansur Building (1929) is neo-Renaissance — [Wikipedia: Zion Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zion_Square)
- **[UNVERIFIED]** Eliezer Ben-Yehuda (1858 to 1922), the reviver of spoken Hebrew. His son Itamar Ben-Avi is often called the first native Hebrew speaker of modern times. He coined many everyday words. This was not checked in this pass. The fact that the street was built in 1922, the same year Ben-Yehuda died, is a coincidence I could not confirm was deliberate.

### Inferences (mission ideas)
- **Word inventor mission:** "Ben-Yehuda had to invent words. Invent a Hebrew word for something that didn't exist in 1900 (selfie, Wi-Fi) and sell it to a passer-by in 15 seconds." Video mission, needs no facts to verify.
- **Snow-cinema quiz:** "What destroyed Jerusalem's first cinema?" with options fire / snow / earthquake / flood (snow).
- **Year chain:** cinema 1912 → street 1922 → mall 1983, sorted as an ordering task.

### Gaps
- The exact list of words Ben-Yehuda coined, and the plaques on the street. Not verified here.
- Whether any plaque about the Zion Cinema exists on the current building.

## 6. Russian Compound: Museum of Underground Prisoners and the Finger of Og

### Takeaway
It has a real prison-break tunnel story (Feb 1948) and an "abandoned giant" column lying in the street. Both are strong teen hooks. The column is outdoors and accessible at night. The museum is almost certainly closed in the evening, so use its exterior and the story.

### Cited Findings
- **[V]** The building was built late in the Ottoman period as a **hostel for Russian Christian women pilgrims**. A Russian inscription above the entrance reads "Marianskya women's hostel". After 1917 the British turned it into the central prison — [Wikipedia: Museum of Underground Prisoners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Underground_Prisoners)
- **[V]** **The 1948 tunnel escape:** Irgun and Lehi prisoners dug from the "escape room", a cell near the fence, **under the last bed in a corner**. The tunnel joined a sewer pipe running under the fence, while comrades outside pretended to repair the pipe. **Twelve prisoners**, disguised as municipality workers, walked out — [Wikipedia: Museum of Underground Prisoners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Underground_Prisoners)
- **[V]** The date was **20 February 1948**, 12 prisoners, including Mattityahu Shmuelevitz (later director-general of the Prime Minister's Office). The rocky soil made digging hard. The British found a ladder and an electrical cable with two light bulbs in the tunnel. A newspaper called it a bold escape "wrapped in mystery". It was copied from an identical plan at **Acre**, where the Jewish prisoners were moved to Jerusalem just before their own planned escape day — [Walla](https://www.walla.co.il/news/israel/3160905)
- **[PARTLY]** A Ministry of Defence museum document reportedly names it as an escape from **room 23**. An earlier escape (December 1943) by two prisoners happened during the installation of a **Christmas tree** in the prison director's house. This is from a search summary of the MoD PDF, which I could not read directly — [MoD museums PDF](https://museums.mod.gov.il/sites/Jerusalem/Documents/escape.pdf)
- **[V]** Prisoners carved names, Stars of David, and underground emblems into floors and window sills. Solitary cells were nicknamed "hell". The dispensary gave "a red bottle and a yellow bottle" for almost every illness. A smuggling-items shelf sits in the warden's office — [Wikipedia: Museum of Underground Prisoners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Underground_Prisoners)
- **[V]** Rabbi Aryeh Levin visited every Shabbat and holiday for **25 years**, unpaid — [Wikipedia: Museum of Underground Prisoners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Underground_Prisoners)
- **[V]** **Finger of Og** (Herod's Pillar): a ~**12 m** stone column lying in front of the Russian Compound, abandoned in the quarry, probably because it cracked or had a flaw. It was likely meant for Herod's Temple (its size matches Josephus' description) or the Byzantine Nea Church — [Wikipedia: Finger of Og](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finger_of_Og); [BAS Library](https://library.biblicalarchaeology.org/?p=127259); [Jerusalem Post](https://jpost.com/archaeology/article-836832)
- **[UNVERIFIED]** The name refers to Og, king of Bashan, a giant in Deuteronomy 3:11. The name's link to the column was not sourced here.

### Inferences (mission ideas)
- **Escape-room mission (the centrepiece):** "12 prisoners walked out dressed as ___ (municipal workers). Find something on your team that could pass as a disguise and walk past the 'guard' (a staffer) without being stopped." A reenactment.
- **Escape riddle:** "They hid the tunnel under the ___ (last bed in the corner)." Or a code of 20 / 2 / 48 → 20248.
- **"Red bottle, yellow bottle":** a humour quiz on the prison doctor's two-bottle cure.
- **Giant's finger measure:** "Measure the Finger of Og in footsteps / team lengths" (≈12 m). It's a numeric task with wide tolerance, all outdoors.
- Stay away from the condemned-cell and gallows material. It falls under the "no deaths" rule.

### Gaps
- Museum opening hours (it is likely closed by 19:00). Not verified.
- Whether the Russian inscription is visible and lit from the street.
- The room-23 detail and the Christmas-tree escape need the MoD PDF read in full (text extraction failed here).

## 7. Safra Square / old City Hall

### Takeaway
The 1930 rounded building facing the Old City is said to carry 1948 battle pockmarks. It is a "find the scars" detail, but treat it carefully: frame it as "this building was on the border for 19 years", not as combat.

### Cited Findings
- **[V]** The old town hall was financed by **Barclays Bank** (its offices were in the rounded corner section facing the Old City's northwest corner). Architect A. Clifford Holliday. It was city hall from 1930 to 1993 — [Wikipedia: Jerusalem Old Town Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem_Old_Town_Hall)
- **[V/PARTLY]** The facade bears pockmarks from the 1948 fighting. It sat beside No Man's Land from 1948 to 1967. The claim that it was "never patched up" comes from one source only — [Wikipedia: Jerusalem Old Town Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem_Old_Town_Hall); [BiblePlaces](https://www.bibleplaces.com/blog/2009/10/kikar-tzahal-idf-square-jerusalem/)
- **[V]** Safra Square, the new municipal complex from the 1990s, is named after the Safra family, and the 1930 building forms its eastern tip — [Wikipedia: Safra Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safra_Square)

### Inferences (mission ideas)
- **Bank-turned-city-hall riddle:** "This city hall was paid for by a ___" (bank) — a good surprise answer.
- **Border-line mission:** "For 19 years this corner was the edge of the country. Stand where you think the border ran and take a photo." Avoid counting bullet holes as a game. A gentler "find a scar the city chose not to hide" is a respectful alternative, and it needs a judgment call by the organisers.

### Gaps
- Whether the pockmarks are visible under night lighting. Whether any plaque explains them.

## 8. Mamilla (Stern House, numbered stones, Mamilla Pool)

### Takeaway
The numbered stones are the strongest "find the detail" mechanic on the whole route. A real historic house was taken apart stone by stone and rebuilt with its numbers left showing. Herzl slept there. The open-air mall is lit and busy at night. **Drop the Mamilla Pool** as a game site.

### Cited Findings
- **[V]** **Stern House**: during the Mamilla redevelopment, every stone was numbered, the building was taken down and rebuilt after the mall's base was built. **The numbers are still visible.** Architect Moshe Safdie chose to leave them facing outward as part of the facade — [Wikipedia: Stern House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stern_House); [REM / Building by numbers](https://realestatemagazine.ca/building-by-numbers-in-jerusalem)
- **[V]** **Herzl spent four nights here in 1898**, after he was displaced from his hotel room, at the invitation of Michael Stern. Today it houses a Steimatzky bookstore and a small museum — [Wikipedia: Stern House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stern_House)
- **[V] (for exclusion)** The Mamilla Pool sits inside what was the city's largest Muslim cemetery, and its preservation is politically contested. It is also unlit parkland at night — [Wikipedia: Mamilla Pool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamilla_Pool); [Emek Shaveh](https://emekshaveh.org/en/mamilla/)
- **[PARTLY]** The name "Mamilla" has competing theories (a saint, a sponsor, a Hebrew corruption). None is confirmed — [Wikipedia: Mamilla Pool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamilla_Pool)

### Inferences (mission ideas)
- **Stone-number code:** "Find stones 1, 2, 3 ... on Stern House. What's the highest number you can see?" Or "find the stone numbered X and photograph it". This is a perfect fit for the photo and numeric task types.
- **Herzl hotel riddle:** "The founder of Zionism got kicked out of his hotel. Where did he sleep?" Teens can find the house from the clue.
- **Jigsaw reenactment:** each team member holds a number and they must "rebuild" a wall in the right order. A team-coordination mission.

### Gaps
- The actual range of visible numbers. Photograph them on the scouting walk.
- Whether the Stern House museum is open on Thursday evening.

## 9. Jaffa Gate (outside only)

### Takeaway
There are three linked "how to enter a city" stories, all told from outside the gate. The Kaiser rode in through a widened opening (1898). Allenby walked in on foot (1917), partly so as not to look like the Kaiser. And a clock tower stood there for ~15 years and then vanished.

### Cited Findings
- **[V/PARTLY]** **1898:** the Ottomans opened the wall beside the gate so Kaiser Wilhelm II and his entourage could ride in on horses and carriages. One source cautions that "a breach was made" is "only partially correct" — [BiblePlaces](https://www.bibleplaces.com/blog/tag/jerusalem/page/156/); [Madain Project: Jaffa Gate](https://madainproject.com/jaffa_gate)
- **[V]** **11 December 1917:** General Allenby entered **on foot** through Jaffa Gate, out of respect for the city and reportedly to avoid comparison with the Kaiser's mounted entry — [Madain Project](https://madainproject.com/jaffa_gate); [BiblePlaces](https://bibleplaces.com/?p=13225)
- **[PARTLY]** **Clock tower:** built for Sultan Abdul Hamid II's jubilee, about 13 m. Its dates vary by source: 1907/1908/1909 to 1917/1921/1922. It was dismantled by the British around 1921 to 1922 and re-erected in a different form at Allenby Square, then finally taken down around 1934 for roadwork (*Palestine Post*, 27 Sept 1934) — [Breadcrumbs at Jaffa Gate](https://approachingjerusalem.substack.com/p/breadcrumbs-at-jaffa-gate); [Madain Project](https://madainproject.com/jaffa_gate); [BiblePlaces](https://www.bibleplaces.com/blog/tag/jerusalem/page/156/)

### Inferences (mission ideas)
- **Two entries, reenacted:** half the team rides in "like the Kaiser" (piggyback), half walks in "like Allenby". A video mission outside the gate. Absurd and memorable.
- **Vanishing clock:** "This gate once had a clock tower. Was it destroyed, moved, or stolen?" (moved, then demolished). A trick question that rewards reading the clue.
- **The car road:** "The road you're looking at is the hole made for a Kaiser." A photo of the vehicle opening (verify on site that the vehicle passage is the 1898 opening).
- Use only the outside plaza and the Mamilla side, per the constraint.

### Gaps
- The tower's start date is contested. Avoid making the exact year an answer key.
- Whether the outside plaza has any plaque about 1898 or 1917.

## 10. Teddy Park

### Takeaway
This is the one location with a scheduled night "show". The fountain runs with music on a timetable that includes 20:00 (and sometimes 21:00), so a mission can be timed to it.

### Cited Findings
- **[V]** The park is named for longtime mayor Teddy Kollek, founder of the Jerusalem Foundation. It sits at the foot of the Old City walls, across from Jaffa Gate. The fountain is the Hassenfeld Family Fountain, with music by the New Jerusalem Orchestra. A visitor centre shows an 8-minute hologram film about Kollek — [Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/teddy-park-is-capitals-new-beach/); [iTravel Jerusalem](https://www.itraveljerusalem.com/ent/teddy-park)
- **[PARTLY]** Show times conflict. iTravel lists night shows at **20:00, plus 21:00 in summer**. Times of Israel says 20:00 and 21:00 nightly. Other sources give other schedules — [iTravel Jerusalem](https://www.itraveljerusalem.com/ent/teddy-park); [Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/teddy-park-is-capitals-new-beach/); [Inbal Hotel](https://www.inbalhotel.com/?p=5038)

### Inferences (mission ideas)
- **Timed mission:** "Be at the fountain for the 20:00 show and film the moment it starts." This works as a timed flash mission (RushPoint's `pushFlashMission`) or as a task with an `expiresAt`.
- **Dance-off:** the team improvises a dance to the fountain music. A video mission.
- In late October, the 21:00 show probably isn't running (if it's summer-only). Confirm with the Jerusalem Foundation or the park before relying on it.

### Gaps
- The October 2026 show schedule. Whether the park is lit and open after 21:00.
- No Kollek quote at the park was found.

## 11. Yemin Moshe and the Montefiore windmill (plus Mishkenot Sha'ananim)

### Takeaway
It offers a British "Operation Don Quixote" against a windmill, residents who were **paid** to live outside the walls, and a replica carriage. These are among the best absurd-but-true stories on the route, and the windmill is lit at night.

### Cited Findings
- **[V]** The windmill was built 1857, completed 1858. Its sails turned only until **1878**, when a steam mill replaced it. It was restored to working order 2008 to 2012 with the Dutch "Christians for Israel" and the Jerusalem Foundation — [ESRA Magazine](https://magazine.esra.org.il/posts/entry/montefiore-s-jerusalem-windmill.html); [Times of Israel blog](https://blogs.timesofisrael.com/montefiores-jerusalem-windmill-the-full-story/)
- **[V]** **1948:** the Haganah put a sniper and machine-gun post on the mill. The British High Commissioner ordered it blown up, and the operation was nicknamed **"Operation Don Quixote"** (Don Quixote famously fought windmills). One account says the soldiers, from **Ramsgate**, saw Montefiore's plaques (Montefiore was from Ramsgate) and **blew off only the very top**. *(Treat the Ramsgate detail as a single-account story.)* — [ESRA Magazine](https://magazine.esra.org.il/posts/entry/montefiore-s-jerusalem-windmill.html); [Times of Israel blog](https://blogs.timesofisrael.com/montefiores-jerusalem-windmill-the-full-story/)
- **[V]** **The carriage** on display is a **replica**. The original was destroyed in a **fire in 1986**. *(No source found that calls it arson. Don't say "arson".)* — [ESRA Magazine](https://magazine.esra.org.il/posts/entry/montefiore-s-jerusalem-windmill.html); [Bein Harim](https://www.beinharimtours.com/montefiore-windmill/)
- **[V]** **Mishkenot Sha'ananim** (1859 to 1860) was the first housing outside the walls. It was paid for by a bequest from **Judah Touro of New Orleans** (reported $50,000 or $60,000) and built by Montefiore. Its stone wall and **heavy door were locked at night**. **Residents were paid** as an incentive because Jews feared Bedouin raids outside the walls. The facade inscription records Touro's bequest and Montefiore's name. Yemin Moshe itself came later, in 1892 to 1894 — [Wikipedia: Mishkenot Sha'ananim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mishkenot_Sha%27ananim)
- **[V]** In 1973 the building became a guesthouse for acclaimed writers, artists and musicians. It is now also home to the Jerusalem Music Center. It bordered no man's land from 1948 to 1967 — [Wikipedia: Mishkenot Sha'ananim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mishkenot_Sha%27ananim)

### Inferences (mission ideas)
- **"Would you take the job?":** "In 1860 people were paid to sleep here, outside the walls. How much would YOU need to sleep one night in a scary place?" Have the team record a 15-second pitch.
- **Don Quixote quiz:** "What were the British jokingly called while attacking this windmill?" Teens who know the novel will get it. Others learn it.
- **Read the facade:** "Whose money built the first house outside the walls? He lived in ___" (New Orleans). The answer comes from the inscription on site, if it is legible at night.
- **Fake carriage:** "One thing here is a fake. Find it." (The carriage replica.) A great find-the-detail mission.
- **Windmill numbers:** 1857 built → 1878 stopped. "How many years did it actually grind?" (~20/21). A numeric task.

### Gaps
- Whether the Mishkenot facade inscription is lit and legible at night.
- The current state of the carriage display (glass case, lit?).

## Cross-route summary for the mission designer
