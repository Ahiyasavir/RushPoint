## Which Amazing Race Israel (המירוץ למיליון) tasks are remembered as the best, and why (especially Jerusalem)?

### Takeaway
In the 2018-2026 window, only season 7 (2019) has a Jerusalem leg, and none since: season 8 (2020) has Israeli tasks only in Tel Aviv, and season 9 (2024) never races in Israel. The Israeli opening legs share one formula: physical height or exposure at a landmark (a crane, the city walls, a beam above the Knesset area), then a knowledge or ordering puzzle tied to the place, then a social task with strangers. I found no source that ranks tasks as "most iconic".

### Cited Findings
- **Season 7 (aired from 15 July 2019), Leg 1, Jerusalem → Tel Aviv:** the start was the Wohl Rose Park helipad in Jerusalem, beside the Knesset. Teams studied ten photographs of Knesset events and their dates, were lifted by boom lift onto a wooden beam, climbed 30 m by inserting metal pegs, and at the top put the photos in date order. Then a farm task at Kibbutz Netzer Sereni: push a basket of citrus up an angled beam with poles without dropping any fruit. Then a social task at Habima Square: find a stranger holding proof of a flight from Ben Gurion within 24 hours and bring that person to the square (the stranger got a US$200 duty free card). The Pit Stop, and later the Finish Line, was the Eretz Israel Museum. Note: Wikipedia places the beam "over Rothschild Street" while the start was in Jerusalem. The geography is ambiguous and should be checked against the episode. — [HaMerotz LaMillion 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HaMerotz_LaMillion_7)
- **Season 7 finale (September 2019, Tel Aviv):** four puzzle boxes at Kenshoo Tel Aviv, each holding a clue toward the Finish Line. — [HaMerotz LaMillion 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HaMerotz_LaMillion_7)
- **Season 8 (Israeli leg filmed December 2020):** the only Israeli tasks were at the finish. Teams unlocked a tablet with a code whose first two digits were the sum of their placings on every earlier leg, and the tablet revealed the Finish Line (Beit Lessin Theater). Jerusalem appears only as racers' hometown. — [HaMerotz LaMillion 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HaMerotz_LaMillion_8)
- **Season 9 (1 July to 25 September 2024):** no legs in Israel. The premiere was delayed by the Gaza war, and some episodes were postponed after national tragedies (Majdal Shams, the recovery of six hostages' bodies). — [HaMerotz LaMillion 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HaMerotz_LaMillion_9)
- **Earlier Jerusalem legs, before the 2018 window but useful as format reference:**
  - Season 4 (November 2014): started at Mishkenot Sha'ananim. Teams worked together to climb a 30 m ladder of poles and chains up the Walls of Jerusalem. The Detour was "Milk or Honey": move five milk bottles by their necks with two long sticks, or harvest a jar of honey while wearing a mesh cage of live bees on the head. The Pit Stop was the City of David. — [HaMerotz LaMillion 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HaMerotz_LaMillion_4)
  - Season 4's northern group had a history-ordering task at Bat Shlomo: match events to years using balloons and envelopes. **A wrong answer meant sitting through a history lecture** before searching again. — [HaMerotz LaMillion 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HaMerotz_LaMillion_4)
  - Season 2 (2011): started at Teddy Stadium, Jerusalem. Teams untangled ropes to find their car key, then climbed rope ladders to a car hanging from a crane, honked, and were lowered. Next was a choir task: songs named colours, the colours led to balls in a field, and the balls' numbers opened a lock. — [HaMerotz LaMillion 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HaMerotz_LaMillion_2)
- Season 7 is the only season since 2018 whose page lists a Jerusalem start, and seasons 2 and 4 also started there. — [search summary of Wikipedia season pages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HaMerotz_LaMillion)

### Inferences
- The Israeli legs repeat three mechanics: (1) **visible physical exposure at a landmark** (climbing walls, cranes, height), (2) **a puzzle bound to the place** (Knesset history photos in date order, events matched to years), and (3) **a social task with real strangers that has a concrete goal** (find a person with a flight ticket). A teen race built on the same three would feel "like the show". That points toward height or stairs or wall walks, place-anchored ordering puzzles, and recruiting strangers with a clear purpose, rather than a random dare.
- Season 4's "wrong answer = listen to a history lecture" is an elegant penalty that costs time and is mildly comic without humiliating anyone. It could be adapted (wrong answer ⇒ a 60 s audio clip before retrying).
- The season 8 code built from cumulative placings is a good "everything you did counts" finale mechanic.
- Since 2018 the show has used Israel mainly as a start and finish frame. Any claim that a specific Jerusalem task is "iconic among teens" is UNSOURCED.

### Gaps
- No Hebrew article, poll or forum ranks "the best tasks ever" in the Israeli show. Hebrew Wikipedia's page is a disambiguation page, and the season articles were not fetched.
- Season 10 or later (2025-2026): ice.co.il mentions a new season on Keshet 12 in 2026 ([ice](https://www.ice.co.il/tv/news/article/1101751)), but its Israeli legs and any Jerusalem tasks were not verified.
- Viewer demographics (whether 15-18 year olds watch it) were not found.

## What do Israeli teens consider prestigious in group challenges?

### Takeaway
The sources confirm the structures (גיבושון = a short screening; מכינות open with a multi-day מסע גיבוש) but give no teen testimony on what feels prestigious. Prestige is plausibly tied to *selection*, meaning the activity screens or tests, but that is inference.

### Cited Findings
- A גיבושון is a short גיבוש of a day or two at most whose main purpose is **screening and testing a participant's ability to meet the unit's tasks**. — [HaMichlol: גיבוש (קבוצה)](https://www.hamichlol.org.il/גיבוש_(קבוצה))
- Most pre-army preparatory programmes (מכינות) open the year with a special event that includes a **multi-day מסע גיבוש**. — [INN](https://www.inn.co.il/news/261456) (via search summary; not fetched in full)
- מכינות are voluntary programmes for post-12th-graders, recognised jointly by the Education and Defence ministries. — [HaMichlol: מכינה קדם צבאית](https://www.hamichlol.org.il/%D7%9E%D7%9B%D7%99%D7%A0%D7%94_%D7%A6%D7%91%D7%90%D7%99%D7%AA); [Kol Zchut](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/מכינה_קדם_צבאית_ושנת_שירות)
- The Ministry of Defence runs a youth portal for pre-army preparation (noar.mod.gov.il). — [noar.mod.gov.il](https://noar.mod.gov.il/PreMilitarySchool/Pages/%d7%a7%d7%95%d7%a8%d7%90%d7%99%d7%9d-%d7%9c%d7%9a.aspx)
- There are paid "חוגי סיירות" programmes for teens (a job ad for a coordinator prefers graduates of a שנת שירות or מכינה), so pre-army fitness and navigation is a commercial category. — [Drushim job ad](https://www.drushim.co.il/job/38301894/7c8ca773/)
- Youth movements run a senior-age group (שכבה בוגרת) with its own גיבוש activities. The Youth Movements Council lists group-building activities split by younger and senior groups. — [מועצת תנועות הנוער](https://www.tni.org.il/1141/%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%A2%D7%A6%D7%AA-%D7%AA%D7%A0%D7%95%D7%A2%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%94%D7%A0%D7%95%D7%A2%D7%A8/%D7%A4%D7%A2%D7%95%D7%9C%D7%AA-%D7%92%D7%99%D7%91%D7%95%D7%A9)
- Bnei Akiva branches hold all-night events ("לילה לבן") for grades 4-12. — [Yehud-Monosson municipality](https://yehud-monosson.muni.il/events/%D7%9C%D7%99%D7%9C%D7%94-%D7%9C%D7%91%D7%9F-%D7%AA%D7%A0%D7%95%D7%A2%D7%AA-%D7%A0%D7%95%D7%A2%D7%A8-%D7%91%D7%A0%D7%99-%D7%A2%D7%A7%D7%99%D7%91%D7%90-2/)

### Inferences (UNSOURCED, from general Israeli cultural context, so verify before relying on them)
- For 16-17 year olds a year or two from the draft, "גיבוש", "סיירות", night navigation, and being *chosen* or *ranked* carry status, because they mirror the army screenings they are about to face. Framing that borrows this register ("מבצע", call signs, a sealed envelope, a briefing, a time window, a team ranking at the end) is likely to read as serious. Childish props (costumes, cartoon characters) are likely to undercut it.
- Real responsibility (navigating by yourself without an adult, holding the team's phone, a real clock, a real prize) likely adds prestige. Hand-holding likely signals "this is for kids".
- Competing against *other groups*, with a public leaderboard, is likely central. That fits the HaMerotz elimination format.

### Gaps
- No primary source (teen survey, article, forum thread) on which challenges teens consider prestigious. Searches for "יום סיירות" and "ניווט לילה שכבה בוגרת" returned nothing specific.
- No Tzofim, Noar Oved or Ariel night-game ("משחק לילה") descriptions were retrieved. The hadracha.org activity bank is the next place to look ([hadracha.org](https://www.hadracha.org/he/displayItems.asp?ct=132)).

## What do reviews and testimonials of Israeli teen city games say teens loved versus found babyish?

### Takeaway
Jerusalem city game products exist (BeeTravel/GoBeeTravel, KKL's school version, CloseApp), but they are marketed "for all ages". Their reviews come from adults. The one actionable signal is a reviewer wanting **more physical activity while moving**. I found no teen-authored reviews.

### Cited Findings
- **"לא ידעתי את זה!"** (GoBeeTravel) is a night phone game along the Old City walls, from Jaffa Gate to Dung Gate, ~1.5 h, with an optional walk on top of the wall. It uses a live GPS map, audio guide, observation clues, questions at varying difficulty, videos, team discussion challenges and photo stops. Price from ₪169 per permit, up to 5 competing teams. Rated 4.85/5 from 20 reviews. Reviewers said it "leads to places you usually don't reach" and praised the curiosity and variety. Criticisms: one reviewer wanted **"more physical activities while moving"**, another found the video audio quiet and the clue at point 9 missing. **None of the quoted reviews are from teens.** — [GoBeeTravel night-wall](https://www.gobeetravel.com/product/night-wall/)
- **"יש כאן אריה"** (BeeTravel): Mahane Yehuda → Jaffa Gate, riddles, filming videos, points, team competition, a certificate at the end. **"אנשים וחומות"**: a night game for groups in Jerusalem. — [iTravelJerusalem: יש כאן אריה](https://www.itraveljerusalem.com/he/tour/there-is-a-lion-fun-game-bee-travel); [אנשים וחומות](https://www.itraveljerusalem.com/he/tour/people-and-walls-bee-travel); [לרוץ על ירושלים](https://www.itraveljerusalem.com/he/tour/bee-travel-mobile-game)
- **KKL "אוצר ירושלים"** is a QR station navigation game for school groups from 5th grade up, about 1 h. The goal is to finish all stations fastest with the fewest mistakes. — [KKL education](https://www.kkl.org.il/education/travels/nivut_games/theme_based/otzar-jerusalem/)
- One Jerusalem escape room ("להימלט מהנזיר") was described as having fairly hard puzzles, less suited to first-timers. The article is from 2016, outside the window. — [Kipa](https://www.kipa.co.il/תרבות/%d7%97%d7%95%d7%95%d7%99%d7%94-%d7%99%d7%a8%d7%95%d7%a9%d7%9c%d7%9e%d7%99%d7%aa-%d7%9c%d7%94%d7%99%d7%9e%d7%9c%d7%98-%d7%9e%d7%94%d7%a0%d7%96%d7%99%d7%a8-2/)
- Children's escape rooms are described as "milder", with puzzles matched to age. — [Karamel](https://www.karamel.co.il/חדר_בריחה_לילדים.asp)

### Inferences
- The commercial Jerusalem games are mostly *walk + observe + quiz + video*. The one reported shortfall is a lack of physical action. For 16-17 year olds, a quiz-heavy, audio-guide game probably reads as a school tour (סיור של בית ספר). Physical, competitive and time-pressured tasks are the gap a teen race can fill.
- KKL's "fastest with fewest mistakes" scoring targets 5th graders and up. Pitching a teen event as a "QR navigation game" may therefore sound like a younger kids' school activity (UNSOURCED).

### Gaps
- No Google Maps reviews were retrieved (WebFetch cannot read them), and no teen-group testimonials came up. Questo-style Israeli companies aimed at teens, and "ציד מטמון עירוני לנוער" operators, were not surfaced by search.
- No source explicitly lists what teens called "babyish" (ילדותי / תינוקי).

## Where does "troll"/silly content stop being fun for 16-17 year olds?

### Takeaway
No source addresses this directly. The YouTube landscape shows that challenge, extreme and dare formats are part of what Israeli teens watch (Dylan Dror's extreme challenges, Ben Kaiser's challenges, Noam Peroz's treasure hunting), and YouTube reaches 98% of 13-17 year olds. The line between fun and humiliating is unsourced.

### Cited Findings
- 98% of Israeli 13-17 year olds use YouTube, second only to WhatsApp at 99% (2024 data, n=252). — [ISOC-IL 2024](https://www.isoc.org.il/sts-data/social-media-use-il-teens-2024)
- teenk Magazine (April 2022) listed Gen Z YouTube channels including Dylan Dror ("fun, extreme content and challenges"), Ben Kaiser (vlogs, challenges, reactions), the Liopro family (sketches, vlogs, challenges), Elior Biloux (football with challenges), Noam Peroz (treasure hunting in unexpected places, provocative commentary) and Yulia Spector (vlogs, challenges). The article makes no claims about what teens find childish. — [teenk Magazine](https://magazine.teenk.co.il/articles/29638)
- YouTube Israel's 2024 year in review was dominated by premium TV-style content, and Kevin Rubin's "Brawl Stars in real life" was singled out as a huge production. — [Time Out](https://timeout.co.il/%D7%99%D7%95%D7%98%D7%99%D7%95%D7%91-2024/)
- Israeli TikTok trends of 2024 were reviewed by a kids' school blog, a weak source. — [Novakid](https://www.novakidschool.com/he/blog/israeli-tiktokers-and-tiktok-trends/)

### Inferences (UNSOURCED, so treat as hypotheses to test with real teens)
- Big-production "game in real life" formats (Kevin Rubin's Brawl Stars) suggest teens value **scale and production**, the feeling of being in a real show, over gag tasks.
- A silly task is probably acceptable when it is *chosen*, *shared by the whole team*, *scored*, and *seen by peers*. It probably tips into "being used as a clown" when it is imposed, aimed at one person, staged in front of strangers for no game reason, or filmed for the organiser's own marketing. The HaMerotz stranger task works because it has a concrete goal (find a traveller), not because it is embarrassing.
- For a pitch in the HaMerotz register, the safe pattern is: courage tasks (height, approaching strangers with a mission) yes, humiliation tasks (singing alone in public, costumes) only as an optional bonus.

### Gaps
- No Hebrew forum, Reddit r/Israel thread or article was found on teens feeling humiliated or "used" in organised games.
- No current (2025-2026) ranking of the YouTubers 16-17 year olds watch, as opposed to younger children. The teenk list is from 2022 and mixes child and teen audiences. Hebrew Wikipedia's list of the largest Israeli channels (June 2025) exists but was not fetched: [list](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%A2%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%A6%D7%99_%D7%94%D7%99%D7%95%D7%98%D7%99%D7%95%D7%91_%D7%94%D7%99%D7%A9%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%9C%D7%99%D7%99%D7%9D_%D7%91%D7%A2%D7%9C%D7%99_%D7%9E%D7%A1%D7%A4%D7%A8_%D7%94%D7%9E%D7%A0%D7%95%D7%99%D7%99%D7%9D_%D7%94%D7%92%D7%93%D7%95%D7%9C_%D7%91%D7%99%D7%95%D7%AA%D7%A8).
- Asking 5-10 real 16-17 year olds directly would close most of these gaps faster than more web search.
