# Smart urban / street game designs ("cool and serious") → mission patterns for a 2.5h teen night race in central Jerusalem

Scope note: research covered Channel 4 *Hunted*, Jet Lag Hide+Seek, Journey to the End of the Night (JTTEN), The Game / Shinteki / DASH / Great Gotham Challenge (GGC) / Midnight Madness, MIT Mystery Hunt runarounds, geocaching multi/mystery caches, I Love Bees (ARG), SF0, Cruel 2 B Kind, Assassin/Killer, CluedUpp, myCityHunt. 15 tool calls. Every *source-backed* claim carries a link; the mission patterns themselves are adaptations (design inferences) built on those sources and are marked as such. Event assumptions used throughout: ~10 teams, teens, one photographer per team, 2.5 hours, after dark, central Jerusalem (city centre / Nachalat Shiva / Ben Yehuda / Jaffa Rd light-rail axis / Mahane Yehuda), RushPoint app as the verifier (code entry, photo with staff review or auto-approve, GPS check-in, quiz/numeric answers, sequence steps, hidden-location missions with a search circle, flash missions, live leaderboard).

## Q1. The pattern catalogue: how each works, why it feels adult, the reveal, verification, duration

### Takeaway
The "serious" feel in every reference game comes from three things, not from theme dressing: (1) the city itself is the puzzle (answers are read off real plaques, signs, phones, objects), (2) there is a live opponent or a clock that makes decisions cost something (Hunted, JTTEN, Jet Lag), and (3) information is scarce and must be earned, traded or deduced (CluedUpp, Jet Lag questions, I Love Bees passwords). Silliness comes from performative stunts (SF0-style dares, costumes); avoid those as core missions.

### Cited Findings — source mechanics the patterns are built on
**Manhunt / pursuit**
- *Hunted*: fugitives evade a team of hunters (ex-police/intelligence personnel) across Britain for a set period (28 → 25 → 21 days by series); survivors who reach an **extraction point on the final day** share £100,000; hunters use CCTV, ANPR, call tracing, drones, dogs, and sometimes offer the public rewards for information; capture requires physical contact; *Celebrity Hunted* runs 14 days — [Wikipedia: Hunted (2015)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunted_(2015_TV_series))
- *Hunted* unofficial rules reported by ex-contestants (not the broadcaster): move every 48 h and at least 5 miles, never reuse the same spot/person, must use an ATM at least twice (on different days) — producers say these "simulate real people on the run" — [robohara.com](https://www.robohara.com/?p=10177). (Secondary source; treat as indicative.)
- *Hunted* official: fugitives bring no funds and must not enlist the help of others — per Sky listing, as summarised in search results — [Sky](https://www.sky.com/watch/series/fdd3518a-d891-4973-9d88-94ed691b3488/tv)
- *Jet Lag: Hide+Seek*: one hider, seekers ask questions the hider must answer truthfully; question families are **matching, measuring (radar, thermometer) and photo**; hider draws cards for **time bonuses** and **curses** that restrict seekers; once seekers enter the hiding zone the hider may no longer move; scored by time hidden + bonuses — [summary of S9 Japan episode](https://chaindesk.ai/tools/youtube-summarizer/we-played-hide-and-seek-across-japan-PHjkSKQSzv4); thermometer = map split at the midpoint between the seekers' two points, answer "warmer/colder" — [r/JetLagTheGame](https://nyc1.lr.ggtyler.dev/r/JetLagTheGame/comments/1i9u0ys/very_confused_about_thermometer); radar = "is the hider within X of us?" hit/miss — [Minecart Rapid Transit wiki](https://wiki.minecartrapidtransit.net/index.php/Hide_%2B_Seek)
- Jet Lag rules come in **three scales** (nation, large city, small city/portion of a city) with different hiding times, bonuses and question sets — [Minecart Rapid Transit wiki](https://wiki.minecartrapidtransit.net/index.php/Hide_%2B_Seek)
- A fan-run city version (Dallas DART): hider stays within ½ mile of a rail station, 30 min hiding time, live location shared with teammates **and a designated safety contact**, optional "two seeker teams race to find the hider" mode — [Ride With Data](https://ridewithdata.substack.com/p/get-ready-for-jet-lag-the-game-hide)
- *JTTEN* (AntiBoredom, SF, 2006): runners start together with a "manifest" of ~6 checkpoints, must visit them in order on foot/public transit; staffed checkpoints sign the manifest and **tell the runner how to recognise the next agent**; tagged runners become chasers (ribbons swap); **safe zones (DMZ) around each checkpoint** stop chasers "babysitting"; organisers cap initial chasers' catches; "the first sighting of chasers should be dramatic"; typical start 7–8pm; fastest ~90 min, most ~3 h; route ~10 miles; checkpoints should be "strange, wonderful, safe, or unsettling", overlooked places; keep rules simple — runners respond to chasers by running, not by asking authorities — [Ludocity: Journey](https://ludocity.org/wiki/Journey); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journey_to_the_End_of_the_Night_(game))

**Puzzle hunts**
- *The Game* (Bay Area/Seattle): 24–48 h non-stop; each clue solves to the next location; clues share a theme; historically clues hidden under park benches, behind bowling pins; it became "increasingly more high-tech and psychological"; **safety warning**: in 2002 a player fell down a mineshaft after taking real warning signs to be part of the game — [Wikipedia: The Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Game_(treasure_hunt))
- *Espionage* (1999 Game, spy-themed): clue attached to the team van's tailpipe; final clue encoded the seven Bay Area bridges the teams had crossed during the night (the route itself was the answer) — [John Owens' Game archive](https://www.ece.ucdavis.edu/~jowens/game/espionage.html)
- *Shinteki*: company running puzzle-hunt team-building modelled on The Game; later events ≤12 h; "Decathlon" = 10 puzzle challenges — [puzzles.wiki](https://www.puzzles.wiki/wiki/Shinteki); a Decathlon envelope of Trivial-Pursuit-style cards whose answers were the names of the puzzle types (a meta) — [John Owens: Shinteki](https://www.ece.ucdavis.edu/~jowens/game/shinteki.html)
- *DASH*: same hunt same day in many cities; teams 3–5; each solve reveals more story and sends teams to a nearby location; physical props (tokens, Post-its, MP3 players); three tracks (expert/beginner/kids); hints unlock as you solve; 2–4 miles of walking; **the clock runs only while solving, not while walking** — [playdash.org FAQ](https://playdash.org/faq.php); [Puzzled Pint](https://puzzledpint.com/tag/dash)
- *MIT Mystery Hunt runaround*: the finale is a physical chain of clues across campus to the hidden coin; runarounds "actually require taking physical actions"; 2008 runaround used an object (card/knife) picked up earlier to open the way; 2011 started with a phone call puzzle and gated a door choice on conditions (room number prime, number pad, a name on it) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Mystery_Hunt); [puzzles.wiki: Runaround](https://www.puzzles.wiki/wiki/Runaround); [2008 runaround PDF](https://www.mit.edu/%7Epuzzle/08/endgame/runaround.pdf); [ARGN on 2011](https://www.argn.com/?p=4767)
- *Great Gotham Challenge* (NYC): each puzzle starts from an online riddle leading to a location and an action; a team crawled into a parked delivery truck to retrieve an envelope and a password; HQ texts the team; a fake poster with a phone number to call for the next clue (one team dialled a real vocal coach by mistake — a stranger-harm risk) — [Room Escape Artist 2025](https://roomescapeartist.com/2025/08/11/great-gotham-challenge-ggc-mmxxv-review/); [No Proscenium](https://www.noproscenium.com/questionable-design-decisions-mar-the-expansive-great-gotham-challenge-the-nopro-review/); [ARGN](https://www.argn.com/tag/great_gotham_challenge/)
- *Midnight Madness* name lineage: The Game (LA 1973) inspired the 1980 film *Midnight Madness*, which in turn named later hunts; a 2025 NYC edition had teams decode a custom Ouija board into GPS coordinates; a New York clue on a subway underpass wall translated into line colours and station names — [ARGN tag DASH (Ouija photo caption)](https://www.argn.com/tag/dash/); [Wikipedia: The Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Game_(treasure_hunt))

**Geocaching**
- Multi/mystery caches derive the next coordinates from on-site plaque data: subtract fixed numbers from plaque years; pick specific digits of specific years; digital-root checksums of dates; remainder of dividing by a 10-digit plaque number; letter→digit lookup from names on a plaque — [GC listing](https://www.geocaching.com/geocache/GCYN4W), [GC10Y43](https://www.geocaching.com/geocache/GC10Y43), [GCGH56](https://www.geocaching.com/geocache/GCGH56), [GCRZQQ](https://www.geocaching.com/geocache/GCRZQQ) (individual listings; mechanics summarised from search results)

**ARG / pervasive**
- *I Love Bees* (2004, Halo 2 promo by 4orty2wo): 210 GPS coordinate pairs (first posted as 220) with times turned out to be **real payphones**; each block of seven locations shared a password; the caller "Melissa" asked identity checks (e.g. "What ship are you assigned to?" → "Apocalypso"); correct codewords unlocked "Axons", 777 total to finish the story — [ARGN payphones](https://www.argn.com/tag/payphones/); [Halopedia: Axon](https://www.halopedia.org/Axon); [Unfiction](https://www.unfiction.com/games/2004/07/01/i-love-bees-aka-the-haunted-apiary/)
- *SF0*: tasks must be documented with a "praxis" — a write-up with evidence; tasks ranged from simple to absurd; the community valued over-delivery (Doorhenge) over points — [Metafilter](https://www.metafilter.com/99140/sf0); [SF0 glossary](https://sf0.org/LSK/Document-documentation/)
- *Cruel 2 B Kind* (McGonigal & Bogost, 2006): pairs get a secret "weapon" that looks like an ordinary kind gesture (serenade, compliment) and a secret weakness; you have no information about your targets — the only way to find them is to attack; acting oddly reveals you; the eliminated join their killers — [Charman-Anderson](https://charman-anderson.com/2006/09/05/the-games-people-play-cruel-2-b-kind/); [Henry Jenkins](https://www.henryjenkins.org/blog/2006/10/if_you_live_in_boston.html)
- *Assassin/Killer*: closed target chain, you may only hit your current target; the victim hands over their own target's code-name; kills by Post-it + phrase (no mock weapon needed); host-declared safe zones and time windows; tie-break when two remain — [UW CSE 143 rules](https://courses.cs.washington.edu/courses/cse143/09wi/assassin.shtml); [Wikipedia: Assassin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassin_(game))
- *CluedUpp*: app-driven outdoor Cluedo; no set route — a map of points of interest (witnesses, items, score multipliers); interview witnesses to eliminate suspects/weapons (e.g. 20 witnesses, 16 suspects, 5 weapons); 2–3 miles, 2–3 hours; no live actors — [Glam Adelaide](https://glamadelaide.com.au/a-giant-detective-adventure-played-all-across-adelaide-is-coming/); [Stripes](https://europe.stripes.com/your-community/cluedupp-cracks-the-case-of-how-to-have-fun-with-friends-and-family.html)
- *myCityHunt* (the closest real product to "Agent Hunt" — no city spy game called "Agent Hunt" was found): GPS app spy game; teams eavesdrop on enemy spies and recruit contacts; **calls from contacts arrive during play and some informants are untrustworthy / some clues mislead**; ~3 h — [App Store: myCityHunt](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/mycityhunt-plus/id6677036699); [myCityHunt Harrisonburg](https://mycityhunt.co.uk/cities/harrisonburg-us-13862/escape-game-harrisonburg-54506)

### Inferences — the catalogue (34 patterns)
Format per pattern: **Source lineage** · How it works · Why it feels smart · Reveal moment · Verify · Time. Times are on-foot estimates for compact central Jerusalem and are design guesses, not measured. "Photographer" = the team's own photographer (event constraint), whose shot is the proof and the marketing asset at once.

**A. Reading the city (codes from the real street)**

1. **Plaque arithmetic (coordinate derivation)** · *geocaching multi-cache* · Brief gives a template "the drop is at 31.78ABC, 35.21DEF"; A–F come from years/numbers on 2–3 real plaques or memorial stones along a street (e.g. "A = last digit of the year on the plaque beside door 12"). · Nothing is told; the city is the cipher. · Typing the computed point into the map and seeing it land on a real doorway. · Numeric answer in app; the derived point becomes a hidden mission that unseals by GPS. · 12–15 min.
2. **Street-name acrostic** · *GGC / Midnight Madness "station names" clue* · A walking route instruction: "take the first letter of every street you cross between X and Y". Letters spell a codeword (Hebrew letters work well). · Route = message, like *Espionage*'s seven bridges. · The last turn completes a word that names the next place. · Text answer (quiz typed answer). · 10 min.
3. **The route is the answer (retrospective meta)** · *Espionage final clue (seven bridges)* · Final puzzle can only be solved by remembering what the team passed: "the number of the bus line at mission 2 × the floor number at mission 5". · Rewards attention all night; the "aha" lands on the whole operation. · Realising the clues were in every previous stop. · Numeric. · 5–10 min (finale).
4. **Sign-letter extraction** · *puzzle hunt indexing* · A grid of (shop sign, letter index) pairs, e.g. "the 3rd letter on the green awning across from the fountain". Read in order → passphrase. · Classic hunt indexing, zero props. · Passphrase used at the next handoff. · Text answer. · 10 min.
5. **Trilingual sign cross-check** · *inference from Jerusalem's Hebrew/Arabic/English signage* · Teams compare the three spellings on one street sign; the letter that differs/is extra across languages is the key. · Feels like real signals analysis; locally specific. · One letter that doesn't belong. · Text. · 5 min. (Verify signs exist at chosen corners on a recce.)
6. **Plaque date timeline** · *geocaching date mechanics* · Photograph four dated plaques/memorials, order them chronologically; the order of their house numbers is a 4-digit lock code. · Sorting evidence = detective work. · The code opens a sealed mission in the app. · Station code (smart_station) or numeric. · 15 min.
7. **Door-condition runaround** · *MIT 2011 "room number prime, keypad, name on it"* · "Your contact's door: an odd number, a blue mailbox, and a name starting with ש". Teams walk a lane filtering doors. · Logical filtering in physical space. · Only one door satisfies all three. · Photo of the door number (no knocking — residents are not part of the game). · 10 min.

**B. Ciphers and codebreaking**

8. **Cipher trail with a found key** · *DASH props / MIT 2008 object-carried-forward* · Mission 3 gives a cipher text; the key (keyword or Atbash hint) is only found at mission 6. Teams carry the unsolved message. · Delayed payoff, sense of a dossier. · Late key decodes an early message. · Text. · Spread across the night (5 min active).
9. **Atbash / Hebrew number-value (gematria) cipher** · *inference: native Hebrew cipher tradition fits Jerusalem* · Atbash (א↔ת) is historical and local, so it feels authentic rather than "kids' code". · The decoded word is a real street name nearby. · Text. · 5–8 min.
10. **Light-rail timetable cipher** · *Midnight Madness subway-colour clue* · Numbers in the brief are station indexes on the Red Line; letters of station names spell the next meeting place. · Uses the city's infrastructure as the codebook. · Text. · 8 min. (Teams read the station map, never board without permission and never near tracks — see safety.)
11. **Metapuzzle envelope** · *Shinteki Decathlon cards whose answers named the puzzle types* · Every mission answer is one word; the final 5–6 answers together solve the meta that names the extraction point. · The night collapses into one answer — the hunt-community gold standard. · Last word clicks into the meta. · Text. · 10 min finale.
12. **Misleading informant (verify before trust)** · *myCityHunt untrustworthy contacts* · Two "intel" messages contradict each other; one has a checkable detail (e.g. "the clock on the building shows Roman numerals") that is false on site. · Teaches trust-but-verify; adult spycraft feel. · Arriving and seeing the detail is wrong → the other source was true. · Choice quiz. · 10 min.

**C. Contacts, handoffs, dead drops**

13. **Informant handoff with a passphrase** · *I Love Bees identity check ("What ship…" → "Apocalypso"); JTTEN agent recognition* · A briefed, consenting shop vendor/barista is the "contact". Team must say the challenge line; contact answers with the counter-line and hands over a sealed card with a code. · Real human, real ritual, real secrecy. · The stranger-looking contact answering the code line. · Code on the card → station code; photographer shoots the handover. · 5 min. Constraint: contacts are pre-arranged and paid/thanked; never ask uninvolved strangers.
14. **Contact recognition chain** · *JTTEN "agents tell you how to recognise the next agent"* · Each staff marshal describes the next one only by a detail ("red scarf, reading a newspaper, bench near the fountain"). · Surveillance and description skills. · Spotting the person across a square. · Marshal's station code. · 8–10 min per hop.
15. **Dead drop (hidden mission, search area)** · *geocaching + RushPoint hidden mission* · App shows only a circle; the drop is a magnetic box/sticker under a bench or behind a public planter (with permission). · Physical search under pressure feels like fieldcraft. · Hand touches the box. · Code inside → station code; GPS arrival unseals. · 10–15 min. Avoid anything resembling a suspicious object (see safety): use flat stickers/QR plates, never packages.
16. **Brush pass (timed exchange between two teams)** · *spy tradecraft framing; inference* · Two teams receive halves of a code and a 3-minute window at a meeting spot; they must exchange halves without stopping (walking past each other). · Coordination with a rival; looks cool on camera. · Both halves combine into one code only both can submit. · Both teams submit the same combined code within the window; photographer captures the pass. · 10 min.
17. **The scheduled call** · *I Love Bees payphones; GGC "call the number on the poster"* · At a fixed time, the team must be at a location; the HQ calls the team phone (or pushes a flash mission) with a voice question only answerable on that spot. · Timing pressure + "they're watching". · Phone rings exactly when they arrive. · Flash mission with a quiz answer; GPS check-in. · 5 min + walk. Use only HQ numbers; never print a number that a stranger might own (GGC mis-dial lesson).
18. **Object carried forward** · *MIT 2008 card/knife used later* · Mission 2 hands each team a physical token (a transparent cipher sheet, a cut postcard half). It is useless until mission 8, where it overlays a poster/board. · Players learn to keep everything; dossier feel. · Overlay reveals the hidden word. · Text. · 5 min active.

**D. Surveillance, tailing and observation**

19. **Tail the courier** · *spy theme; JTTEN pursuit inverted* · A staff "courier" (known costume-free detail) walks a set loop; team must report three things he does (which shop he looks into, what he buys, which bench he sits on) without being spotted (courier may "burn" a team that gets within 5 m). · Pure surveillance skill, zero physical risk if courier walks slowly on pedestrian streets. · Courier's last act reveals a code (writes it on a café napkin he leaves). · Quiz with 3 answers; burned teams get a penalty. · 15 min. Pedestrian zones only (Ben Yehuda, Nachalat Shiva).
20. **Stakeout counting** · *observation tasks; inference* · "From this bench, count how many people enter X in 5 minutes" — no, avoid profiling strangers; instead count fixed things: lit windows on a facade, light-rail cars passing. · Patience and precision. · Numeric within tolerance. · Numeric with tolerance. · 5–7 min.
21. **Photo-match (find where this was shot)** · *Jet Lag photo questions* · App shows a tight crop (a doorknocker, a mosaic tile, a graffiti fragment); team finds it and reproduces the exact frame. · Visual intelligence; makes the photographer the star. · Overlaying their shot on the crop. · Photo, staff review. · 10 min.
22. **Surveillance log reconstruction** · *CluedUpp witnesses* · Several "witness statements" (app text) each describe something visible from one spot; team triangulates which corner the "agent" stood on. · Deduction from testimony. · Standing in the one place where all statements are true. · GPS check-in (geofence) at the right corner. · 12 min.

**E. Pursuit, evasion and extraction**

23. **Hunted-lite: the burned team** · *Hunted; JTTEN* · For 20 min a randomly chosen team is the "fugitive"; others get periodic "intel" (Jet Lag–style: radar hit/miss, thermometer warmer/colder, a photo the fugitive must send). Capture = photographing the fugitive team's photographer with their team flag, no contact. · The single strongest "serious" mechanic; intel is earned not given. · Fugitives' 20-min mark survives or a rival team finds them. · Photo submitted by hunters (staff review); flash mission. · 20 min. Walking only; capture by photo not touch.
24. **Question budget (Jet Lag questions as currency)** · *Jet Lag question families + cards* · To locate a hidden HQ point, teams "buy" questions with points: radar (is it within 300 m of us?), thermometer, matching (is it on the same street as us?), photo. · Information economics; every ask costs. · Final search circle shrinks to one courtyard. · Answers delivered by HQ in chat; arrival via hidden mission. · 15–20 min.
25. **Timed extraction** · *Hunted extraction point on the final day* · Only in the last 15 min the extraction point is revealed via the meta; teams must arrive inside a window; late = no extraction bonus. · Classic finale tension. · The meta answer names the place. · GPS check-in with expiry (mission time limit). · 15 min.
26. **Safe houses and DMZs** · *JTTEN safe zones; Assassin safe zones* · During pursuit missions, designated cafés/marshal posts are safe; you can't be "caught" inside 30 m. · Makes pursuit fair and keeps kids near staff. · — · App geofence. · Rule, not a mission.
27. **Move-or-burn rule** · *Hunted unofficial "move every 48 h, never reuse a spot"* · Teams cannot submit two missions from the same block back-to-back; forces route planning. · Feels like real tradecraft constraints. · — · Routing rule. · Rule.

**F. Rival-team interaction (no physical contact)**

28. **Target chain (Assassin by photo)** · *Assassin target chain; Cruel 2 B Kind* · Each team secretly has another team as "target" and must get a clean photo of the target team's flag/sign; the "kill" passes the target's own target to you. · Chain logic and paranoia, very adult; no weapons — the camera is the weapon. · A photo submitted with the right team visible. · Photo, staff review (real-time). · Runs as a background layer for 30–45 min.
29. **Kind ambush (Cruel 2 B Kind variant)** · *Cruel 2 B Kind* · Secret "weapon" = a specific nonverbal sign (a gesture, a phrase) aimed at other teams only; using it on a non-target reveals you. · Bluffing and reading people; harmless to bystanders. · Making the wrong move exposes your identity. · Self-report confirmed by the victim team's code. · Background layer.
30. **Double agent** · *myCityHunt untrustworthy informants; social deduction* · One member of each team (secretly, by app notification to a single phone) is told a bonus objective that conflicts mildly with the team's (e.g. pass one true clue to a specific rival). At the end teams vote who it was. · Social deduction lands strongly with teens. · Finale reveal on the big screen. · Rival submits the passed clue's code; vote via quiz. · 5 min active + finale. Caution: keep it light; no real betrayal of points that decides the winner.
31. **Intel market** · *Hunted public rewards for information; trade* · At the final checkpoint HQ "buys" correct intel; teams can trade their solved answers with rivals at a marshal-run exchange table (both must submit the traded code). · Negotiation and strategy. · — · Codes. · 10 min.
32. **Interception (flash mission race)** · *JTTEN dramatic first sighting; flash missions "first" mode* · HQ broadcasts "a courier package was dropped at X — first team takes it". Only one team wins; others get a smaller "eyes on" reward for photo proof of being there. · Live, everyone-sees-it competition. · Leaderboard jump. · Flash mission, first-come. · 5–10 min.

**G. Narrative structure devices**

33. **Dossier reveal per stage** · *DASH: each solve reveals more story* · Each completed stage releases one page of the case file (a photo, an intercepted message). · Story is a reward, not a lecture. · Final page names the "mole". · App instructions/media. · —
34. **Evidence documentation (praxis)** · *SF0 praxis* · The photographer's set of 5 shots per stage forms the "operation report" shown at the debrief. · Turns the photographer into the team's intelligence officer. · Slideshow at the end. · Photo missions. · —

## Q2. Narrative framing that makes a whole night coherent without being cheesy; briefing texts

### Takeaway
The best references frame the night as **one operation with one end-state** (Hunted: reach the extraction; MIT: find the coin; I Love Bees: unlock all axons) and deliver story in small earned pieces (DASH reveals story per solve). Cheese comes from over-explaining; seriousness comes from restraint, real stakes (a clock, a rival) and in-world documents.

### Cited Findings
- Hunted's whole arc hangs on a single end-goal — reach the extraction point on the final day — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunted_(2015_TV_series))
- DASH: "each solved puzzle reveals more of the story" — [playdash.org FAQ](https://playdash.org/faq.php)
- The Game: clues "often share an overall theme or story" — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Game_(treasure_hunt))
- I Love Bees: a story of 777 unlocks, each needing a time-and-place codeword; identity checks were in-world ("What ship are you assigned to?") — [ARGN](https://www.argn.com/tag/payphones/); [Halopedia](https://www.halopedia.org/Axon)
- JTTEN deliberately aims at moods (fear, desire, alienation…) and says special checkpoint experiences should *amplify the location's mood, not overshadow it*; the end point should be a warm gathering place that recognises finishers — [Ludocity](https://ludocity.org/wiki/Journey)
- myCityHunt frames contacts and enemy spies, with untrustworthy informants as a story device — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/mycityhunt-plus/id6677036699)
- Hunted hunters are police/intelligence — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunted_(2015_TV_series)) — which this event must NOT emulate (constraint: no police roleplay).

### Inferences
Three frames that fit the constraints (no police, no weapons, no fake threats):

1. **"The Archive" (recovery operation)** — An old Jerusalem archive's catalogue was split into pieces and hidden across the city centre a century ago; tonight a rival group is also collecting them. Teams are independent "recovery cells". End-state: assemble the catalogue's index (the meta) and reach the reading room (extraction) before 22:30. Historical, local, zero security imagery.
2. **"The Heist" (an inverted heist — returning, not stealing)** — A crew must *put back* a stolen object (a map, a key) without being noticed. Avoids glorifying theft; keeps heist tropes (crew roles, blueprints, timing windows). Photographer = the crew's "eye".
3. **"Signal" (intelligence network)** — An informal network of contacts (shopkeepers) passes a message across the city one hop at a time; a double agent is inside the teams. End-state: decode the message and name the double agent.
Avoid: "terrorist", "bomb", "agents of [real service]", fake IDs, police, anything that could be misread by passers-by or security staff in Jerusalem.

**Sample briefing texts** (authored for this event, Hebrew plural voice per project rule; not sourced):

## Q3. How rival teams can interact (intercept, sabotage, trade) without physical risk

### Takeaway
Every reference game that mixes pursuit with the public bounds it with **safe zones, catch caps, simple rules and non-contact tagging**; the camera (photo-as-tag) and shared codes (both teams must submit) give rivalry without touching. Sabotage should be informational (misleading intel, hiding a clue's meaning) never physical (moving/destroying drops).

### Cited Findings
- JTTEN uses safe zones around every checkpoint so chasers don't camp there, caps initial chasers' catches, and grows the chaser pool from tagged runners; it wants runners to respond to chasers by running, not by asking authorities — [Ludocity](https://ludocity.org/wiki/Journey). (Running is incompatible with this event's no-running-near-traffic rule → adapt to walking + photo-tag.)
- Assassin rules: only your current target is fair game, the target can only evade; safe zones and time windows declared by the host; tagging can be a Post-it + phrase instead of mock weapons — [UW CSE 143](https://courses.cs.washington.edu/courses/cse143/09wi/assassin.shtml); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassin_(game))
- Cruel 2 B Kind's attacks are kind gestures, so bystanders see only friendliness; misfires reveal your identity — [Charman-Anderson](https://charman-anderson.com/2006/09/05/the-games-people-play-cruel-2-b-kind/)
- Jet Lag rivalry is informational: questions, curses that restrict the other side, and a "two seeker teams race" mode; fan versions require sharing live location with a safety contact — [chaindesk summary](https://chaindesk.ai/tools/youtube-summarizer/we-played-hide-and-seek-across-japan-PHjkSKQSzv4); [Ride With Data](https://ridewithdata.substack.com/p/get-ready-for-jet-lag-the-game-hide)
- Hunted's hunters pay the public for information — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunted_(2015_TV_series)) — the analogue here is a staffed "intel market", not paying strangers.
- Safety precedents: The Game's mineshaft accident (real warnings mistaken for game content) and a 1999 World Trade Center incident where a found bottle of green liquid triggered a partial hotel evacuation — [Wikipedia: The Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Game_(treasure_hunt)); GGC players mistakenly called a real person from a game poster — [No Proscenium](https://www.noproscenium.com/questionable-design-decisions-mar-the-expansive-great-gotham-challenge-the-nopro-review/)

### Inferences
Safe interaction menu (all walking, no contact, staff-adjacent):
- **Photo-tag** replaces physical tag (Pattern 23, 28). Rule: tag photo must show the target team's flag and be taken from ≥3 m; never inside safe zones (cafés, marshal posts, light-rail platforms).
- **Shared-code missions** force cooperation between rivals (Pattern 16 brush pass, 31 intel market).
- **Informational sabotage only**: curses (e.g. "the team you choose cannot use the map for 5 min" — enforced in-app, not by rivals), misleading intel planted by HQ (Pattern 12), double agent (30). Never allow moving, hiding or removing physical drops — a removed drop breaks the game for everyone and creates "suspicious object" behaviour.
- **First-come flash missions** (32) give interception drama with a consolation reward so nobody sprints: award second place for being there within the window, which removes the incentive to run.
- **Jerusalem security-context rules** (inference from the WTC bottle incident): no unattended bags/boxes/packages as drops — use flat stickers, QR plates, laminated cards attached by staff, or human contacts; brief local businesses and, prudently, local police/municipal security in advance; no costumes, toy weapons, balaclavas, "police"/"Shabak" wording, or covert behaviour near security posts, the light rail, or crowded checkpoints.
- **Traffic**: all pursuit layers limited to pedestrian streets (Ben Yehuda, Nachalat Shiva, Yoel Moshe Salomon, Rivlin) with Jaffa Rd crossings only at lights; a running penalty (any team seen running loses a stage bonus) — makes "no running" a game rule rather than a nag.
- **Strangers**: only pre-arranged contacts; tasks never ask players to approach, follow, photograph or count unconsenting members of the public (Pattern 20 rewritten to count objects for this reason).
